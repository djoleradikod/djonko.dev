<InShort>
From **5.2**, nothing converts: the subscription already carries 9.x licenses alongside 8.x keys (<KB id={452764} />). Upgrade or deploy VCF Operations 9, **register it**, then upgrade vCenter. An upgraded vCenter runs in evaluation for 90 days and needs a 9.x primary license before then. 8.x hosts under a 9.x vCenter keep their keys for now. From **9.0 to 9.1**, upgrade VCF Operations first, deploy the **License Server** with management services, and let licenses **transfer**: automatically in connected mode, by file exchange in disconnected mode. Upgrade out of order and you get vCenters "not connected to a license server" and hosts in evaluation (<KB id={440471} />, <KB id={442133} />).
</InShort>

## 5.2 to 9.0, step by step

1. **VCF Operations 9.0** in place: upgraded from Aria Operations or newly deployed. It runs in evaluation for up to 90 days.
2. **Register it** with the console, connected or disconnected. "VCF Operations is not registered" after the upgrade is the reminder (<KB id={438533} />).
3. **Upgrade vCenter.** It enters a 90-day evaluation and must receive a 9.x primary license with enough capacity.
4. **Assign the license** to the upgraded vCenter.
5. **Leave 8.x hosts alone** until they are upgraded. They keep v8 keys with the same features. A 9.0 management domain can manage 5.x and 9.0 workload domains.
6. **Clean up** old keys on the Pre-Version 9 tab once the infrastructure they covered is gone (<KB id={418555} />).

## 9.0 to 9.1, step by step

1. **Upgrade VCF Operations first.** Don't use the legacy fleet management appliance for this step (<KB id={440630} />).
2. **Deploy management services and the License Server.** The License Server needs DNS A and PTR records in lowercase.
3. **Transfer licenses.** In connected mode it is automatic. In disconnected mode, run the registration, verification, confirmation and license file exchange. Don't make assignments while it runs.
4. **Then upgrade vCenter and ESX.** An ESX upgrade precheck saying the vCenter "is not licensed by a licensing server" means step 1 or 2 was skipped (<KB id={440863} />).

<Callout type="field">
Put a licensing checkpoint in the runbook between "VCF Operations upgraded" and "vCenter upgraded": registered, License Server healthy (9.1), licenses visible and assignable. It takes ten minutes and prevents the ticket that arrives 89 days later.
</Callout>

## Pitfalls

- **Treating evaluation as success.** Everything works for 90 days. Then it doesn't.
- **Upgrading vCenter before VCF Operations** in the 9.1 path. Hosts come back with evaluation licenses.
- **Past-due banners on a fresh 9.1** can mean the License Server was never deployed, not that you missed a report (<KB id={453149} />).
