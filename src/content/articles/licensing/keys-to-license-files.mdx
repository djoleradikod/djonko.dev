<InShort>
In 9.x there are no keys to type into vCenter. You buy a subscription, and the **VCF Business Services console** (vcf.broadcom.com) turns it into a **default license** with your purchased capacity. A **VCF Operations** instance registers with the console and receives a signed **license file**. You then assign a **primary license** (VCF or VVF) to each **vCenter**, and everything connected to that vCenter is licensed through it. Usage is reported back at least every 180 days. VCF Operations is mandatory for this: vCenter 9 has no field for a key (<KB id={404155} />).
</InShort>

- **Default license.** Capacity from every active subscription of the same product in a Site ID pools into one default license. Each VCF core includes 1 TiB of vSAN.
- **Splitting.** One allocation cannot serve two VCF Operations instances. To license several instances, split the default license in the console so each gets its own share (<KB id={437242} />).
- **Primary and add-on.** VCF and VVF are primary licenses and must be assigned first. vSAN capacity beyond the included amount, and Private AI Foundation, are add-ons in the same file. NSX and HCX are no longer licensed separately.
- **Still on keys.** vDefend Firewall, Avi Load Balancer, Tanzu Platform and Data Services Manager keep license keys. VMware Live Recovery is SaaS.

## What this changes for operations

1. **VCF Operations becomes infrastructure.** If it is down for long enough, licensing cannot be refreshed. Plan its availability and backups accordingly.
2. **A calendar item appears.** Every instance has a usage-reporting deadline. See [the 180-day cycle](/vcf/library/licensing/180-day-cycle/).
3. **Licensing has a network path.** Connected mode needs outbound HTTPS from every VCF Operations node. See [connected or disconnected](/vcf/library/licensing/connected-vs-disconnected/).
4. **Mixed estates are normal during migration.** 8.x components keep their keys while 9.x components use the file (<KB id={406836} />).

<Callout type="field">
On a 5.x to 9.x project, give licensing its own workstream with an owner on the customer side who has access to the console for the right Site ID. Find that person early, because the technical steps need their portal access.
</Callout>

## Pitfalls

- **Trying to upgrade keys in the support portal.** There is nothing to upgrade. The 9.x license comes from the console.
- **Registering against the wrong Site ID.** It produces false expiry banners and failed updates. See [licensing errors decoded](/vcf/library/licensing/licensing-errors-decoded/).
- **Treating a monitoring-only VCF Operations as a licensing authority.** It does not need to be registered, and should not be, unless it will license vCenters.
