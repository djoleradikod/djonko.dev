<InShort>
**A perpetual licence outlasts its support contract.** After Support and Subscription (SnS) expires, hosts, VMs and licensed features keep working, and new patches, upgrades and support cases stop, apart from critical patches for vSphere 8.x <AsOf date="2026-11-07" />. Broadcom documents all of that. What was installed after expiry isn't covered in either KB. There the public record is a letter published by the press, and reports about it, demanding removal and warning of audits. Read your own agreement with counsel. This note isn't legal or purchasing advice.
</InShort>

Broadcom ended standalone perpetual sales [in a move to subscription-only offers](https://blogs.vmware.com/cloud-foundation/2024/01/22/vmware-end-of-availability-of-perpetual-licensing-and-saas-services/) announced in December 2023. The table separates what Broadcom has published from what reporters say they saw.

| Question | Broadcom documents | Press reports |
|---|---|---|
| Does the environment stop at expiry? | No. There is "no automated shutdown or disconnection mechanism", VMs stay on, vMotion and snapshots work, and the edition's features stay unlocked ([KB 429208](https://knowledge.broadcom.com/external/article/429208/perpetual-license-functionality-after-su.html), 12 Feb 2026) | |
| Is the licence revoked? | Expiry "does not revoke the underlying software entitlement", same KB | |
| What ends? | Portal downloads of patches and releases, and new support requests, same KB | |
| Critical patches? | Still available to all perpetual customers on vSphere 8.x, for alerts scored CVSS 9.0 or higher ([KB 314603](https://knowledge.broadcom.com/external/article/314603/zero-day-ie-critical-security-patches-fo.html), 29 Oct 2025) | In July 2025 some holders couldn't download them. A spokesperson said "only entitled customers have access to the patches at this time", and that a separate delivery cycle for non-entitled customers would follow, without giving a date. Users said support staff told them it could take 90 days ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/software/2025/07/23/vmware-portal-prevents-some-users-from-downloading-patches/699211)) |
| Updates installed after expiry? | Neither KB mentions removing anything. Check your agreement and any letter you receive | The letter [Ars Technica published](https://cdn.arstechnica.net/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/2025.05.07-12.26.01-SNAGIT-0038.pdf), dated April 2025 and signed by a Broadcom managing director, demands that use of post-expiry updates and patches "be ceased", "with the exception of zero-day security patches for vSphere 7.x and 8.x, CVSS score greater than or equal to 9.0, so long as those are generally provided by VMware at no cost". Anything else implemented past expiry "must be immediately removed/deinstalled", and the letter calls such use a breach and an infringement ([Ars Technica](https://arstechnica.com/gadgets/2025/05/broadcom-sends-cease-and-desist-letters-to-subscription-less-vmware-users/), 7 May 2025). Some customers had one within a week of expiry ([Techzine](https://www.techzine.eu/news/privacy-compliance/131220/broadcom-sends-cease-and-desist-letter-to-holders-of-perpetual-vmware-licenses), 8 May 2025) |
| Audits? | | The same letter says failing post-expiration reporting requirements may bring an audit, Ars Technica reports |
| Moving to VCF 9? | Convert first: "Customers with perpetual licenses will need to switch to subscription licenses prior to upgrading" ([FAQ Q27](https://www.vmware.com/docs/vmware-cloud-foundation-9-1-general-faqs), 4 Nov 2026) | |

The reports carry no Broadcom statement beyond the letter itself. It [didn't respond to Ars Technica](https://arstechnica.com/gadgets/2025/05/broadcom-sends-cease-and-desist-letters-to-subscription-less-vmware-users/) and [hadn't commented](https://www.networkworld.com/article/3981450/perpetual-vmware-license-holders-receive-demand-letter-from-broadcom.html) when Network World reported them. Ars Technica also says the letters seem to go out soon after a contract expires, whether or not the customer still runs VMware, so a letter may not mean anything specific was found about your estate.

Courts appear in several reports, and the ones cited here concern support, not installed updates. The Register [reported in July 2025](https://www.theregister.com/software/2025/07/23/vmware-portal-prevents-some-users-from-downloading-patches/699211) that a Dutch court ordered VMware to keep providing software support for at least two years to Rijkswaterstaat, which ran it under a perpetual licence, and [wrote on 5 November 2026](https://www.theregister.com/virtualization/2026/11/05/vmware-quietly-offers-european-users-extended-sales-and-support-for-low-end-vsphere/5307732) that local courts in the Netherlands "enforced a VMware support contract". It [also reports](https://www.theregister.com/virtualization/2026/10/26/tesco-replacing-vmware-with-proxmox-in-its-stores-and-on-the-edge/5305281) that Tesco is suing VMware over support for perpetual-licence software, with hearings likely in 2027. These are press summaries of specific disputes, so don't plan on them.

## The date that changes the critical-patch exception

The exception covers "supported versions", and KB 314603 defines those as 8.x. General support for vSphere 8 [ends on 11 October 2027](https://knowledge.broadcom.com/external/article/459135/end-of-general-support-for-vsphere-80.html), and [KB 456708](https://knowledge.broadcom.com/external/article/456708/upcoming-end-of-service-for-vsphere-8-vs.html) says that after that date "there will be no Technical Support and no new patches" for those versions. Neither KB mentions the critical-patch exception, and the letter limits its own exception to patches "generally provided by VMware at no cost". So ask in writing whether it survives, and plan as if it doesn't.

KB 314603 names 8.x only, so vSphere 7 is outside it today. The April 2025 letter does name 7.x and 8.x, so an estate on 7.x should ask in writing, and assume no critical patches reach it through this route until Broadcom answers.

Promised access has also been interrupted before. Hock Tan [announced the patches in April 2024](https://www.theregister.com/software/2024/04/16/broadcom-extends-support-for-vmwares-old-perpetual-license/1135019) so customers could use perpetual licences "in a safe and secure fashion", and the July 2025 report above says access had been failing since late May, about 13 months later. [The Register later described](https://www.theregister.com/virtualization/2026/11/05/vmware-quietly-offers-european-users-extended-sales-and-support-for-low-end-vsphere/5307732) that as a pause of months, which Broadcom put down to changes on its website. So try your own access before you need it.

## What to have ready

This list is advice, and it works whichever way your agreement reads.

1. **The agreement itself.** Find the version you signed, the expiry date it gives and any post-expiration reporting clause, because the letter ties its audit warning to that requirement.
2. **A build record.** Note what each host and vCenter ran on the expiry date, and every patch since, with its date and the [advisory](https://www.broadcom.com/support/vmware-security-advisories) behind it. The letter separates zero-day patches from everything else, and both it and KB 314603 draw the line at CVSS 9.0, so the score belongs in the record.
3. **A portal test.** Register at support.broadcom.com and confirm you can reach a critical patch now, while nothing is urgent.
4. **A change freeze on two things.** Add no new ESXi hosts and attempt no major version upgrade while SnS is lapsed, because KB 429208 says those need active SnS for licence conversion.
5. **A route for any letter.** Send it to legal and procurement before anyone changes a host. Removing a patch has its own security cost, and the people who have read the agreement should weigh that.

<Callout type="analysis" title="What this means for a design">
Running vSphere 8 on perpetual licences without SnS has a dated limit: after 11 October 2027 KB 456708 says no new patches for 8.x, and no Broadcom page cited here says the critical-patch exception continues. Any plan that runs past that date should name who accepts the patch risk and until when. [The October 2027 decision](/vcf/library/value/october-2027-decision/) compares the routes from there, and [licensing through upgrades](/vcf/library/licensing/licensing-through-upgrades/) covers the conversion if you stay.
