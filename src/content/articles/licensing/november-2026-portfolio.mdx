<InShort>
No source says a running estate must change on the day (<AsOf date="2026-11-06" />). What moved is what you can order and which features are promised next.

- **Announced by Broadcom:** vSphere Express, the EEA dates for vSphere 8 Standard and Enterprise Plus, three Advanced Services, DSM moving to TrueSource.
- **Only reported:** every Express price, the one-year term, the fixed 128-core pack and who qualifies.
- **Only planned:** native disaster recovery, VPC policy-based connectivity, vSAN S3 and infrastructure compliance. Keep them out of a design until a release ships them.
</InShort>

| If you run | What moved | Ask before relying on it |
|---|---|---|
| VCF 9.x | New catalog entries, and five features planned for the next release | Which release a Feature Comparison mark refers to |
| VVF 9.x | Native DR planned. HyperArmor marked as an add-on; Live Recovery isn't | Whether HyperArmor is orderable on VVF yet |
| vSphere 8 Standard or Enterprise Plus, in the EEA | On sale to 31 Oct 2027. Extended Support to subscription end or 31 Oct 2028, whichever is earlier | What Extended Support covers |
| vSphere 8 Standard or Enterprise Plus, elsewhere | Nothing from Broadcom. Resellers report end of sale in October 2026 | Your own contract and quote |
| VCF 5.x or VVF on vSphere 8 | Not named in the EEA paragraph. vSphere 8 general support still ends 11 Oct 2027 ([KB 459135](https://knowledge.broadcom.com/external/article/459135/end-of-general-support-for-vsphere-80.html)) | Whether anything extends to you: the sentence doesn't say so |
| Up to 128 cores in total | Express, if Broadcom accepts you | Eligibility, in writing |
| Advanced Cyber Compliance (ACC) | One add-on becomes two at expiry | Price of the two together |
| Data Services Manager (DSM) | New product family, licensing unstated | Metric and term at renewal |

This isn't advice to buy or renew, and it isn't legal or purchasing advice. Your order document decides what you hold. For edition contents cell by cell, use the [entitlement matrix](/vcf/library/value/using-what-vcf-includes/), which this note doesn't repeat.

## vSphere Express

Broadcom's [4 Nov post](https://blogs.vmware.com/cloud-foundation/2026/11/04/broadcom-vcf-portfolio-updates/) puts it on vSphere 9, available now in all regions, "for customers with 128 cores across all environments", and names five capabilities after "such as": vMotion, HA, Distributed Switch, vCenter Quick Patch, TPM 2.0 and vTPM 2.0.

The [TechDocs page](https://techdocs.broadcom.com/us/en/vmware-cis/vsphere/vsphere/9-1/vsphere-express.html) (2 Nov) has more design content than the post:

- ESX, vCenter, VCF Operations and a license server are all mandatory components. VCF Operations is for licensing only.
- There are no add-ons, and the default licence can't be split or shared across sites.
- The console blocks allocation above 128 cores "per customer or site ID".
- A vSphere 8 estate upgrades to Express 9.1.1 or later from vCenter and ESXi 8.0, with Aria Operations 8.18.6 and vSphere Replication 9.0.2.2 as optional sources.
- It can later converge to VCF or extend to VVF.
- For the feature list, it sends you to your sales representative.

A spokesperson [told The Register](https://www.theregister.com/virtualization/2026/11/04/vmware-returns-to-low-end-server-virtualization-with-vsphere-express/5307466) it is a per-core subscription priced "significantly lower" than VVF or VCF, and declined to give a figure. Broadcom's Prashanth Shenoy told the same reporter that instances are discrete with no central management, and that releases will follow the VVF and VCF cadence.

Channel sources fill in the rest, and each of these parties makes money from the answer.

| Source and stake | Claim |
|---|---|
| [TD SYNNEX](https://www.tdsynnex.com/na/us/vmware/marketplace-offer/), distributor selling it, undated US page | SKU VCF-VSP-EXP, "128 cores = $75 MSRP" and $9,600, one-year term, "packaged for commercial customers". [Techzine](https://www.techzine.eu/blogs/infrastructure/145578/vsphere-express-a-return-to-an-affordable-vmware/) reports the same listing |
| [Software Express](https://www.software-express.de/hersteller/vmware/vsphere/express/), German reseller, 5 Nov | 9,631.77 euro net, marked as a guide price. Exactly 128 cores, one pack per end customer, no extension. New customer means no VMware purchase ever or for at least 3 years. No DRS, memory tiering, SR-IOV, GPU, Proactive HA, live patching, VCF Automation or Private AI |
| [C4C Group](https://www.c4cgroup.co.uk/guides/vsphere-express/), sells renewal negotiation and doesn't resell VMware, updated 4 Nov | Same limits, attributed to reseller briefings. States that Broadcom has published no price or eligibility rules |

The two channel sources disagree on existing customers. C4C says your reseller should request Broadcom's authorisation. Software Express says the selection is already fixed and can't be applied for ("eine Bewerbung ist nicht möglich"), and expects details in mid-November at the earliest.

Still unconfirmed are the price, term, pack size, exclusions and who qualifies, along with whether a customer holding VCF elsewhere can buy Express for a small site. The post's "across all environments" and TechDocs' "per customer" read against it, while "or site ID" leaves the question open.

Express touches vSphere 8 estates of 128 cores or fewer, and anyone drawing a small-site design. The 16-core minimum applies to "most products" per the [Licensing Model](https://techdocs.broadcom.com/us/en/vmware-cis/vcf/vcf-9-0-and-later/9-1/licensing/licensing-overview/licensing-model.html), which doesn't say whether Express is one. C4C's Express checklist counts every CPU at a minimum of 16, but it gives that as Broadcom's general rule and doesn't say Broadcom applies it to Express.

<Callout type="analysis" title="What 128 cores buys: constructed example">
This assumes that the 16-core minimum applies, that each host has two sockets and that every host counts.

| CPU | Cores per host | Hosts that fit | Total |
|---|---|---|---|
| 16 cores | 32 | 4 | 128 |
| 24 cores | 48 | 2 (a third reaches 144) | 96 |
| 32 cores | 64 | 2 | 128 |

CPU choice sets the cluster size: on these hosts a third host for HA headroom is out at 24 cores per socket and up. With no add-ons available, this design also has no route to extra vSAN capacity. vSAN isn't in the component list at all. Express also adds VCF Operations and a license server to host, and it uses the same licensing infrastructure as VCF, so read the [180-day cycle](/vcf/library/licensing/180-day-cycle/) and confirm that it applies. Core rules are in [core counting and CPU choice](/vcf/library/value/core-counting-and-cpu-choice/).
</Callout>

## EEA terms for vSphere 8 Standard and Enterprise Plus

Broadcom gives this one sentence, at the end of the Express section: for customers in EEA countries, the two editions "will remain available for sale until October 31, 2027", with Extended Support in active subscriptions until the subscription ends or 31 Oct 2028, whichever is earlier. No reason is given.

[The Register](https://www.theregister.com/virtualization/2026/11/05/vmware-quietly-offers-european-users-extended-sales-and-support-for-low-end-vsphere/5307732) (5 Nov) says the paragraph was missing from the version VMware sent it before publication, and reads the move as an apparent concession to European regulators. Independent analyst Michael Warrilow suggests "regulatory muscle" as the reason, and says he can find no detail on the level of support or the commitments required. The Register also says it often heard VMware seldom issued quotes for these editions, and that VMware's Paul Turner blamed channel incentives since fixed. C4C says the UK is outside the EEA and that resellers report end of sale elsewhere in October 2026. Broadcom's post doesn't state the position outside the EEA.

Three things remain unconfirmed:

- What Extended Support includes. [KB 459135](https://knowledge.broadcom.com/external/article/459135/end-of-general-support-for-vsphere-80.html) gives 11 Oct 2027 for ESXi, vCenter and vSAN 8.x and doesn't mention this offer.
- What makes a customer "in" the EEA: contracting entity or site of use.
- What happens if a term runs past 31 Oct 2028. Support stops at that date under the wording, and the remaining months aren't addressed.

The terms reach subscribers on those two editions in EEA countries. The sentence says nothing about perpetual licences, VVF or VCF 5.x.

<Callout type="analysis" title="This site's reading of the dates">
Don't assume the whole design gets the extra year. The sentence names two vSphere editions only, and [Aria Operations 8.18](https://knowledge.broadcom.com/external/article/422643/end-of-general-support-for-vmware-aria-o.html) still ends on 11 Oct 2027. None of these sources carries a statement from backup, storage or hardware vendors. A plan that holds vSphere 8 past that date needs an answer for each component beside it. Express doesn't fill the same slot, since TechDocs says it has no version 8 keys.
</Callout>

Whether order lines mean this or Protection and Recovery | Price, and any conversion for ACC holders |

Read "new" with care, because [KB 428834](https://knowledge.broadcom.com/external/article/428834), scoped to Live Recovery 9.0.x, says cyber recovery was sold under Advanced Cyber Compliance as of November 2025. The [FAQ](https://www.vmware.com/docs/vmware-cloud-foundation-9-1-general-faqs) Q86 says ACC holders keep using it until their entitlement ends, then have "the option to purchase" Live Recovery and Compliance and Configuration Management. This note reads that as ACC splitting into two catalog entries. That's inference, since no Broadcom page says so outright.

ACC holders feel this first, because a one-line renewal becomes two. Anyone whose recovery design assumes a capability sits in the core comes next. FAQ Q78 and Q79 draw that line: vSAN replication and vSphere Replication are in, EDR-backed cyber recovery and cloud DR are out. The matrix records [where those answers conflict](/vcf/library/value/using-what-vcf-includes/).

## Data Services Manager to TrueSource

FAQ Q84 gives a date, 4 Nov 2026, and a location, "presented within TrueSource data services". The question asks whether DSM is included in VCF 9.1, and the answer doesn't say yes or no. Q85 says it deploys independently of the VCF installer. The Feature Comparison marks TrueSource Data Services for Edge and VCF, with no VVF mark. The 4 Nov post doesn't mention DSM, and the press reports checked on 6 Nov 2026 don't cover the move.

Broadcom's own pages conflict on this. TechDocs still lists DSM as its own add-on (5 Nov), and Feature Comparison p10 names DSM among pre-bundled services, marked for Edge and VCF with no add-on footnote. The [31 Aug TrueSource release](https://mfn.se/all/a/broadcom/broadcom-strengthens-spring-security-and-adds-coverage-of-java-python-and-node-js-ecosystems-with-truesource-e37fa20b) describes Data Services as artifacts, support and deployment expertise for PostgreSQL, RabbitMQ, MySQL and Valkey, with "tiered site licensing options". It doesn't name DSM.

None of the sources checked on 6 Nov 2026 says whether DSM's licence metric changes to that tiered model, what happens to a current DSM term, or which engines stay covered.

If you have DSM in a design or on a renewal, compare your order document with the new name before the renewal quote arrives.

## The next release, Q1FY27

The post leaves the release unnamed and hedges twice, with "currently planned" and "currently estimated". It places five features in that release: AI Infrastructure Quickstart, vSAN native S3-compatible object storage, VCF infrastructure configuration management and compliance, VPC policy-based connectivity, and native disaster recovery in VCF and VVF.

The sources disagree on when. StorageReview reads Q1FY27 as Broadcom's fiscal quarter, running through the start of February. The Register's 4 Nov piece gives calendar Q1 2027 for the S3 feature, which runs to 31 March, two months later. [Broadcom's 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001730168/000173016826000076/avgo-08022026x8kxex99.htm) ends fiscal Q4 2026 on 1 Nov 2026, so a 13-week quarter ends on 31 Jan 2027. That date is this site's arithmetic. The post doesn't define "Q1FY27".

They also disagree on status. The Register writes that VMware has added disaster recovery and policy-based VPC networking to VVF and VCF, previously sold as add-ons. The post calls both planned. The Feature Comparison, titled 9.1.1, already marks both rows (p6, p9), and marks object storage as tech preview for Edge and VCF only (p7). Follow the post on timing until release notes say otherwise.

VVF designs have the most riding on this, since native DR would change what a VVF site can do without add-ons. VCF designs that plan object storage or VPC policy depend on it too.

<Callout type="analysis" title="How to treat planned features in a design">
A design written now should work without all five, and record each as a dependency with a review date. Recheck when the release notes and that release's Feature Comparison appear. The first thing to read is the RPO, RTO and scale of native DR: [StorageReview](https://www.storagereview.com/news/broadcom-vcf-portfolio-update-vsphere-express-live-recovery) notes Broadcom hasn't published targets. This note is due for review on 15 Feb 2027, after the fiscal reading of the window closes.
</Callout>
