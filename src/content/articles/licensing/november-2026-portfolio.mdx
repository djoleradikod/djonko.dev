<InShort>
No source says a running estate must change on the day <AsOf date="2026-11-06" />. What moved is what you can order and which features are promised next.

- **Announced by Broadcom:** vSphere Express, the EEA dates for vSphere 8 Standard and Enterprise Plus, three Advanced Services, DSM moving to TrueSource.
- **Only reported:** every Express price, the one-year term, the fixed 128-core pack and who qualifies.
- **Only planned:** native disaster recovery, VPC policy-based connectivity, vSAN S3 and infrastructure compliance. Keep them out of a design until a release ships them.
</InShort>

| If you run | What moved | Ask before relying on it |
|---|---|---|
| VCF 9.x | New catalog entries, and five features planned for the next release | Which release a Feature Comparison mark refers to |
| VVF 9.x | Native DR planned. HyperArmor marked as an add-on; Live Recovery isn't | Whether HyperArmor is orderable on VVF yet |
| vSphere 8 Standard or Enterprise Plus, in the EEA | On sale to 31 Oct 2027. Extended Support to subscription end or 31 Oct 2028, whichever is earlier | What Extended Support covers |
| vSphere 8 Standard or Enterprise Plus, elsewhere | Nothing from Broadcom. Resellers report end of sale in October 2026 | Your own contract and quote |
| VCF 5.x or VVF on vSphere 8 | Not named in the EEA paragraph. vSphere 8 general support still ends 11 Oct 2027 ([KB 459135](https://knowledge.broadcom.com/external/article/459135/end-of-general-support-for-vsphere-80.html)) | Whether anything extends to you: the sentence doesn't say so |
| Up to 128 cores in total | Express, if Broadcom accepts you | Eligibility, in writing |
| Advanced Cyber Compliance (ACC) | One add-on becomes two at expiry | Price of the two together |
| Data Services Manager (DSM) | New product family, licensing unstated | Metric and term at renewal |

This isn't advice to buy or renew, and it isn't legal or purchasing advice. Your order document decides what you hold. For edition contents cell by cell, use the [entitlement matrix](/vcf/library/value/using-what-vcf-includes/), which this note doesn't repeat.

## vSphere Express

Express touches vSphere 8 estates of 128 cores or fewer, and anyone drawing a small-site design. Its component and licensing limits are documented. Price, term, feature exclusions and who qualifies are only reported, so get eligibility in writing before a design depends on it.

Express runs on vSphere 9, is available now in all regions and is "for customers with 128 cores across all environments" ([4 Nov post](https://blogs.vmware.com/cloud-foundation/2026/11/04/broadcom-vcf-portfolio-updates/)). The post names five capabilities after "such as": vMotion, HA, Distributed Switch, vCenter Quick Patch, TPM 2.0 and vTPM 2.0.

More of the limits that shape a design are on the [TechDocs page](https://techdocs.broadcom.com/us/en/vmware-cis/vsphere/vsphere/9-1/vsphere-express.html) (2 Nov):

- There are no add-ons, and the default licence can't be split or shared across sites.
- The console blocks allocation above 128 cores "per customer or site ID".
- ESX, vCenter, VCF Operations and a license server are all mandatory components, with VCF Operations there for licensing only.
- A vSphere 8 estate upgrades to Express 9.1.1 or later from vCenter and ESXi 8.0, with Aria Operations 8.18.6 and vSphere Replication 9.0.2.2 as optional sources.
- It can later converge to VCF or extend to VVF.
- For the feature list, the page sends you to your sales representative.

Express is a per-core subscription priced "significantly lower" than VVF or VCF, a spokesperson [told The Register](https://www.theregister.com/virtualization/2026/11/04/vmware-returns-to-low-end-server-virtualization-with-vsphere-express/5307466), declining to give a figure. Instances are discrete with no central management, and releases will follow the VVF and VCF cadence, Broadcom's Prashanth Shenoy told the same reporter.

Channel sources fill in the rest, and each of these parties makes money from the answer.

Exactly 128 cores, one pack per end customer, no extension. New customer means no VMware purchase ever or for at least 3 years. No DRS, memory tiering, SR-IOV, GPU, Proactive HA, live patching, VCF Automation or Private AI |
| [C4C Group](https://www.c4cgroup.co.uk/guides/vsphere-express/), sells renewal negotiation and doesn't resell VMware, updated 4 Nov | Same limits, attributed to reseller briefings. States that Broadcom has published no price or eligibility rules |

Two channel sources give existing customers opposite answers, which is a reason to get eligibility in writing. C4C says your reseller should request Broadcom's authorisation. Software Express says the selection is already fixed and can't be applied for ("eine Bewerbung ist nicht möglich"), and expects details in mid-November at the earliest.

The price, term, pack size, exclusions and who qualifies all remain unconfirmed. So does whether a customer holding VCF elsewhere can buy Express for a small site. "Across all environments" in the post and "per customer" in TechDocs read against it, while "or site ID" leaves the question open, so a VCF customer's small-site design shouldn't assume Express yet.

One open sizing question is whether the 16-core minimum applies. It covers "most products", and the [Licensing Model](https://techdocs.broadcom.com/us/en/vmware-cis/vcf/vcf-9-0-and-later/9-1/licensing/licensing-overview/licensing-model.html) doesn't say whether Express is one. C4C's Express checklist counts every CPU at a minimum of 16, but it gives that as Broadcom's general rule and doesn't say Broadcom applies it to Express.

<Callout type="analysis" title="What 128 cores buys (constructed example)">
This assumes that the 16-core minimum applies, that each host has two sockets and that every host counts.

| CPU | Cores per host | Hosts that fit | Total |
|---|---|---|---|
| 16 cores | 32 | 4 | 128 |
| 24 cores | 48 | 2 (a third reaches 144) | 96 |
| 32 cores | 64 | 2 | 128 |

CPU choice sets the cluster size: on these hosts a third host for HA headroom is out at 24 cores per socket and up. vSAN isn't in the component list at all, and with no add-ons available this design has no route to extra vSAN capacity. Express also adds VCF Operations and a license server to host. It uses the same licensing infrastructure as VCF, so read the [180-day cycle](/vcf/library/licensing/180-day-cycle/) and confirm that it applies. Core rules are in [core counting and CPU choice](/vcf/library/value/core-counting-and-cpu-choice/).
</Callout>

## EEA terms for vSphere 8 Standard and Enterprise Plus

The new dates reach subscribers on vSphere 8 Standard and Enterprise Plus in EEA countries. For those customers, the editions "will remain available for sale until October 31, 2027", with Extended Support in active subscriptions until the subscription ends or 31 Oct 2028, whichever is earlier. All of this rests on one sentence at the end of the Express section of Broadcom's post, which gives no reason. It says nothing about perpetual licences, VVF or VCF 5.x, so ask before relying on it for any of them.

Outside the EEA, Broadcom's post doesn't state the position. The UK is outside the EEA and resellers report end of sale elsewhere in October 2026, C4C says, so the contract and quote in hand are what to work from.

An on-sale date is worth testing against a real quote. [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/virtualization/2026/11/05/vmware-quietly-offers-european-users-extended-sales-and-support-for-low-end-vsphere/5307732) says it often heard VMware seldom issued quotes for these editions, and that VMware's Paul Turner blamed channel incentives since fixed.

Any motive is only an outside reading. The paragraph was missing from the version VMware sent [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/virtualization/2026/11/05/vmware-quietly-offers-european-users-extended-sales-and-support-for-low-end-vsphere/5307732) before publication, that piece (5 Nov) says, and it reads the move as an apparent concession to European regulators. Independent analyst Michael Warrilow suggests "regulatory muscle" as the reason, and says he can find no detail on the level of support or the commitments required.

Three things remain unconfirmed, so settle them before a plan relies on these dates:

- What Extended Support includes. ESXi, vCenter and vSAN 8.x carry a date of 11 Oct 2027 in [KB 459135](https://knowledge.broadcom.com/external/article/459135/end-of-general-support-for-vsphere-80.html), which doesn't mention this offer.
- What makes a customer "in" the EEA: contracting entity or site of use.
- What happens if a term runs past 31 Oct 2028. Support stops at that date under the wording, and the remaining months aren't addressed.

<Callout type="analysis" title="Holding vSphere 8 past 11 Oct 2027">
Don't assume the whole design gets the extra year, because the sentence names two vSphere editions only. [Aria Operations 8.18](https://knowledge.broadcom.com/external/article/422643/end-of-general-support-for-vmware-aria-o.html) still ends on 11 Oct 2027, and none of these sources carries a statement from backup, storage or hardware vendors. A plan that holds vSphere 8 past that date needs an answer for each component beside it. Express doesn't fill the same slot: it has no version 8 keys, TechDocs says.
</Callout>

## Advanced Services

ACC holders feel this first, because a one-line renewal becomes two. They can keep using ACC until their entitlement ends, then have "the option to purchase" Live Recovery and Compliance and Configuration Management ([FAQ](https://www.vmware.com/docs/vmware-cloud-foundation-9-1-general-faqs) Q86). That reads as ACC splitting into two catalog entries, but it's an inference, since no Broadcom page says so outright. Read "new" with care too: cyber recovery was sold under Advanced Cyber Compliance as of November 2025, according to [KB 428834](https://knowledge.broadcom.com/external/article/428834), scoped to Live Recovery 9.0.x.

Recovery designs come next if they assume a capability sits in the core. vSAN replication and vSphere Replication are in, while EDR-backed cyber recovery and cloud DR are out (FAQ Q78 and Q79). The matrix records [where those answers conflict](/vcf/library/value/using-what-vcf-includes/).

All three services are separate purchases under footnote 13 of the [Feature Comparison](https://www.vmware.com/docs/vmware-cloud-foundation-9-1-feature-comparison-and-upgrade-paths) (p17 for the marks).

## Data Services Manager to TrueSource

If you have DSM in a design or on a renewal, compare your order document with the new name before the renewal quote arrives. None of the sources checked on 6 Nov 2026 says whether DSM's licence metric changes to the tiered model below, what happens to a current DSM term, or which engines stay covered.

[FAQ](https://www.vmware.com/docs/vmware-cloud-foundation-9-1-general-faqs) Q84 gives a date, 4 Nov 2026, and a location, "presented within TrueSource data services". That question asks whether DSM is included in VCF 9.1, and the answer doesn't say yes or no, so it settles no entitlement. DSM deploys independently of the VCF installer (Q85). The Feature Comparison marks TrueSource Data Services for Edge and VCF, with no VVF mark. The 4 Nov post doesn't mention DSM and the press reports checked on 6 Nov 2026 don't cover the move.

Broadcom's own pages conflict on whether DSM is an add-on, which is why the order document comes first. TechDocs still lists DSM as its own add-on (5 Nov), and Feature Comparison p10 names DSM among pre-bundled services, marked for Edge and VCF with no add-on footnote.

The [31 Aug TrueSource release](https://mfn.se/all/a/broadcom/broadcom-strengthens-spring-security-and-adds-coverage-of-java-python-and-node-js-ecosystems-with-truesource-e37fa20b) describes Data Services as artifacts, support and deployment expertise for PostgreSQL, RabbitMQ, MySQL and Valkey, with "tiered site licensing options". That release doesn't name DSM.

## The next release, Q1FY27

VVF designs have the most riding on this, since native DR would change what a VVF site can do without add-ons. VCF designs that plan object storage or VPC policy depend on it too.

The post places five features in that release: AI Infrastructure Quickstart, vSAN native S3-compatible object storage, VCF infrastructure configuration management and compliance, VPC policy-based connectivity, and native disaster recovery in VCF and VVF. It leaves the release unnamed and hedges twice, with "currently planned" and "currently estimated".

The sources disagree on when, by two months, and the post doesn't define "Q1FY27". StorageReview reads it as Broadcom's fiscal quarter, running through the start of February. Fiscal Q4 2026 ended on 1 Nov 2026 ([Broadcom's 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001730168/000173016826000076/avgo-08022026x8kxex99.htm)), so a 13-week quarter ends on 31 Jan 2027. The Register's 4 Nov piece gives calendar Q1 2027 for the S3 feature, which runs to 31 March.

They also disagree on status, and the post is the one to follow on timing until release notes say otherwise. It calls disaster recovery and policy-based VPC networking planned. The Register writes that VMware has added both to VVF and VCF, previously sold as add-ons. The Feature Comparison, titled 9.1.1, already marks both rows (p6, p9), and marks object storage as tech preview for Edge and VCF only (p7).

<Callout type="analysis" title="How to treat planned features in a design">
A design written now should work without all five, and record each as a dependency with a review date. Recheck when the release notes and that release's Feature Comparison appear. Read the RPO, RTO and scale of native DR first, since Broadcom hasn't published targets, [StorageReview](https://www.storagereview.com/news/broadcom-vcf-portfolio-update-vsphere-express-live-recovery) notes. This note is due for review on 15 Feb 2027, after the fiscal reading of the window closes.
</Callout>
