<InShort>
Build VCF 9 training around the life of a ticket, and open each topic on what changed from the version the room actually runs. A 5.2 team and a 9.0 team need different comparisons, so settle the baseline before writing a slide. Two smaller calls follow: show a diagram or a real error string before explaining it, and keep KB numbers in an index. The method comes from the [five-part series](/talks/), and it carries over to any platform change a team has to absorb.
</InShort>

## Settle the baseline first

A comparison only helps if its left column is what the room knows, and VCF 9 has moved twice for some teams. The facts each room needs differ like this <AsOf date="2026-11-07" />:

| Room runs | Open each topic on | What the comparison has to carry |
|---|---|---|
| 5.2.x | 5.x against 9.1 | The [SDDC Manager UI is being deprecated](https://techdocs.broadcom.com/us/en/vmware-cis/vcf/vcf-9-0-and-later/9-1/deployment/upgrading-cloud-foundation.html) and its [workflows now sit in VCF Operations and the vSphere Client](https://techdocs.broadcom.com/us/en/vmware-cis/vcf/vcf-9-0-and-later/9-1/building-your-private-cloud-infrastructure/sddc-manager-workflows.html). The [VCF Installer replaces Cloud Builder and its spreadsheet](https://www.vmware.com/docs/vmware-cloud-foundation-9-1-general-faqs). |
| 9.0.x | 9.0 against 9.1 | [Fleet lifecycle and SDDC lifecycle components replace the VCF Operations fleet management appliance](https://techdocs.broadcom.com/us/en/vmware-cis/vcf/vcf-9-0-and-later/9-1/lifecycle-management.html), and the upgrade [decommissions and powers off the appliance](https://techdocs.broadcom.com/us/en/vmware-cis/vcf/vcf-9-0-and-later/9-1/deployment/upgrading-cloud-foundation.html). |
| Nothing yet (new build at 9.1.1) | No comparison: start from the fleet diagram | [VCF Installer 9.1.1 deploys a new fleet directly at 9.1.1](https://techdocs.broadcom.com/us/en/vmware-cis/vcf/vcf-9-0-and-later/9-1/release-notes/vmware-cloud-foundation-9-1-1-0-release-notes.html). A [fleet is one or more VCF instances with an instance of VCF Operations and of VCF Automation](https://www.vmware.com/docs/vmware-cloud-foundation-9-1-general-faqs). |

<Callout type="analysis" title="When the room is mixed">
Broadcom allows a [sequential or skip-level upgrade from 5.2.x to 9.1.x](https://techdocs.broadcom.com/us/en/vmware-cis/vcf/vcf-9-0-and-later/9-1/deployment/upgrading-cloud-foundation.html), so some 5.2 teams can reach 9.1 without ever operating 9.0, and they have no use for a 9.0 column. Run two editions of the opening if the audience splits. If it can't be split, teach 5.x against 9.1 and give the 9.0 engineers the appliance change as a single aside, since it's the smaller story.
</Callout>

## Follow the ticket

Engineers meet a platform as a problem with a timeline: something is reported, they orient themselves, find the owning component, read the right log, then fix or escalate. Ordering the material that way answers "when would I need this?" before anyone asks it. Someone who joins halfway through still knows where they are in the story.

The order also shows what 9.1 offers at the end of that path. [Log Assist sends support bundles to Broadcom from the VCF Operations console](https://www.vmware.com/docs/vmware-cloud-foundation-9-1-general-faqs), which means the escalation step can be taught in a console the first module already covered.

Leave feature tours to [Broadcom's training and certification programs](https://www.vmware.com/docs/vmware-cloud-foundation-9-1-general-faqs) and spend the session on the ticket path.

## Show, then tell

Start each concept with something concrete: the fleet diagram, the exact message a customer will paste into a ticket, the path of a log file. The explanation comes second. A diagram of the mechanism, such as which service calls which and from which address, gives an engineer something to reason from when a failure doesn't match any slide.

## Keep references out of the way

KB numbers are necessary and they pull attention from the idea. [Daniele Procida's](https://diataxis.fr/colophon/) [Diátaxis](https://diataxis.fr/) treats reference and explanation as separate forms of documentation, for a reason that applies to slides: ["One hardly reads reference material; one consults it."](https://diataxis.fr/reference/) Treat a live session as explanation, and the numbers go in a compact index at the end, where people can look them up afterwards.

## Date anything with a sequence in it

Upgrade order goes stale faster than concepts do. The [9.1.1 release notes](https://techdocs.broadcom.com/us/en/vmware-cis/vcf/vcf-9-0-and-later/9-1/release-notes/vmware-cloud-foundation-9-1-1-0-release-notes.html), last updated 5 Nov 2026, say "DO NOT use the Upgrade All button in VCF Operations UI" until the 9.1.1 patching dependencies between components are met, and they carry a 12 Oct notice of new component patches. They also start any upgrade from 5.2.x or 9.0.x to 9.1.1 with VCF Operations 9.1.1. Put the date on any slide that gives a sequence, link the release notes from it, and check them again before each delivery.

## Building and testing it

Outline every module and agree the order before building any of them. Check each claim against a public KB, TechDocs page or reputable blog, and mark anything where only a title was seen.

One run-through with an experienced colleague who will say "that's not quite right" is cheaper than corrections in front of a full room. Keep the customer edition to what is public, and let the engineer edition name internal tools, both built from one source so they don't drift apart.

End every module with one thing people can use on Monday: a command, a checklist or a decision table. A module without one is background, and it may work better as a reading link. Keep the humour light, skip jokes that will age, and let the content carry the session.
