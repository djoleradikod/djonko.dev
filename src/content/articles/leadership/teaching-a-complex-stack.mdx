## 1. Follow the ticket

Engineers don't meet a platform as a product catalogue. They meet it as a problem with a timeline: something is reported, they orient themselves, find the owning component, read the right log, fix or escalate. Ordering the material that way means every module answers "when would I need this?" before it is asked. It also makes the series useful for people who join halfway through: they know where they are in the story.

## 2. Show, then tell

Each concept starts with something concrete: the fleet diagram, the exact message a customer will paste, the path of a log file. The explanation follows. A diagram that shows the mechanism, such as which service calls which and from which address, is remembered long after the bullet points.

## 3. Anchor on what changed

The audience already knows 5.x. Starting every topic with a two-column comparison respects that, and it puts the learning effort exactly where it's needed. It is also the format used throughout this site.

## 4. Keep references out of the way

KB numbers are necessary and distracting. On slides they sit in a compact, expandable index; on this site, in the KB drawer at the end of each note. People can look up the details later, and in the session they follow the idea.

## Building and testing it

- **Plan first.** Outline every module, agree the order, then build.
- **Vet the sources.** Every claim was checked against a public KB, TechDocs page or a reputable blog, and labelled where only a title was seen.
- **Dry run with a skeptic.** One run-through with an experienced colleague who will say "that's not quite right" saves a room full of corrections.
- **Two editions.** An internal edition can name internal tools. A customer edition keeps only what is public.

<Callout type="field">
End every module with one thing people can use on Monday: a command, a checklist, a decision table. If a module has no such thing, it's background, and it might be better as a reading link.
</Callout>

## What to avoid

- **Feature tours.** They're the vendor's job.
- **Humour that ages.** Keep it light, but the content should carry the session.
- **Unchecked claims.** One wrong slide undermines the other forty.
