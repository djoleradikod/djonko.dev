<InShort>
An assessment answers five questions, each of which decides part of the plan. **Where are we?** Versions, topology and drift decide the path and the first step. **What's in the way?** Baseline-managed clusters, unsupported hardware, add-on versions and certificates decide the pre-work. **What changes for the people?** The new consoles, SSO, licensing and the services runtime decide the training and the change plan. **What are the dependencies?** DNS, firewalls, IP pools and the depot decide the lead times. **What's the risk appetite?** Windows, rollback points and sequencing decide the schedule. The deliverable is a sequenced plan with owners, not an inventory.
</InShort>

## The question bank

**Where are we?**
- Exact versions of every component, including add-ons (Avi, replication, HCX, Aria products).
- Number of instances, domains and clusters, and which share NSX or SSO.
- Anything changed outside SDDC Manager: passwords, certificates, manual patches.

**What's in the way?**
- Clusters still on baselines rather than images (images are required from 9.0).
- Hardware and firmware against the compatibility guide for the target release.
- Certificates: issuer, expiry, and whether SANs will satisfy 9.1's stricter checks.
- Aria products and how each one maps to its 9.x successor.

**What changes for the people?**
- Who will operate VCF Operations day to day, and who owns licensing in the console.
- Identity: current SSO model, identity provider, and the target VCF SSO model.
- Runbooks that reference SDDC Manager UI paths that will move.

**What are the dependencies?**
- DNS and IP addresses for new components: VCF Operations, the License Server, the services runtime pool, VCF Automation.
- Firewall changes, especially outbound from runtime nodes in 9.1.
- Depot method: online, proxy, or offline.

**What's the risk appetite?**
- Change windows and freeze periods.
- Rollback expectations for each phase.
- Whether a lab rehearsal is required before production.

## The plan structure

1. **Pre-work** with lead times: DNS, IPs, firewall, certificates, images, add-on upgrades.
2. **Phase gates** in the official order, with a go or no-go check at each (<KB id={440630} />).
3. **Risk register**: each risk with likelihood, impact, owner and mitigation.
4. **People plan**: training, runbook updates, licensing ownership.

<Callout type="field">
Present the plan with one recommendation and the reason for it, plus the alternatives you considered. Say what you would do and why, and don't hand over five options.
</Callout>

## Pitfalls

- **Assessing only the management domain.** Workload domains carry risk too.
- **Ignoring add-ons.** They can decide the order.
- **No owner for licensing.** It can become a blocker.
