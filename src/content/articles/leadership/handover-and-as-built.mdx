<InShort>
The handover pack answers the questions the operations team will ask in their first month. **What is there?** An as-built: instances, domains, components, versions, names, addresses and owners. **Why is it like that?** The decision records. **Who holds the keys?** Credential and certificate ownership, the licensing portal owner, and break-glass access. **What has to happen regularly?** A calendar: backups checked, licensing deadlines, certificate renewals, health reports. **What do we do when...?** The first runbooks: power events, health checks, collecting a bundle, escalating.
</InShort>

## The pack

| Section | Contents |
|---|---|
| As-built | One table per instance: component, FQDN, IP, version, role, owner |
| Decisions | The decision records, with status |
| Access | Who owns which credentials and certificates, where they're stored, break-glass procedure |
| Calendar | Recurring tasks with frequency and owner |
| Runbooks | Shutdown and startup, weekly health check, support bundle, escalation |
| Contacts | Support contracts, account team, internal escalation path |
| Open items | Known issues, accepted risks, planned changes |

## Handing it over

1. **Walk through it** with the receiving team, on the live platform.
2. **Let them run one runbook** while you watch, and fix what confused them.
3. **Agree an end date** for support from the project team.

<Callout type="field">
Generate the as-built tables from the platform where you can (API queries for names, versions and addresses) rather than typing them. A generated table can be refreshed at the next upgrade. A typed one is out of date the day after handover.
</Callout>

## Pitfalls

- **Documents in a project folder** the operations team can't access.
- **No licensing owner** named. It becomes urgent at the worst time.
- **Handover by email.** Walk through it together.
