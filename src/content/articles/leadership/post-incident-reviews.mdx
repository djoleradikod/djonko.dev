<InShort>
Run the review within a few days, while memories and logs are fresh. Build a **timeline** from evidence: logs, tickets, chat. Separate the **trigger** (what happened) from the **conditions** (why it could happen, and why it wasn't caught sooner). Keep it **blameless**: people acted reasonably on what they knew, so ask what they knew. End with **two or three actions**, each with an owner and a date, and prefer actions that become **automatic checks** over actions that rely on memory.
</InShort>

## A structure

1. **Summary**: impact, duration, one-line cause.
2. **Timeline**: times in one time zone, each with its source.
3. **Detection**: how it was noticed and how it could have been noticed sooner.
4. **Trigger and conditions**: what happened, and what allowed it.
5. **What went well**: so it is kept.
6. **Actions**: owner, date, and how completion will be checked.

## Platform conditions, and the check that catches them

| Condition | Automatic check |
|---|---|
| Certificate expired unnoticed | Certificate expiry in the weekly report |
| Partition filled slowly | Disk trend per appliance |
| Password changed outside VCF | Weekly `sos --health-check` for disconnected accounts |
| Licensing deadline missed | Days-to-deadline in the monthly report |
| Backup silently failing | Last good backup per component |

See [scripted health checks](/vcf/library/labs/scripted-health-checks/).

<Callout type="field">
Close every review by asking "which check would have told us a week earlier?" If the answer is a check you can automate, that's the action that matters most.
</Callout>

## Pitfalls

- **Skipping the review** for incidents that "were only an hour".
- **Action lists that grow** every review and never shrink.
- **Naming individuals** in the cause. It ends honest reporting.
