<InShort>
VCF has [no "easy button" rollback](https://blogs.vmware.com/cloud-foundation/2025/12/18/upgrading-vmware-cloud-foundation-5-2-to-9-0-the-top-10-questions-answered/) for the whole platform, as Jonathan McDonald put it in December 2025, so your real control is refusing to start the next step. Run a go or no-go at each gate below. Treat the gate before ESX as the last one where you can roll NSX Manager back without Broadcom Support, which this site infers for 9.1 from the 9.0 guide, and hold for longer than a window only where a workload domain can wait after its management domain and doesn't share an NSX instance.
</InShort>

<Callout type="note" title="Corrected on 7 November 2026">
This note listed "between phases and between domains" as safe pause points with mixed versions "supported within limits". Broadcom's upgrade pages give no such list, and domains sharing an NSX instance can't finalize until all of them finish. It also credited the upgrade order to KB 440630 (the order is in TechDocs), applied a 9.0.2 snapshot procedure to every version, and gave unsourced gate checks for stale locks and disconnected accounts, which Broadcom's prerequisites now replace.
</Callout>

## The gates and the way back from each

The order is mandatory (<KB id={440630} />), and [the upgrade guide](https://techdocs.broadcom.com/us/en/vmware-cis/vcf/vcf-9-0-and-later/9-1/deployment/upgrading-cloud-foundation.html) sets it out for 5.2.x and 9.0.x sources <AsOf date="2026-11-07" />. The gates below follow it and merge smaller steps, such as vSphere Replication between VCF Operations and SDDC Manager. Coming from 5.2.x changes two plans. VMware Identity Manager and Aria Operations for Logs have no upgrade path, and [McDonald's June 2026 guide](https://blogs.vmware.com/cloud-foundation/2026/06/18/how-to-upgrade-to-vmware-cloud-foundation-9-1/) says vCenter must use Reduced Downtime Upgrade, so don't write in-place as your fallback.

[Customised content backed up](https://techdocs.broadcom.com/us/en/vmware-cis/vcf/vcf-9-0-and-later/9-1/deployment/upgrading-cloud-foundation/upgrade-backup-and-restore.html), because the upgrade resets it to defaults. Three details change what you write in the plan. <KB id={450430} /> gives the 9.0.2 to 9.1 phases and snapshots "all primary nodes" of VCF Operations, narrower than TechDocs' each node and <KB id={342576} />'s all nodes including cloud proxies. Take the widest, because the revert covers the proxies too. NSX and vCenter backups can be file-based or image-based on [the prerequisites page](https://techdocs.broadcom.com/us/en/vmware-cis/vcf/vcf-9-0-and-later/9-1/deployment/upgrading-cloud-foundation/upgrade-the-management-domain-to-vmware-cloud-foundation-5-2/upgrade-prequisites.html), yet [Broadcom's backup blueprint](https://techdocs.broadcom.com/us/en/vmware-cis/vcf/vcf-9-0-and-later/9-1/design/design-blueprints-for/security-modernization/vcf-backup-and-restore.html) lists NSX Manager as file-based only and recommends file-based for SDDC Manager and vCenter too. This site's call is to follow the blueprint, since <KB id={433822} /> also rules out VM snapshots on NSX. And the blueprint says VCF Operations image backups must capture all nodes at the same time and shouldn't run during dynamic threshold calculations, which start at 2 a.m. by default, so check that hour against your window.

## Why the ESX gate carries the most weight

[Broadcom's 9.0 guide to the manual NSX upgrade](https://techdocs.broadcom.com/us/en/vmware-cis/vcf/vcf-9-0-and-later/9-0/deployment/upgrading-cloud-foundation/upgrade-the-management-domain-to-vmware-cloud-foundation-5-2/upgrading-nsx--to-version-9/manual-upgrade-to-nsx-9-by-using-upgrade-coordinator/upgrade-management-plane-from-nsx-3-2-1-x-and-later.html) lets you roll NSX Manager back from its local backup until edge or host transport node upgrades begin. After that, Broadcom Support must first roll those nodes back. In 9.1, [Edges move to the end of the domain upgrade](https://techdocs.broadcom.com/us/en/vmware-cis/vcf/vcf-9-0-and-later/9-1/release-notes/vmware-cloud-foundation-9-1-0-0-release-notes/what-s-new/whats-new-vcf-ops.html), so ESX is the first step to cross that line. That's an inference, because the 9.0 page has no counterpart at the matching 9.1 path (checked 7 Nov 2026), so confirm the boundary with Broadcom before your plan relies on it.

The gate before this one already ties NSX and vCenter together. In the optimized sequence [NSX Manager upgrades before vCenter switchover](https://techdocs.broadcom.com/us/en/vmware-cis/vcf/vcf-9-0-and-later/9-1/release-notes/vmware-cloud-foundation-9-1-0-0-release-notes/what-s-new/whats-new-vcf-ops.html). Cancelling vCenter at that point would leave NSX upgraded, which is also inference from that order, so give NSX its own way back in the plan and judge both again before you let hosts start.

## Where a hold is documented

Broadcom's upgrade pages define no general pause points (checked 7 Nov 2026). The boundary they do document is [workload domains following "at any time after" their instance's management domain](https://techdocs.broadcom.com/us/en/vmware-cis/vcf/vcf-9-0-and-later/9-1/deployment/upgrading-cloud-foundation.html), as Day-N work (<KB id={440630} />). So write a long hold there, one domain at a time, and check [mixed versions](/vcf/library/upgrades/mixed-versions/) for which domains qualify.

A shared NSX instance removes that choice. [NSX, vCenter and ESX must upgrade on every domain sharing it](https://techdocs.broadcom.com/us/en/vmware-cis/vcf/vcf-9-0-and-later/9-1/deployment/upgrading-cloud-foundation/upgrade-the-management-domain-to-vmware-cloud-foundation-5-2/updating-vcenter-and-nsx.html) before finalize, and cancelling the automatic plan on one dependent domain cancels it on all of them. So schedule that group as a single change and let its slowest domain set the window.

A stop between steps inside a management domain has no status on those pages, and it can cost you in three ways:

- After VCF Operations upgrades, [certificate and password management for Automation, Identity Broker, Logs and Networks is unavailable](https://techdocs.broadcom.com/us/en/vmware-cis/vcf/vcf-9-0-and-later/9-1/deployment/upgrading-cloud-foundation.html) until each reaches 9.1.
- VCF Operations snapshots older than [72 hours cause performance issues](https://knowledge.broadcom.com/external/article/342576), and TechDocs says to delete them after the upgrade. Deleting them too soon loses the way back for the next phase, so a hold past that age trades performance against the revert. Record the removal time per phase.
- [The Mixed-Version page gives a vCenter upgraded to 9.1 an evaluation of up to 90 days](https://techdocs.broadcom.com/us/en/vmware-cis/vcf/vcf-9-0-and-later/9-1/licensing/licensing-overview/pre-version-9-licenses-and-support.html), and it needs a primary license with enough capacity in that time. [Mixed versions](/vcf/library/upgrades/mixed-versions/) covers which upgrades get that window. Check your terms with Broadcom, because this isn't legal or purchasing advice.

## The checklist at every gate

The first four lines come from [Broadcom's core prerequisites](https://techdocs.broadcom.com/us/en/vmware-cis/vcf/vcf-9-0-and-later/9-1/deployment/upgrading-cloud-foundation/upgrade-the-management-domain-to-vmware-cloud-foundation-5-2/upgrade-prequisites.html). The health line is Broadcom's readiness set for 9.1 (<KB id={454898} />), which needs a healthy report from each tool before the upgrade. Running these at every gate and adding the rest is this site's advice, because passwords, certificates and health change between phases.

```text
[ ] No failed workflows, and no SDDC Manager resource activating or in error
    (any of these: contact Broadcom Support before starting)
[ ] No active alarms on hosts or vSphere clusters
[ ] No expired or expiring passwords or certificates
[ ] Upgrade binaries present; release notes read for upgrade issues
[ ] SoS health check, VCF Diagnostic Tool for vSphere and management
    services platform checks show nothing failed or critical
[ ] Backups for this gate's components, success confirmed on the target
[ ] Snapshots only where this gate allows them, with a removal time
[ ] Prechecks run, every error fixed, every warning explained and accepted in writing
[ ] Named people available: platform, network, identity, decision owner
[ ] Time left exceeds this phase's measured duration plus margin
```

## During the window

ESX is where a stop rule can fail you. Clusters [upgrade in parallel by default](https://techdocs.broadcom.com/us/en/vmware-cis/vcf/vcf-9-0-and-later/9-1/deployment/upgrading-cloud-foundation/upgrade-the-management-domain-to-vmware-cloud-foundation-5-2/upgrade-esxi-for-vmware-cloud-foundation-5-2-1.html), and when one fails the upgrade continues on the rest. If your rule is "stop at the first failure", pick the sequential option or small batches, and accept the longer window.

Two more limits belong in the runbook. After the Edge upgrade and finalize, [allow about 15 minutes for auto-reconciliation](https://techdocs.broadcom.com/us/en/vmware-cis/vcf/vcf-9-0-and-later/9-1/deployment/upgrading-cloud-foundation/upgrade-the-management-domain-to-vmware-cloud-foundation-5-2/update-the-nsx-edge-cluster-and-finalize-nsx-update.html) before scheduling another upgrade, or that upgrade may be cancelled or reconciliation may fail. A 9.1.0.x to 9.1.1 patch window has [its own order](https://techdocs.broadcom.com/us/en/vmware-cis/vcf/vcf-9-0-and-later/9-1/release-notes/vmware-cloud-foundation-9-1-1-0-release-notes.html): don't press Upgrade All until the dependencies are met, patch the management services fleet lifecycle component first, and run nothing else while VCF Operations patches.

<Callout type="analysis" title="Rules to agree in the planning meeting">
Keep a timestamped log of starts, finishes, warnings and decisions. Put a time on each revert decision, because McDonald's own practice is to assess and roll back, or [call support if the window is running out](https://blogs.vmware.com/cloud-foundation/2025/12/18/upgrading-vmware-cloud-foundation-5-2-to-9-0-the-top-10-questions-answered/). A constructed example: "If VCF Operations isn't on 9.1 by 02:00, revert and stop." If a phase fails twice with the same cause, hold and investigate before a third attempt. Agree all of this beforehand, so the decision owner applies a rule in the window instead of arguing one.
</Callout>
