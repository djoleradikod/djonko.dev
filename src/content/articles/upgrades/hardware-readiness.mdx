<InShort>
**Check five things in the order of what a failure costs: CPU, server model, vSAN devices, NICs and other I/O, boot device.** Stop at the first failure that replaces the host, because the later checks then apply to a different machine. If you are buying, reject anything deprecated. If you are upgrading, deprecated means proceed and budget the refresh. Three results can stop an upgrade outright: a discontinued CPU, an End of Life I/O device and a boot disk under 8 GB. An unlisted server or vSAN device costs you support, and can fail VCF deployment validation.
</InShort>

Model-by-model lists live in the [deprecation radar](/vcf/library/architecture/deprecation-radar/) and the KBs below <AsOf date="2026-11-06" />.

| # | Check | Blocks you | Only deprecated | Where to check | If it fails |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | CPU | Discontinued series: the installer halts | Cascade Lake, EPYC 7001 and 7002, and others. Skylake-SP, through an override | Tables 1 and 2 of <KB id={318697} />, then <KB id={428874} /> | Blocked: replace the host. Deprecated: see below |
| 2 | Server model | Not listed for your target release: no support, and validation can fail on vSAN | Listed as "VCF Supported. Confirm w/Vendor" | Broadcom Compatibility Guide, server program | Ask the OEM to submit it, or replace |
| 3 | vSAN devices | ESA without listed NVMe TLC devices. Not relevant without vSAN | [OSA hybrid, OSA over RDMA](https://techdocs.broadcom.com/us/en/vmware-cis/vcf/vcf-9-0-and-later/9-0/release-notes/vmware-cloud-foundation-90-release-notes/platform-product-support-notes/product-support-notes-vsan.html) | vSAN section of the Guide, ReadyNode bill of materials | Stay on all-flash OSA, or buy devices. | 4 | NICs, HBAs, other I/O | End of Life devices: ESX won't claim them | Restricted devices, and the NICs named in the 9.1 notes | Spreadsheets on <KB id={391170} />, the Guide, [9.1 notes](https://techdocs.broadcom.com/us/en/vmware-cis/vcf/vcf-9-0-and-later/9-1/release-notes/vmware-cloud-foundation-9-1-0-0-release-notes/vcf-91-product-support-notes.html) | Replace before the upgrade starts |

<Callout type="analysis" title="Whose reasoning this is">
Broadcom publishes the statuses and doesn't rank them. The order and the buy-or-proceed rule are this site's, and they follow the money: each step can make the later ones pointless, and step 5 is the cheapest fix.
</Callout>

## 1. CPU decides whether the host has a future

CPU support ends in two phases (<KB id={318697} />). Discontinued means "Installation will be halted". Deprecated means the installer warns and continues, and the CPU stays supported until the next major release, the one after 9.x, discontinues it.

So a deprecated result limits the hardware's life without giving a date. Upgrade Cascade Lake or EPYC Rome hosts and plan their replacement, and don't buy them. A series in neither table has no notice against it today, and the KB shows nothing beyond that. Ice Lake and EPYC 7003 are in that position, so go on to step 2.

Keep Skylake-SP hosts if you hold support terms from Intel or an OEM and the model is listed, but don't price a purchase around them. Customers without those terms "bear the risks", and Skylake-SP was extended to 9.1.x on 4 July 2026, then on 18 September to all of 9.x in deprecated mode, and no further ([KB 428874](https://knowledge.broadcom.com/external/article/428874)). The install route is in a PDF attached to that KB, and its filename says override.

Identify Skylake hosts by model number: Skylake-D and Skylake-W are discontinued, yet they share raw CPUID `0x00050654` with Skylake-SP. A software-only RPQ for End of Life hardware needs VMware management approval, so don't plan on one you don't hold.

Per Epping's [Franken cluster post](https://www.yellow-bricks.com/2025/05/28/does-vsan-support-a-franken-cluster-configuration/) (28 May 2025), vSAN allows an AMD replacement alongside an old Intel cluster, but vMotion doesn't work across the mix, and he treats it as a short migration state. Read it if that is your replacement.

## 2. Server model decides who answers the phone

Lam frames this step in his [hardware considerations post](https://williamlam.com/2025/05/vcf-9-0-hardware-considerations.html) (30 May 2025, before 9.0 shipped): "Our OEM partners ultimately decide which devices to certify for each release". A deprecated CPU is supported only on a listed model, so ask the OEM to submit a missing one (KB 318697).

Upgrade on "VCF Supported. Confirm w/Vendor" only with the OEM's support in writing. The status is easy to misread. It marks systems the OEM didn't submit: VMware gives best-effort fixes for VCF software only, hardware and firmware support comes from the OEM, and the system should be replaced at the earliest availability ([KB 428874](https://knowledge.broadcom.com/external/article/428874)).

## 3. vSAN devices can be the most expensive answer after the host

The architecture choice belongs to [the vSAN note](/vcf/library/architecture/vsan-esa-osa/), and the hardware test is short. [TechDocs](https://techdocs.broadcom.com/us/en/vmware-cis/vcf/vcf-9-0-and-later/9-1/vsan-deployment-administration-and-monitoring/vsan-planning-and-deployment/requirements-for-creating-a-virtual-san-cluster/hardware-requirements-for-virtual-san.html) asks ESA for NVMe TLC devices and 128 GB of host memory, with capacity devices, drivers and firmware listed in the vSAN section of the Guide. Drives come from the OEM's ReadyNode bill of materials, with floors of 1.6 TB and 1 DWPD, and there is "no plan" for SAS or SATA (<KB id={326717} />). An emulated ReadyNode built from listed parts is supported, but [generic builds](https://blogs.vmware.com/cloud-foundation/2023/09/13/support-for-readynode-emulated-configurations-in-vsan-esa/) from non-ReadyNode vendors aren't.

A failure here can mean two things, so tell them apart before pricing drives. Deployment validation can fail because the hardware really isn't listed (<KB id={427591} />). It can also report a false "Not HCL compatible" on verified ReadyNodes when the installer's compatibility data is over 90 days old (<KB id={442952} />, VCF 9.0.x, Dell example). Refresh that file first, and price drives only for a failure that remains.

Before a vSAN purchase, hear Epping and John Nicholson's [episode #113](https://www.yellow-bricks.com/2026/03/02/113-procuring-hardware-for-a-vsan-based-vcf-infrastructure-featuring-john-nicholson/) (2 March 2026) on ReadyNodes, NICs and switches, reuse under ESA and memory prices. Epping works at Broadcom, so weigh the reuse case accordingly.

## 4. NICs and I/O can do the most damage

Upgrading a host with an End of Life device may fail, and "non-recoverable consequences may occur", including lost storage access, network access and host configuration ([KB 391170](https://knowledge.broadcom.com/external/article/391170)). A halted installer leaves you where you started, and this failure can leave you worse off.

Compare your devices with the two spreadsheets attached to the KB. It is written for 9.0, and the later NIC notices appear only in the [9.1 notes](https://techdocs.broadcom.com/us/en/vmware-cis/vcf/vcf-9-0-and-later/9-1/release-notes/vmware-cloud-foundation-9-1-0-0-release-notes/vcf-91-product-support-notes.html), so read those too. Restricted devices work, with best-effort fixes and removal in the next major, so run them but don't buy them.

Don't rely on [vSphere Lifecycle Manager's hardware check](https://techdocs.broadcom.com/us/en/vmware-cis/vsphere/vsphere/9-0/managing-host-and-cluster-lifecycle/hardware-compatibility-checks.html) to stop you. It reports these problems, but "hardware incompatibilities do not prevent remediation" unless you configure it to stop, so read its result yourself.

## 5. Boot device is the cheap one, and the documents disagree

Specify 128 GB or more on anything you buy and the disagreement below stops mattering. The [ESX 9.1 requirements](https://techdocs.broadcom.com/us/en/vmware-cis/vsphere/vsphere/9-1/esx-installation-and-setup/installing-and-setting-up-esxi/esxi-requirements/esxi-hardware-requirements.html) (2 Nov 2026) require 32 GB and advise 128 GB for new servers. <KB id={317631} />, undated, says "128 GB minimum for 9.x". The [vSAN 9.1 page](https://techdocs.broadcom.com/us/en/vmware-cis/vcf/vcf-9-0-and-later/9-1/vsan-deployment-administration-and-monitoring/vsan-planning-and-deployment/requirements-for-creating-a-virtual-san-cluster/hardware-requirements-for-virtual-san.html) (5 Nov 2026) still describes USB or SD at 32 GB, and 16 GB SATADOM or disk on hosts above 512 GB of memory. The [design library](https://techdocs.broadcom.com/us/en/vmware-cis/vcf/vcf-9-0-and-later/9-1/design/design-library/vsphere-detailed-design/esx-design.html) recommends 128 GB for management hosts.

Leave SD and USB boot off a purchase as well, since newer OEM servers carrying them "will not certify successfully" (KB 317631). On existing hosts, a 16 GB device clears the 8 GB block but is deprecated for removal in the next major, as is USB or SD boot, so swap them at the next maintenance window.

## What to read off an existing host

- **Model:** `esxcli hardware platform get` shows vendor and product name, as firmware reports them ([KB 384493](https://knowledge.broadcom.com/external/article/384493/hardware-information-in-esxi-host-summar.html)).
- **CPU:** `esxcli hardware cpu list` lists the host's CPUs ([ESXCLI reference](https://developer.broadcom.com/xapis/esxcli-command-reference/latest/namespace/esxcli_hardware.html)). Match the model number to the KB tables.
- **I/O and NVMe IDs:** `esxcli hardware pci list` gives vendor, device, subvendor and subdevice IDs ([KB 452030](https://knowledge.broadcom.com/external/article/452030/how-to-identify-viddidsvidssid-for-esx-d.html)).
- **Boot device:** `esxcli storage core device list | grep -B 30 "Is Boot Device: true"` ([KB 305267](https://knowledge.broadcom.com/external/article/305267/determine-the-boot-drive-for-the-esxi-ho.html), written for hosts up to 8.x).
- **Partition sizes:** `esxcli storage filesystem list` ([ESX System Storage Overview](https://techdocs.broadcom.com/us/en/vmware-cis/vsphere/vsphere/9-0/esx-installation-and-setup/installing-and-setting-up-esxi/esxi-requirements/esxi-system-storage-overview.html)).

For a fleet, Lam's [Compatibility Guide scripts](https://williamlam.com/2025/05/programmatically-accessing-the-broadcom-compatibility-guide-bcg.html) (6 May 2025) take those IDs and return listed releases for I/O and vSAN devices. He says no official API sits behind them, so confirm in the Guide itself anything you'll spend money on.

## A services firm's checklist, checked

[eCorpIT](https://ecorpit.com/vsphere-8-end-of-general-support-vcf-9-upgrade-2027/) sells this assessment and published its version on 1 November 2026. Its CPU table is right, and its checklist has one real hole: it doesn't mention I/O or boot devices.

| eCorpIT wrote | Broadcom says | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Blocked CPU list; Skylake-SP "via override, 9.x only" | <KB id={318697} /> and <KB id={428874} /> agree | Holds. It omits E3-1500 v5 |
| Deprecated CPUs are "still supported for the whole 9.x line" | KB 318697: only while the OEM keeps the model listed | Incomplete |
| "Confirm w/Vendor" models "get VMware software support" | KB 428874: "Best effort" for VCF software only | Overstated |
| "Beyond CPUs, check four things": server listing, the move from vLCM baselines to images, vVols, NSX Federation | <KB id={391170} /> and the 9.1 notes add I/O devices and boot devices | The page mentions neither, and an End of Life device is the one that can cost you storage and network access |

Once your five answers are in, take them to the [upgrade route chooser](/vcf/library/upgrades/upgrade-route-chooser/) and the [support clock](/vcf/library/upgrades/support-clock/).
