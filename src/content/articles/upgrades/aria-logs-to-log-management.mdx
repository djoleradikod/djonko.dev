<InShort>
Aria Operations for Logs 8.x cannot be upgraded in place. In 9.0 you deploy **VCF Operations for logs** as a new cluster, run it in parallel, move integrations across, and transfer up to 90 days of history (<KB id={402314} />). In 9.1 the appliance disappears altogether. **Log management** becomes a Day-N service on the services runtime, searched from VCF Operations under Operate > Logs. vCenter and NSX collection is off until you enable it, and some upgrade screens behave in ways the documentation does not warn you about.
</InShort>

## 5.2 or 8.x to 9.0

1. Deploy VCF Operations for logs 9.0 as a new instance. Both can run together.
2. Remove the integrations from the 8.18 instance, then configure them on the new one: vCenter, NSX, ESX syslog targets and agents.
3. Transfer the history you need, up to 90 days.
4. Keep 8.18 read-only for reference until the retention requirement is met, then retire it.

## 9.0 to 9.1

Log management installs on the services runtime as a Day-N service, and the runtime may add workers to fit it.

- **Run Prechecks returns HTTP 500.** There is no back-end precheck for this side-by-side move. Click Upgrade instead (<KB id={442588} />).
- **Stuck at "Install VCF services runtime"** while the 9.0 log VMs still run. The work often finished and only the status was lost. Restarting the fleet and SDDC build and upgrade pods clears it (<KB id={443718} />).
- **Backup of "Operations Logs" fails** on a corrupt backup repository. Fixed in 9.1.1 (<KB id={451919} />).
- **"Log management was not discovered with the provided import details".** The 9.0 cluster was imported with wrong details. Recovery involves deregistering and redeploying (<KB id={451907} />).

## After the move

- **Enable collection** for vCenter and NSX under VCF Operations, Administration, Integrations, VMware Cloud Foundation. You can collect directly or through a cloud proxy, which round-robins across log endpoints.
- **Re-check vCenter after its 9.1 upgrade.** vCenter 9.1 ships its own agent, and the upgrade wipes the old syslog configuration. Re-enable collection (<KB id={433089} />).
- **Trust chain.** A missing intermediate CA in the runtime's platform trust breaks the VCF Operations to log management integration, and shows the "Install" screen where logs should be (<KB id={441515} />).
- **Noisy "logs are getting dropped" banner.** Often a false positive from a tight dynamic threshold. Recalculate thresholds before chasing it (<KB id={451758} />).
- **Disaster recovery.** Restoring log management onto a rebuilt runtime needs a post-restore step (<KB id={436904} />).

<Callout type="field">
Agree the history requirement with the security team before the project starts. "We need a year of logs" is a different design from "90 days is fine". It can mean keeping the old cluster for months, or exporting to a long-term store.
</Callout>

## Pitfalls

- **Assuming logs keep flowing after the vCenter upgrade.** They don't. Add a log check to the post-upgrade test list.
- **Certificates without SANs.** 9.1 removed the CN fallback for log forwarders. Reissue certificates with SANs.
- **Forgetting the buffer.** Cloud proxies buffer about 20 GB when log management is unreachable. That is enough for a maintenance window, not for a week-long outage.
