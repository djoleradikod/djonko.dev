<InShort>
**Choose by who owns your PKI.** If Microsoft AD CS is already your enterprise PKI, configure it once at fleet level with Basic authentication. Otherwise stay on VMCA, the default, which needs no CA configuration and auto-renews once the toggle is on. Take an external CA only when policy demands that chain, because every one of those certificates becomes a manual replacement for its whole life. OpenSSL is for test and development. Keep ESX on VMCA unless policy says otherwise, for the reason under [ESX and vCenter](#esx-and-vcenter-run-their-own-rules).

**Auto-renewal runs 60 days before expiry**, for end-entity certificates on components with non-disruptive updates. A certificate already inside 60 days needs a manual renewal first, and an expired one can't be renewed from VCF Operations.
</InShort>

The issuer map and the list of who stores whose thumbprint live in [the trust model](/vcf/library/architecture/trust-model/). This note covers what to do with them.

## CA type against renewal behaviour

Each cell names its release. The 9.1.1 column comes from [Broadcom's operations table](https://techdocs.broadcom.com/us/en/vmware-cis/vcf/vcf-9-0-and-later/9-1/fleet-management/certificate-management-9-0.html) and the [auto-renewal page](https://techdocs.broadcom.com/us/en/vmware-cis/vcf/vcf-9-0-and-later/9-1/fleet-management/certificate-management-9-0/set-up-automatic-renewal-of-certificates-in-vmware-cloud-foundation.html), both updated 5 Nov 2026.

| CA type | 9.1.1: auto-renew and manual renew | 9.1.1: what it costs you | Earlier releases | Pick it when |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **VMCA** (default) | Yes to both, 60 days before expiry. No CA configuration needed | Can't sign wildcard SANs. Renewal validity is 730 days in 9.x <AsOf date="2026-11-07" />, a known issue. "Later versions" should drop to no more than 397, with no release named (<KB id={425527} />), so read the first renewed certificate | 9.0.2 added ESX and Machine SSL renewal at 60 days (<KB id={427937} />) | No policy requires your own chain. It is [meant for certificates inside your environment](https://blogs.vmware.com/cloud-foundation/2026/10/09/certificates-trust-network-encryption/), "not a general-purpose CA" |
| **Microsoft CA** | Yes to both, once configured at fleet level | Basic authentication on the CA, a template, a service account with certificate management privileges | 9.1 and later: CA and Web Enrollment roles can sit on separate machines. Earlier releases: [same server](https://techdocs.broadcom.com/us/en/vmware-cis/vcf/vcf-9-0-and-later/9-1/fleet-management/certificate-management-9-0/configure-a-certificate-authority_9-0.html). 9.1.0 after upgrade from 9.0.1: can be misread as External CA (<KB id={443150} />) | AD CS is already your PKI |
| **OpenSSL** | Yes to both, once configured | Self-signed root on VCF Operations. [Can't chain](https://techdocs.broadcom.com/us/en/vmware-cis/vcf/vcf-9-0-and-later/9-1/fleet-management/certificate-management-9-0/configure-a-certificate-authority_9-0.html) into an external hierarchy. Renewal adds 397 days (<KB id={425527} />, 9.x) | 9.0: [ran on SDDC Manager, instance components only](https://blogs.vmware.com/cloud-foundation/2026/10/14/managing-certificates-at-scale-with-vmware-cloud-foundation-9-1-1/). 9.1 and later: on VCF Operations, for instance and management components | Test or development, which is the use Broadcom documents |
| **External CA** (DigiCert and the like) | No to both. Generate CSR, import, replace, every time | One manual change per certificate per validity period, plus re-trust each time | [Replace with VMCA-signed](https://techdocs.broadcom.com/us/en/vmware-cis/vcf/vcf-9-0-and-later/9-1/release-notes/vmware-cloud-foundation-9-1-1-0-release-notes/vcfoperations-9-1-1-0-release-notes.html) is new in 9.1.1, so before it the only route off was another CSR | Policy requires a chain you can't issue from AD CS |
| **Self-signed** | No to both | Managed components can't stay self-signed, so replace them | [9.1.1 labels them self-signed](https://techdocs.broadcom.com/us/en/vmware-cis/vcf/vcf-9-0-and-later/9-1/release-notes/vmware-cloud-foundation-9-1-1-0-release-notes/vcfoperations-9-1-1-0-release-notes.html). The [9.0 page](https://techdocs.broadcom.com/us/en/vmware-cis/vcf/vcf-9-0-and-later/9-0/fleet-management/certificate-management-9-0/set-up-automatic-renewal-of-certificates-in-vmware-cloud-foundation.html) and <KB id={427937} /> still list them as auto-renewable, so don't plan on that after 9.0 | Never in production |

Cost is what separates the top row from the fourth. An external chain looks tidy until the renewal date arrives on dozens of components, each a three-step job with its own re-trust. If compliance only asks that certificates aren't self-signed, VMCA already meets that and renews itself. Plankers's [October post](https://blogs.vmware.com/cloud-foundation/2026/10/09/certificates-trust-network-encryption/) is good on the wider point: a certificate mostly proves encryption, and trusting the other end is a separate decision.

The Fleet Management appliance's own CA, which the [June 2025 VCF blog](https://blogs.vmware.com/cloud-foundation/2025/06/19/automatic-certificate-renewal-in-vcf-9/) described, isn't among the [9.1 CA types](https://techdocs.broadcom.com/us/en/vmware-cis/vcf/vcf-9-0-and-later/9-1/fleet-management/certificate-management-9-0/configure-a-certificate-authority_9-0.html). "Configure CA for Fleet" there only takes Microsoft CA or OpenSSL settings. [The upgrade guide](https://techdocs.broadcom.com/us/en/vmware-cis/vcf/vcf-9-0-and-later/9-1/deployment/upgrading-cloud-foundation.html) decommissions the fleet management appliance during the upgrade, and that's the likely reason, though Broadcom doesn't connect the two. The OpenSSL form still asks for the SDDC Manager FQDN as its Common Name even though the CA now runs on VCF Operations, so enter what the form asks for.

## What the toggle doesn't reach

Choosing a CA settles only one kind of certificate. [Broadcom's three categories](https://techdocs.broadcom.com/us/en/vmware-cis/vcf/vcf-9-0-and-later/9-1/fleet-management/certificate-management-9-0.html) decide whether VCF Operations can touch it at all.

| Certificate | Who renews it (9.1.1) | What VCF Operations gives you |
|---|---|---|
| User-managed | The toggle, or you | Full lifecycle |
| Externally managed | You, in the component's own UI, API or CLI | View and expiry alerts |
| System-managed | The component, during routine work such as upgrades | No view. An alert on unrecoverable failure, [with a KB link since 9.1.1](https://blogs.vmware.com/cloud-foundation/2026/10/14/managing-certificates-at-scale-with-vmware-cloud-foundation-9-1-1/) |
| Root and intermediate | Never auto-renewed | Intermediate expiry on vCenter and ESX is [tracked since 9.1](https://blogs.vmware.com/cloud-foundation/2026/10/14/managing-certificates-at-scale-with-vmware-cloud-foundation-9-1-1/) |
| vCenter STS and VMCA root | [Managed from 9.1.1](https://blogs.vmware.com/cloud-foundation/2026/10/14/managing-certificates-at-scale-with-vmware-cloud-foundation-9-1-1/). Treat as manual, since the 9.1 page excludes roots from auto-renewal | Certificate management, with no separate workflow named |

The docs page conflicts with itself on system-managed certificates: one table says no monitoring, while its capability list says VCF Operations monitors their expiry. The 9.1.1 notes promise expiry alarms across VCF components, including certificates VCF Operations doesn't manage. Assume the alarm exists but don't build a process on it. <KB id={456305} /> shows why: on 9.1.1 an identity broker Postgres certificate raises alerts that come back an hour after clearing.

The 9.1 pages publish no list of components with non-disruptive updates, so read the Auto-renewal column for each component before promising anything. The [9.0 page](https://techdocs.broadcom.com/us/en/vmware-cis/vcf/vcf-9-0-and-later/9-0/fleet-management/certificate-management-9-0.html) did list them: ESX, vCenter Machine SSL, NSX Manager and VIP, SDDC Manager, identity broker, VCF Operations, Automation, logs, networks and Fleet Management. New in 9.1.1 are [cloud proxy, license server and vSphere Supervisor](https://techdocs.broadcom.com/us/en/vmware-cis/vcf/vcf-9-0-and-later/9-1/fleet-management/certificate-management-9-0.html); HCX and Avi need [their management packs](https://techdocs.broadcom.com/us/en/vmware-cis/vcf/vcf-9-0-and-later/9-1/fleet-management/certificate-management-9-0.html).

Activate the instance toggle before any domain toggle. In 9.0 a [known issue](https://techdocs.broadcom.com/us/en/vmware-cis/vcf/vcf-9-0-and-later/9-0/release-notes/vmware-cloud-foundation-90-release-notes/component-specific/vcfoperations-90-known-issues.html) let the instance setting override domains to Off. The 9.1 known issues no longer list it, but the order costs nothing. Management components and instances are toggled separately. After setup, certificates can take up to 6 hours to appear and accounts up to 30 hours (6 + 24 on the 9.0 list; the 9.1.1 notes give the same 6-hour adapter collection cycle).

## ESX and vCenter run their own rules

Replacing ESX certificates from VCF Operations [changes `vpxd.certmgmt.mode` to Custom](https://techdocs.broadcom.com/us/en/vmware-cis/vcf/vcf-9-0-and-later/9-1/fleet-management/certificate-management-9-0.html). From then on each new host needs a CA-signed certificate before it is commissioned (<KB id={427937} />), which adds a step to every host you ever add. That's why the advice above keeps ESX on VMCA unless policy says otherwise.

The same KB describes a second renewal mechanism. Configured in vCenter (VVF 9.0.2 or vCenter 8.0 U3h), native renewal fires at 10 days for ESX, adjustable with `vpxd.certmgmt.certs.autoRenewThreshold`, and at 5 days for Machine SSL, fixed, with a 30-day alarm and no service restart. It needs mode `vmca`. The KB doesn't say whether Fleet Management renewal for hosts continues after the switch to Custom, so check the Auto-renewal column on the host list after your first CA-signed host.

<Callout type="note" title="Corrected on 7 November 2026">
This note said nothing auto-renews when the mode is `custom`, and gave 10 and 5 days as general thresholds. KB 427937 ties both to renewal configured in vCenter. Renewal configured in Fleet Management runs at 60 days.
</Callout>

## Getting SANs and replacements right

In 9.1 and later the component FQDN [must be a DNS SAN](https://techdocs.broadcom.com/us/en/vmware-cis/vcf/vcf-9-0-and-later/9-1/fleet-management/certificate-management-9-0.html), because VCF Operations does RFC 6125 hostname verification; an IP-only certificate fails. [A dot-less entry fails the whole CSR](https://techdocs.broadcom.com/us/en/vmware-cis/vcf/vcf-9-0-and-later/9-1/fleet-management/certificate-management-9-0.html). Behind a load balancer, [list the FQDNs and IPs](https://techdocs.broadcom.com/us/en/vmware-cis/vcf/vcf-9-0-and-later/9-1/fleet-management/certificate-management-9-0/generate-certificates-signing-requests-in-vmware-cloud-foundation.html) of the balancer and every node. A Supervisor with no FQDN can use a structured name such as `supervisor.local`, a Punycode label or `localhost`, whichever your CA accepts. The identity broker's exception is in [the trust model](/vcf/library/architecture/trust-model/). Before a planned replacement, export each component's SANs and compare them with the FQDNs registered in VCF Operations, which turns a mid-upgrade surprise into a paper exercise.

Broadcom contradicts itself on shortnames. <KB id={453793} /> wants the FQDN, IP and shortname of every VCF Operations node during a 9.0.x to 9.1 upgrade, and the [9.1 certificate page](https://techdocs.broadcom.com/us/en/vmware-cis/vcf/vcf-9-0-and-later/9-1/fleet-management/certificate-management-9-0.html) says a shortname kills the CSR. On 9.1.1, leave shortnames out, because the page governs the CSR workflow you're in. If you hit that error while still on 9.0.x, the KB is the only documented fix. That's an inference from the two scopes, and Broadcom doesn't reconcile them.

Run one certificate task at a time. The docs say bulk operations act "simultaneously", the [October blog](https://blogs.vmware.com/cloud-foundation/2026/10/14/managing-certificates-at-scale-with-vmware-cloud-foundation-9-1-1/) says components go in sequence with up to 10 in a run, and the 9.1.1 notes say concurrent rotation isn't supported. So 50 certificates means five runs, each finished before the next. Mid-upgrade, [the guide](https://techdocs.broadcom.com/us/en/vmware-cis/vcf/vcf-9-0-and-later/9-1/deployment/upgrading-cloud-foundation.html) blocks certificate work on Automation, identity broker, logs and networks until each reaches 9.1, so [rotate them before step 1](/vcf/library/day-2/runbook-diff-91/).

After any replacement, walk [the trust model's list](/vcf/library/architecture/trust-model/) of what stores the old certificate, including [vSphere Client plugins](/vcf/library/integrations/vsphere-client-plugins/), which are verified by thumbprint.

```bash
openssl x509 -in certificate.crt -pubkey -noout -outform pem | sha256sum

```

## When a certificate has already expired

VCF Operations [can't renew](https://techdocs.broadcom.com/us/en/vmware-cis/vcf/vcf-9-0-and-later/9-1/fleet-management/certificate-management-9-0/manually-renew-certificates-in-vmware-cloud-foundation.html) an expired certificate, and Broadcom's pages describe no replacement route from it either (checked 7 Nov 2026), so plan to work on the component. For vCenter, start by listing what expired:

```bash
# On vCenter, as root: every store entry with its expiry (KB 344201)
for store in $(/usr/lib/vmware-vmafd/bin/vecs-cli store list | grep -v TRUSTED_ROOT_CRLS); do
  echo "[*] Store: $store"
  /usr/lib/vmware-vmafd/bin/vecs-cli entry list --store "$store" --text | grep -ie "Alias" -ie "Not After"
done
```

Take a backup or a powered-off snapshot first. Linked vCenters are powered off together and each is snapshotted, and vCenter HA is removed before snapshots (<KB id={344201} />). Then use vCert (<KB id={385107} />, version 6.1.2 on 7 Nov 2026), option 1 to find what expired and option 6 to reset all certificates if several stores are affected, and restart services.

Across several component types, TechDocs points at `vcf-cert-tool` (<KB id={445742} />), which puts the vCert, CARR and certmgr repair workflows behind one tool. It's "intended to be used at the direction of Broadcom Support", so open a case first: it can leave a system inoperable, and it doesn't update trust relationships. Plan the re-trust walk afterwards.

Keep a scheduled expiry report even with auto-renewal on. Anything on auto-renewal that shows under 60 days means the renewal didn't run, since that's when it should have fired, and the report catches it before the certificate expires.
