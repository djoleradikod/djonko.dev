<InShort>
Four places fill up again and again: **SDDC Manager**, with old bundles and its database partition; **vCenter's** `/storage/log` and `/storage/seat`; the **VCF Operations** nodes; and, in 9.1, the **services runtime** volumes. SDDC Manager's precheck marks less than 1 GB free for its database or less than 10 GB in `/opt` as critical (<KB id={439946} />). The fix is rarely "delete files until it works". Each location has a documented clean-up and a reason it filled.
</InShort>

## SDDC Manager

- **Thresholds.** The precheck flags `dbDataDirFreeSpaceGb` at 1 GB or less and `vmOptDirFreeSpaceGb` at 10 GB or less as critical (<KB id={439946} />).
- **Old bundles** are the usual cause. Use `bundle_cleanup.py` to remove superseded bundles safely (<KB id={447248} />). Don't delete bundle directories by hand, because the database still references them.
- **When:** after every upgrade, and before downloading the next release.

## vCenter

- **`/storage/log` full from ESX syslog.** vCenter can end up receiving host syslog it was never sized for (<KB id={434671} />). Send host logs to the log platform instead, and clean up as the KB describes.
- **`/storage/seat` full.** Events, tasks and statistics outgrow the partition, often from chatty integrations or long retention settings (<KB id={410118} />). Fix the retention, then follow the KB to reclaim space.
- **Before every upgrade:** run the vCenter diagnostic tool, and check `df -h` on the appliance. The upgrade needs room on several partitions.

## VCF Operations

Analytics nodes need disk headroom for growth and for upgrades. Some features need extra disks on every node. The data retriever is one documented example (<KB id={318768} />). Watch the self-monitoring dashboards, and add disk before the cluster goes read-only.

## 9.1 services runtime

The management services dashboard shows volume utilisation with clear thresholds: up to 80% is good, 80 to 90% is warning, 90 to 95% is severe and over 95% is critical (<KB id={417255} />). Day-N services, especially log management, are the usual consumers. Don't try to clean runtime volumes from the node shell. Use the documented scale and retention options.

```bash
# Quick read-only checks
# SDDC Manager
df -h /data /opt /var/log /nfs/vmware/vcf/nfs-mount 2>/dev/null
# vCenter
df -h /storage/log /storage/seat /storage/db /storage/core
```

<Callout type="field">
Add a disk report to the monthly health check: one line per appliance and partition, with the percentage used. A trend line catches the slow fill-ups that cause failures in the middle of an upgrade.
</Callout>

## Pitfalls

- **Deleting logs that are evidence.** If you are mid-incident, collect a support bundle before cleaning.
- **Fixing the symptom, not the cause.** A `/storage/log` that filled from host syslog will fill again until the syslog target changes.
- **Snapshots consuming datastore space** under the appliances. Check the datastore, not just the guest partitions.
