<InShort>
Express Patch and maintenance release name what you install. Live patch and Quick Patch name how an ESX or vCenter build lands, and each build decides that for itself. Read the build's release notes before you size the window: two of the four ESX builds below needed no reboot, and two did. No vCenter notes page says Quick Patch yes, so keep a vCenter window until the patch details in the product show otherwise <AsOf date="2026-11-07" />.
</InShort>

## Choose by your change and your window

| Your situation | Do this | What it costs |
|---|---|---|
| Moving from 9.1.0.x to 9.1.1 | Maintenance release, in the [documented dependency order](https://techdocs.broadcom.com/us/en/vmware-cis/vcf/vcf-9-0-and-later/9-1/release-notes/vmware-cloud-foundation-9-1-1-0-release-notes.html). Fleet lifecycle first, and no Upgrade All until the dependencies are met. | ESX 9.1.1.0 reboots and moves VMs. vCenter 9.1.1.0 has no Quick Patch. |
| ESX Express Patch, Live Patchable Yes, no spare capacity or no window | Turn on Enforce Live Patch, so a host that needs a reboot is blocked and doesn't evacuate. | Partial maintenance mode. VMs that can't fast-suspend-resume stay on your list. |
| ESX Express Patch, Live Patchable No | Plan it as a rolling maintenance. If the window is short, use Custom Selection and split the cluster across windows. | Evacuation and reboot. Pinned VMs power off. |
| vCenter patch | Book a window unless the patch details show the Quick Patch icon, services and downtime. | Under a minute if Quick Patch applies, and up to an hour or more if not, by O'Leary's figures. |
| Management or SDDC Manager Express Patch | Fleet lifecycle first if it's in the set, then depot and runtime, then the rest. | No VM impact, by Hawley and Camarda's claim. A vendor claim, so read the notes. |

Read [William Lam's 8 July post](https://williamlam.com/2026/07/vcf-9-1-understanding-vcf-express-patches.html) for what an Express Patch is and where Sync and Change Target Version sit, his [15 July post](https://williamlam.com/2026/07/vcf-9-1-july-express-patch-is-more-than-security-fixes.html) for one set's contents, and his [10 August tip](https://williamlam.com/2026/08/quick-tip-selective-esx-host-patching-in-vcf-9-1.html) for host selection. Lam works for Broadcom. The 8 July post never mentions live patch, Quick Patch or restarts and leaves applicability to the release notes, so the table above is the decision it doesn't make.

## What each one covers and costs

| | Express Patch | ESX live patch | vCenter quick patch | Maintenance release |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Covers** | One component per build, numbered x.x.x.01xx to 99xx (<KB id={410435} />). Cumulative, per Lam. | Eligible ESX builds, including vmkernel, user-space daemons and NSX components. | Eligible vCenter builds. Replaces only the RPMs and binaries that changed. | Bug fixes and security. About quarterly in the first support phase (<KB id={410435} />). |
| **Doesn't cover** | Components with no build that month. | Major updates and driver changes, per McDonald. DPU hosts (<KB id={419942} />). | Version transitions, which go to Reduced Downtime Upgrade. | |
| **Restarts or moves** | Set by the component. | No reboot or evacuation. hostd may restart, with a brief vCenter disconnect. vmx patches can trigger fast-suspend-resume. | The patch details list services and estimated downtime. No opt-in. | Read each component. ESX 9.1.1.0 needs a reboot and VM migration or shutdown. |
| **Needs** | VCF 9.1 ([McDonald, 29 June](https://blogs.vmware.com/cloud-foundation/2026/06/29/installing-express-patches-with-vmware-cloud-foundation-9-1/)). | vLCM UI or API, not esxcli. No parallel remediation. TPM hosts from 9.1. | Described for VCF 9.1, with no stated minimum. | 9.1.0.x as the source for 9.1.1. |
| **Afterwards** | A per-component build in VCF Operations. | The compliance scan lists VMs that can't fast-suspend-resume, and they need migrating or power cycling. | No source describes a separate state. Check the build. | Same record. Support stays August 2028. |

The live patch column draws on [O'Leary, July 2025](https://blogs.vmware.com/cloud-foundation/2025/07/15/live-patch-gets-even-better-in-vsphere-with-vmware-cloud-foundation-9-0/) and [May 2026](https://blogs.vmware.com/cloud-foundation/2026/05/12/whats-new-with-vsphere-9-1/), and [McDonald, 8 Oct 2026](https://blogs.vmware.com/cloud-foundation/2026/10/08/upgrade-to-vmware-cloud-foundation-9-1-and-express-patching-webinar-top-10-questions-answered/). The quick patch column draws on [O'Leary, 12 May 2026](https://blogs.vmware.com/cloud-foundation/2026/05/12/vcenter-quick-patch/).

Check the live patch limits before relying on one. Partial maintenance mode [blocks new VMs and vMotion](https://blogs.vmware.com/cloud-foundation/2024/07/11/vmware-vsphere-live-patch/) on the host (2024 wording), so DRS can't relieve a hot host mid-cycle. VMs that can't fast-suspend-resume were, in July 2025, Fault Tolerance, DirectPath I/O, vSphere Pods and shared-disk clustering, but the May 2026 post lists live patch for newly supported DirectPath devices, so trust the scan over either list. Two narrower cases can also catch a cluster. vSAN compute clusters hit a pre-check failure on 9.1.0.0100 (<KB id={442180} />), and a baseline-to-image transition on 9.1.1 needs live patch off first (<KB id={456672} />).

"Security" doesn't predict "no reboot": 9.1.0.0200 is security only and still reboots. ## Where the sources disagree

- Blog posts can't settle whether a build gets Quick Patch, because they scope it three ways. O'Leary has "security patches", [Hawley and Camarda](https://blogs.vmware.com/cloud-foundation/2026/06/30/security-patching-in-vcf-9/) have "security and minor fixes", and [Hahn](https://blogs.vmware.com/cloud-foundation/2026/05/28/modernizing-the-private-cloud-why-vcf-9-1-lifecycle-management-is-a-game-changer/) ties it to "the frequency of security patches". O'Leary's example of a fit is 9.1.0 to 9.1.1, yet the 9.1.1.0 notes say No. Use the build's field.
- Lam's 8 July post allows any order, Fleet Lifecycle first if included. A week later he puts the depot service before the VCFMS runtime, and the 9.1.1 notes say a depot patch blocks other patching until it finishes. The table at the top uses that stricter sequence.
- O'Leary describes running Quick Patch from VAMI or CLI, but the vCenter notes say that in VCF "you apply this patch by using VCF Operations".
- Expect management patches to change behaviour. McDonald scopes an Express Patch as [CVEs and bug fixes](https://blogs.vmware.com/cloud-foundation/2026/10/01/navigating-the-ai-threat-era-upgrading-to-vmware-cloud-foundation-9-1-and-applying-express-patches/), but Lam's July post also shows new installer IP options.
- A suffix doesn't identify a set: Lam's "EP2" has parts with different suffixes (ESX 0200, SDDC Manager 0400). The names differ as well. Lam, McDonald and <KB id={410435} /> say Express Patch, while the TechDocs patch pages say "Patch Releases" and never use the term. TechDocs writes "Live Patching for ESX" and a "Quick Patch" field, O'Leary "ESX live patch" and "vCenter quick patch".

## How to track it, and what it doesn't change

Record a component and build pair per target, since a suffix won't identify a set. Custom Selection leaves a cluster's builds mixed until you finish, and Lam doesn't describe how compliance reports that, so log the excluded hosts and a return date. Sync, under Build > Lifecycle > VCF Management > Upgrade in VCF Operations, refreshes what's on offer (Lam). For a change board, [Lam's Upgrade Planner](https://williamlam.com/2026/10/major-enhancements-to-vcf-9-1-upgrade-planner-tool.html) (22 Oct) exports plans as text or Markdown.

A patch doesn't move your support date: 9.1.1 [runs to August 2028](https://blogs.vmware.com/cloud-foundation/2026/10/13/customer-favorite-capabilities-accelerating-your-path-to-vcf-9-1-1/), and [the support clock](/vcf/library/upgrades/support-clock/) explains why later 9.1.x releases appear to gain nothing.

[The rollback table](/vcf/library/upgrades/rollback-checkpoints/) still applies. ESX is forward only there, and the Broadcom pages cited here describe no way to undo a live patch (checked 7 Nov 2026), so plan a live patch as forward only too. NSX gets file backup, [never VM snapshots](https://knowledge.broadcom.com/external/article/433822). VCF Operations snapshots [hurt performance past 72 hours](https://knowledge.broadcom.com/external/article/342576), so each monthly patch needs its own.

Take any build whose CVE touches you, and otherwise hold a reboot-required ESX build one cycle. Cumulative builds make skipping a month cheap, and the next build may be live-patchable. That advice is this site's own, and Broadcom [argues against deferring](https://blogs.vmware.com/cloud-foundation/2026/10/01/navigating-the-ai-threat-era-upgrading-to-vmware-cloud-foundation-9-1-and-applying-express-patches/), which suits a vendor promoting its cadence. Timing also matters ahead of a major upgrade: an Express Patch taken before one can raise back-in-time issues, depending on when you take it, Lam warns in a [10 July comment on his 8 July post](https://williamlam.com/2026/07/vcf-9-1-understanding-vcf-express-patches.html). Run the planner first once a next release is named.

## A constructed example

The flag decides whether this cluster needs one window or two. Nothing here is measured. **Assumptions:** one 12-host cluster, patched serially; a 4-hour window (240 minutes); 35 minutes per host to evacuate and reboot; 6 minutes per host to live patch; two hosts carry pinned VMs that can't fast-suspend-resume.

- **Build says Yes.** 12 × 6 = 72 minutes, which fits the window, with Enforce on so nothing evacuates by surprise. The pinned VMs go on a list for power cycling at their owners' next outage.
- **Build says No.** 12 × 35 = 420 minutes, which misses the window. Six hosts fit (6 × 35 = 210; seven would be 245). Custom Selection takes six non-pinned hosts in window one, leaving builds mixed until window two takes the last six, with the pinned VMs powered off. Write the mixed state into the record.
