<InShort>
Each edition includes the following, going by Broadcom's tables, TechDocs, the bill of materials and the Edge contract terms, <AsOf date="2026-11-06" />.

- **vSphere Express:** ESX, vCenter, VCF Operations for licensing only and the license server, up to 128 cores per customer, with no add-ons. Broadcom publishes no full feature list.
- **VVF:** adds VKS, log and cost management and 0.25 TiB of vSAN per core. It has no NSX networking, VCF Automation, network flow analysis, predictive capacity or compliance management, and HCX is not a component.
- **VCF Edge:** the Feature Comparison marks it the same as VCF on every row but one (vSAN witness). The differences are contractual: Edge Locations only, 8 cores per CPU minimum, at least 10 sites in the first year, at most 256 cores per site.
- **VCF:** everything marked in the table, except that firewalling and general-purpose load balancing need add-ons.
- **Advanced Services:** none is included in any edition. Broadcom's documents disagree on whether VVF can buy vDefend and Tanzu.
</InShort>

This note describes entitlement only. It isn't advice to buy, renew or leave, and it isn't legal or purchasing advice: your order document and the program documentation for your product decide what you hold. The Broadcom documents cited here give no price for any of these (checked 6 Nov 2026), so none appears here.

## Where Broadcom's documents disagree

Get a written answer on these before relying on either reading. Each number matches a matrix cell.

1. **vDefend on VVF.** FC p17 marks vDefend Firewall with ATP as available for VVF. The TechDocs Advanced Services page lists vDefend for VCF only.
2. **Tanzu on VVF.** The same pages split the other way: FC p17-18 leaves VVF blank for Tanzu Platform and Tanzu Data Intelligence, and TechDocs lists both for VCF and vSphere Foundation.
3. **HyperArmor.** FC p17 marks it for VVF. The post describes it as a defense layer for the ESX component of VCF. The TechDocs Advanced Services page doesn't list it yet.
4. **Private AI and the PAIF-N add-on.** FC p9 and p16-17 mark Private AI for VCF with no condition, and FAQ Q15 lists Private AI Services in the VCF licence. FC p10 puts footnote 10, "Requires PAIF-N add-on", on the Private AI Services heading in VCF Automation. ADD says that from 3 Nov 2025 you "might also receive" a PAIF-N licence, based on your subscription. The Edge SPD settles it for Edge alone: the component is included only as your transaction document indicates.
5. **Data Services Manager.** FAQ Q84 says it moved into TrueSource data services on 4 Nov, and the BOM marks the Data Services VCF service as add-on. TechDocs still lists it as its own add-on, and FC p10 names DSM among pre-bundled services with no add-on footnote. Under the Edge SPD, a Private AI entitlement carries DSM only for PostgreSQL with Private AI Services.
6. **Replication and RPO.** FAQ Q78 (4 Nov) puts vSAN remote replication and vSphere Replication RPOs "as low as one minute" in the core VCF entitlement. [KB 428834](https://knowledge.broadcom.com/external/article/428834), scoped to Live Recovery 9.0.x and showing no date, says remote snapshot replication and RPOs under 5 minutes need Advanced Cyber Compliance. The FAQ and FC p7 are the newer 9.1 statements, but the BOM marks the Protection and Recovery VCF service as add-on and doesn't say which functions that covers.
7. **Disaster recovery.** FC p6, titled 9.1.1, marks a Disaster Recovery row for VVF, Edge and VCF. The post calls disaster recovery a new native feature in VCF and VVF, planned for the next release, which it estimates for Q1FY27. VPC policy-based connectivity has the same split (FC p9 against the post). Ask which release a mark refers to.
8. **Fleet lifecycle on VVF.** FC p13 says VVF customers use vLCM for lifecycle. The BOM lists fleet lifecycle and SDDC lifecycle components for VVF.
9. **vSAN witness.** FC p6 marks "vSAN Witness Host/2 Node Support" for Edge only, and its 2-Node Cluster row marks all three. The BOM lists witness appliances for VCF and VVF. FAQ Q71 presents a locally deployed witness as what sets Edge apart. The SPD confirms a witness licence for Edge, limited to embedded hardware at an Edge Location.

Four smaller mismatches don't change a cell:

- **The name Live Recovery.** The [Protection and Recovery 9.1 release notes](https://techdocs.broadcom.com/us/en/vmware-cis/vcf/protection-and-recovery/9-1/release-notes/protection-and-recovery-91-release-notes.html) (build date 12 May 2026) say VMware Live Recovery is now Protection and Recovery. The 4 Nov post, FC and TechDocs all sell VMware Live Recovery as an Advanced Service, so check which one an order line means. The BOM also marks Protection and Recovery Yes for VVF and Express, while FC and AS don't offer Live Recovery on VVF. EXP treats vSphere Replication as part of protection and recovery, which may explain the mark, but the BOM doesn't say.
- **External DNS on VVF.** FC p3 marks it and FC p10 doesn't. The BOM says yes.
- **The Express core limit.** The post says 128 cores "across all environments". EXP says 128 per customer, and that the console blocks allocation above 128 "per customer or site ID".
- **vSAN rounding on VVF.** [KB 313548](https://knowledge.broadcom.com/external/article/313548/vcf-subscription-portability-policy.html) rounds 0.25 TiB per core up to a whole TiB, but its version list predates 9. ADD doesn't mention rounding. Despite its URL, that KB doesn't cover portability.

## vSphere Express: what is and isn't known

Express is missing from the Feature Comparison and the FAQ, so its column rests on three Broadcom sources. EXP lists the mandatory components (ESX, vCenter, VCF Operations, license server) and the optional ones (vSphere Replication, VMware Tools, Remote Console). The post names five capabilities, introduced with "such as". EXP then says Express entitles you to "certain vSphere features" and tells you to consult your sales representative for the list. The BOM adds Protection and Recovery as a component and shows dashes for everything else in the Operations and Automation stack.

EXP sets these limits: 128 cores per customer, default licence only with no splitting or sharing across sites, no add-ons, version 9 or later only, and VCF Operations for licensing only. The same page documents converging an Express estate to VCF, and extending it to VVF by applying the VVF licence.

Exclusions come from parties with a commercial interest. [C4C Group](https://www.c4cgroup.co.uk/guides/vsphere-express/), which sells advice on buying and renewing enterprise technology, and reseller [Software Express](https://www.software-express.de/hersteller/vmware/vsphere/express/) both list DRS, memory tiering, SR-IOV, GPU and vGPU, Proactive HA, live patching, VCF Automation and Private AI as not included. C4C attributes this to reseller briefings and notes that Broadcom's announcement lists only what is included. Treat it as their claim until your sales representative confirms it in writing. [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/virtualization/2026/11/04/vmware-returns-to-low-end-server-virtualization-with-vsphere-express/5307466) reports that instances are discrete with no central management, that sales run through distributors, and that a spokesperson declined to give a price.

## HCX on VVF

The BOM shows a dash for VCF Operations HCX under VVF, and the HCX guide says a VVF site with no valid licence runs in Evaluation Mode, limited to 90 days and 20 migrations per migration type. Such a site can get feature inheritance from a paired VCF site, and [KB 440126](https://knowledge.broadcom.com/external/article/440126/hcx-licensing-requirements-for-migrating.html) describes a source site inheriting from a licensed VCF destination. FC p6 marks "Hot and Cold Migration to the Cloud" for VVF without naming HCX, so don't read that row as an HCX entitlement. The HCX page sits in the VCF 9.1.x documentation (5 Nov), though its URL path still says vcf-9-0.

## Load balancing dates

[KB 401492](https://knowledge.broadcom.com/external/article/401492/) limits the NSX load balancer from VCF 9.0 to VCF appliances and layer 4 for vSphere Supervisor. General-purpose load balancing leaves the VCF entitlement, and Broadcom recommends Avi. [KB 439411](https://knowledge.broadcom.com/external/article/439411) and FC footnote 12 let customers holding the equivalent Avi licences keep using NSX load balancing until 30 May 2027. FC footnote 12 adds that VVF customers are entitled to the Foundation Load Balancer for the use case its rows describe, and that VCF and Edge customers can use either that or the NSX load balancer.

## Using the matrix against a tool list

<Callout type="analysis" title="This site's reasoning, not Broadcom's">
A mark says you hold the right to use a capability. It says nothing about whether that capability meets the requirement a separately bought tool serves today. Take each tool with a renewal date, find its row, and record retention, integrations, reporting and who owns it next to the answer. Included components also run on your management capacity, so their footprint belongs in any comparison.

Finance can act on the first question, which is whether the row reads Included, Add-on, No or Conflict for the edition on the order. The architect answers the second, which is whether the capability does the job.
</Callout>

| Separately bought tool | Row to check | Limit to check |
|---|---|---|
| Central syslog or log analytics | Log management | Whether retention and search suit any security team using the feed |
| Flow and topology tools | Network flow analysis | VVF exports flows but has no analysis component |
| Capacity planning, showback | Cost management, predictive capacity | VVF has cost but not predictive capacity |
| Service catalogue, provisioning scripts | VCF Automation | Not on VVF or Express |
| Kubernetes distribution | VKS and Supervisor | VVF lacks ArgoCD, secret store and multi-cluster lifecycle |
| Migration tools | HCX | VVF evaluation limits, unless paired with a VCF site |
| Short-term snapshot tools | vSAN ESA snapshots | Conflict 6. Snapshots on the same platform don't replace an independent backup |
| Firewall or load balancer | Distributed and gateway firewall, Load balancing | NSX being included doesn't include either for general use |
