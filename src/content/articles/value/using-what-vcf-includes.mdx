<InShort>
Each edition includes the following, going by Broadcom's tables, TechDocs, the bill of materials and the Edge contract terms, <AsOf date="2026-11-06" />.

- **vSphere Express:** ESX, vCenter, VCF Operations for licensing only and the license server, up to 128 cores per customer, with no add-ons. Broadcom publishes no full feature list.
- **VVF:** adds VKS, log and cost management and 0.25 TiB of vSAN per core. It has no NSX networking, VCF Automation, network flow analysis, predictive capacity or compliance management, and HCX is not a component.
- **VCF Edge:** the Feature Comparison marks it the same as VCF on every row but one (vSAN witness). What separates them is contractual: Edge Locations only, 8 cores per CPU minimum, at least 10 sites in the first year, at most 256 cores per site.
- **VCF:** everything marked in the table, except that firewalling and general-purpose load balancing need add-ons.
- **Advanced Services:** none is included in any edition. Broadcom's documents disagree on whether VVF can buy vDefend and Tanzu.
</InShort>

This note covers entitlement only: your order document and the program documentation for your product decide what you hold. It isn't advice to buy, renew or leave, and it isn't legal or purchasing advice. The Broadcom documents cited here give no price for any of these (checked 6 Nov 2026), so none appears here.

## Where Broadcom's documents disagree

Get a written answer on these before relying on either reading. Each number matches a matrix cell.

1. **vDefend on VVF.** Whether VVF can buy vDefend Firewall with ATP depends on the document: FC p17 marks it as available, and the TechDocs Advanced Services page lists vDefend for VCF only.
2. **Tanzu on VVF.** The same pages split the other way. TechDocs lists Tanzu Platform and Tanzu Data Intelligence for VCF and vSphere Foundation, and FC p17-18 leaves VVF blank for both.
3. **HyperArmor.** FC p17 marks it for VVF, but the post describes it as a defense layer for the ESX component of VCF. The TechDocs Advanced Services page doesn't list it yet, so it can't break the tie.
4. **Private AI and the PAIF-N add-on.** On VCF, the documents leave open whether Private AI needs an extra licence. FC p9 and p16-17 mark Private AI for VCF with no condition, and FAQ Q15 lists Private AI Services in the VCF licence. Yet FC p10 puts footnote 10, "Requires PAIF-N add-on", on the Private AI Services heading in VCF Automation, and ADD says that from 3 Nov 2025 you "might also receive" a PAIF-N licence, based on your subscription. The Edge SPD settles it for Edge alone: the component is included only as your transaction document indicates.
5. **Data Services Manager.** Three answers are on record. FAQ Q84 says it moved into TrueSource data services on 4 Nov, with the BOM marking the Data Services VCF service as add-on. It's still its own add-on in TechDocs. And FC p10 names DSM among pre-bundled services with no add-on footnote. Under the Edge SPD, a Private AI entitlement carries DSM only for PostgreSQL with Private AI Services.
6. **Replication and RPO.** The core VCF entitlement includes vSAN remote replication and vSphere Replication RPOs "as low as one minute", FAQ Q78 (4 Nov) says. [KB 428834](https://knowledge.broadcom.com/external/article/428834), scoped to Live Recovery 9.0.x and showing no date, says remote snapshot replication and RPOs under 5 minutes need Advanced Cyber Compliance. The FAQ and FC p7 are the newer 9.1 statements, but age alone doesn't settle it: the BOM marks the Protection and Recovery VCF service as add-on and doesn't say which functions that covers.
7. **Disaster recovery.** FC p6, titled 9.1.1, marks a Disaster Recovery row for VVF, Edge and VCF. The post calls disaster recovery a new native feature in VCF and VVF, planned for the next release, which it estimates for Q1FY27. VPC policy-based connectivity has the same split (FC p9 against the post). Ask which release a mark refers to.
8. **Fleet lifecycle on VVF.** FC p13 says VVF customers use vLCM for lifecycle, while the BOM lists fleet lifecycle and SDDC lifecycle components for VVF.
9. **vSAN witness.** FC p6 marks "vSAN Witness Host/2 Node Support" for Edge only, yet its 2-Node Cluster row marks all three, and the BOM lists witness appliances for VCF and VVF. FAQ Q71 presents a locally deployed witness as what sets Edge apart. Edge does get a witness licence, limited to embedded hardware at an Edge Location, the SPD confirms.

Four smaller mismatches don't change a cell:

- **The name Live Recovery.** Check which product an order line means. VMware Live Recovery is now Protection and Recovery, according to the [Protection and Recovery 9.1 release notes](https://techdocs.broadcom.com/us/en/vmware-cis/vcf/protection-and-recovery/9-1/release-notes/protection-and-recovery-91-release-notes.html) (build date 12 May 2026), yet the 4 Nov post, FC and TechDocs all sell VMware Live Recovery as an Advanced Service. The BOM also marks Protection and Recovery Yes for VVF and Express, while FC and AS don't offer Live Recovery on VVF. EXP treats vSphere Replication as part of protection and recovery, which may explain the mark, but the BOM doesn't say.
- **External DNS on VVF.** FC p3 marks it, FC p10 doesn't, and the BOM says yes.
- **The Express core limit.** Both sources say 128 cores and scope them differently: "across all environments" in the post, and per customer in EXP, which adds that the console blocks allocation above 128 "per customer or site ID".
- **vSAN rounding on VVF.** [KB 313548](https://knowledge.broadcom.com/external/article/313548/counting-cores-for-vmware-cloud-foundati.html) rounds 0.25 TiB per core up to a whole TiB, but its version list predates 9 and ADD doesn't mention rounding, so neither page confirms the rule for 9. Despite its URL, that KB doesn't cover portability.

## vSphere Express: what is and isn't known

The full Express feature list has to come from your sales representative: EXP says Express entitles you to "certain vSphere features" and tells you to consult them for the list. Express is also missing from the Feature Comparison and the FAQ, so its column rests on three Broadcom sources. The mandatory components are ESX, vCenter, VCF Operations and the license server, and the optional ones are vSphere Replication, VMware Tools and Remote Console (EXP). The post names five capabilities but introduces them with "such as", so that list is open-ended. Protection and Recovery is also a component in the BOM, which shows dashes for everything else in the Operations and Automation stack.

Express stops at 128 cores per customer, on the default licence only with no splitting or sharing across sites, no add-ons, version 9 or later only, and VCF Operations for licensing only (EXP). The same page documents two ways up: converging an Express estate to VCF, and extending it to VVF by applying the VVF licence.

The exclusion lists come from parties with a commercial interest, so treat them as those parties' claim until your sales representative confirms it in writing. [C4C Group](https://www.c4cgroup.co.uk/guides/vsphere-express/), which sells advice on buying and renewing enterprise technology, and reseller [Software Express](https://www.software-express.de/hersteller/vmware/vsphere/express/) both list DRS, memory tiering, SR-IOV, GPU and vGPU, Proactive HA, live patching, VCF Automation and Private AI as not included. C4C attributes this to reseller briefings and notes that Broadcom's announcement lists only what is included. Instances are discrete with no central management and sales run through distributors, according to [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/virtualization/2026/11/04/vmware-returns-to-low-end-server-virtualization-with-vsphere-express/5307466), which also reports that a spokesperson declined to give a price.

## HCX on VVF

A VVF site with no valid licence runs HCX in Evaluation Mode, limited to 90 days and 20 migrations per migration type, the HCX guide says, and the BOM shows a dash for VCF Operations HCX under VVF. Such a site can get feature inheritance from a paired VCF site, and [KB 440126](https://knowledge.broadcom.com/external/article/440126/hcx-licensing-requirements-for-migrating.html) describes a source site inheriting from a licensed VCF destination. FC p6 marks "Hot and Cold Migration to the Cloud" for VVF without naming HCX, so don't read that row as an HCX entitlement. The HCX page sits in the VCF 9.1.x documentation (5 Nov), though its URL path still says vcf-9-0.

## NSX load balancing and the 30 May 2027 date

From VCF 9.0 the NSX load balancer is limited to VCF appliances and layer 4 for vSphere Supervisor. General-purpose load balancing leaves the VCF entitlement, and Broadcom recommends Avi ([KB 401492](https://knowledge.broadcom.com/external/article/401492/)). Customers holding the equivalent Avi licences can keep using NSX load balancing until 30 May 2027 ([KB 439411](https://knowledge.broadcom.com/external/article/439411) and FC footnote 12). The same footnote entitles VVF customers to the Foundation Load Balancer for the use case its rows describe, and lets VCF and Edge customers use either that or the NSX load balancer.

## Using the matrix against a tool list

<Callout type="analysis" title="What a mark leaves open">
A mark says you hold the right to use a capability. It says nothing about whether that capability meets the requirement a separately bought tool serves today. Take each tool with a renewal date, find its row, and record retention, integrations, reporting and who owns it next to the answer. Included components also run on your management capacity, so their footprint belongs in any comparison.

Two questions follow, with different owners. Finance can act on whether the row reads Included, Add-on, No or Conflict for the edition on the order. The architect answers whether the capability does the job.
</Callout>

| Separately bought tool | Row to check | Limit to check |
|---|---|---|
| Central syslog or log analytics | Log management | Whether retention and search suit any security team using the feed |
| Flow and topology tools | Network flow analysis | VVF exports flows but has no analysis component |
| Capacity planning, showback | Cost management, predictive capacity | VVF has cost but not predictive capacity |
| Service catalogue, provisioning scripts | VCF Automation | Not on VVF or Express |
| Kubernetes distribution | VKS and Supervisor | VVF lacks ArgoCD, secret store and multi-cluster lifecycle |
| Migration tools | HCX | VVF evaluation limits, unless paired with a VCF site |
| Short-term snapshot tools | vSAN ESA snapshots | Conflict 6. Snapshots on the same platform don't replace an independent backup |
| Firewall or load balancer | Distributed and gateway firewall, Load balancing | NSX being included doesn't include either for general use |

For the capacity arithmetic behind the vSAN row, see [the vSAN entitlement](/vcf/library/value/vsan-entitlement/). For core minimums, see [core counting and CPU choice](/vcf/library/value/core-counting-and-cpu-choice/).
