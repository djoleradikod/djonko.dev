<InShort>
VCF Operations finds four kinds of capacity that nobody is using: **powered-off VMs**, **snapshots**, **orphaned disks** and **idle VMs**. By default a VM is idle when its CPU stays below 100 MHz for the whole of each 24-hour period, for a number of consecutive days you set (<KB id={445643} />). Powered-off VMs, snapshots and orphaned disks hold storage. Idle VMs also hold memory and CPU allocation. Reclaim the low-risk categories first, give every candidate an owner and a waiting period, and record the capacity returned. The same feature existed in Aria Operations in 5.x. In 9.x it is in every estate because VCF Operations is mandatory.
</InShort>

## The four categories

| Category | Holds | Risk of reclaiming | Typical action |
|---|---|---|---|
| Snapshots | vSAN or datastore capacity | Low, once confirmed unused | Consolidate after owner check |
| Orphaned disks | Capacity, no VM attached | Low to medium | Archive, then delete |
| Powered-off VMs | Capacity | Medium | Confirm owner, archive, delete |
| Idle VMs | Memory, CPU allocation, capacity | Highest | Owner review, power off, wait, then treat as powered-off |

Idle VMs are last because some of them are idle on purpose. Standby nodes, disaster recovery targets and month-end batch servers can show no CPU use for weeks. The KB advises reclaiming powered-off VMs and snapshots before idle VMs for this reason.

## Settings that decide what is flagged

Idle VM settings live under **Administration > Global Settings > Resource Reclamation**:

- The CPU threshold (default 100 MHz) and the share of time below it (default 100%).
- The **idle period**: consecutive days that meet the criteria before a VM is reclaimable.
- The **exclusion period**: days after provisioning before a new VM is considered, so VMs being built are not flagged.

Orphaned disk data is collected daily, by default at 20:00, and cost is calculated at 21:00. Keep the collection before the cost calculation if you change the schedule, or the savings figures will be a day behind.

## Process

1. Review the **Capacity > Reclaim** view weekly with the people who can confirm ownership. A weekly cycle fits change windows and gives time to find owners.
2. Snapshots and orphaned disks: consolidate or archive after a quick owner check. These are candidates for scheduled actions through Automation Central once the team trusts the process.
3. Powered-off VMs: tag with the owner and a removal date, keep them for an agreed period, then archive or delete.
4. Idle VMs: ask the owner first. If nobody answers, power the VM off with a dated note and treat it as powered-off from that point. Someone who still needs it will call before the removal date.
5. Record the capacity returned. VCF Operations reports realised savings per category, which gives you the figure for the [cost case](/vcf/library/value/building-the-cost-case/).

<Callout type="warn">
Do not reclaim the vSphere Cluster Services (vCLS) VMs. They support DRS and HA and will show as idle. Exclude them, and any other system VMs, from the reclaim views.
</Callout>

## Why this matters for licence cost

Reclaimed storage reduces the raw capacity you need to install at the next expansion, which matters because [vSAN entitlement](/vcf/library/value/vsan-entitlement/) is counted on raw capacity. Reclaimed memory from idle VMs lowers the host count in memory-bound clusters. Neither changes the current invoice. Both change the next purchase, which is why the reclaimed figure must be recorded and used when the next growth request arrives.

## Pitfalls

- **Old snapshots as backups.** A snapshot kept in case it is needed one day is a sign the backup process is not trusted. Fix the backup, then remove the snapshot.
- **Deleting orphaned disks that are not orphaned.** Disks used by appliances, replication targets or detached for a migration can look orphaned. Archive before deleting.
- **No waiting period.** Deleting a powered-off VM the day it is found removes the safety net. An agreed retention period costs little capacity and avoids restores.
