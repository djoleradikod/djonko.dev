<InShort>
**Private AI Services is catalogued for VCF and VCF Edge and not for VVF, but plan on needing a licence line to switch it on.** It sits behind the "Private AI Foundation with NVIDIA" add-on licence even where that product comes with VCF in your region, according to the [9.1 requirements page](https://techdocs.broadcom.com/us/en/vmware-cis/private-ai/foundation-with-nvidia/9-1/deploying-private-ai-foundation-with-nvidia/requirements-for-deploying-private-ai-foundation-with-nvidia.html). So look for that line on your site ID in the VCF Business Services console before anyone prices this. vGPU adds NVIDIA licences per GPU, and passthrough doesn't. Pursue it for LLM inference and RAG on data that can't leave your estate. Private AI Services can't run vision, speech or time-series models. That's the position <AsOf date="2026-11-07" />. This isn't legal or purchasing advice: your order document decides what you hold.
</InShort>

## "Included" and "add-on": the rule to work to

The August 2025 message was broader: the services, "previously sold separately", would come "without an additional purchase", [Broadcom's blog](https://blogs.vmware.com/cloud-foundation/2025/08/26/vmware-private-ai-foundation-with-nvidia-explore25/) said, and [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2025/08/26/vmware_explore_vcf_evolution/) reported "at no extra cost" the same day. TechDocs is narrower: from 3 November 2025, "based on your VCF subscription, you might also [receive a license](https://techdocs.broadcom.com/us/en/vmware-cis/vcf/vcf-9-0-and-later/9-1/licensing/licensing-overview/add-on-licenses-in-vcf-9-0.html)" (5 Nov 2026). Even if the product is "available as part of VCF for your geographical region", you still need the licence, the requirements page adds.

<Callout type="analysis" title="Working assumption">
These statements fit together if the add-on is a licence line that some subscriptions receive at no separate charge, though no Broadcom page says that outright. So treat the requirements page and footnote 10 as the operating rule, and the line's presence on your site ID as the test. Its name in the [private license guide](https://vcf.broadcom.com/vcf/clm/assets/VCF-9.1-Licensing-Private-License-Files.pdf) (August 2026) is "VMware Private AI Foundation with NVIDIA (cores)".
</Callout>

Four questions stay open on Broadcom's pages (checked 7 Nov 2026), so get the answers in writing:

- which subscriptions or regions receive the line
- the add-on's price if yours doesn't
- whether a CPU-only deployment needs it: the requirements list has no exception, yet the [assignment page](https://techdocs.broadcom.com/us/en/vmware-cis/private-ai/foundation-with-nvidia/9-1/deploying-private-ai-foundation-with-nvidia/assign-the-private-ai-foundation-license-to-the-management-domain.html) allocates capacity "only to the GPU-enabled workload domains"
- whether the unfootnoted catalogue rows work without the line

NVIDIA's guide gives the metric and no dollar figure, so it sizes the count and not the cost. Constructed example: three hosts with two GPUs each need six NVIDIA licences on vGPU, and none on passthrough without NIMs.

This site reads the November 2026 pages as using PAIS for the software in VCF and PAIF-N for the licence line and the documentation set. Older sources draw the line elsewhere, so when a quote says "PAIF", ask which it means.

## Prerequisites that shape the design

A GPU deployment needs at least three GPU-enabled hosts in the workload domain's initial cluster (requirements page). CPU-only needs no GPUs, and the [blueprint](https://techdocs.broadcom.com/us/en/vmware-cis/vcf/vcf-9-0-and-later/9-1/design/design-blueprints-for/application-modernization/private-ai-services-blueprint(1).html) offers it for embedding models.

The enablement path then sets the networking. Going through VCF Automation means "NSX VPC networking model only" and an All Apps organization, while the vCenter path takes VDS or NSX VPC with no VCF Automation dependency (design library). [VLAN-backed VPC](/vcf/library/architecture/vpc-connectivity-choice/) is the open case: neither the design library nor the blueprint rules it out in so many words, but the blueprint names only the Centralized and Distributed VLAN connectivity models, so get Broadcom's confirmation before designing on it.

For capacity, the design library estimates 2-5 TB of Harbor for 10-20 LLMs, about twice GPU memory as system RAM, and two or more replicas per endpoint. PostgreSQL with pgvector is needed unless you run the model-endpoints-only profile.

For the build, read William Lam's [10 September 2026 post](https://williamlam.com/2026/09/vcf-9-1-1-deploying-your-own-ai-models-with-vcf-private-ai-services-pais-3-0.html) on PAIS 3.0 in 9.1.1, a passthrough lab run that doesn't cover licensing. AI Infrastructure Quickstart is only [planned for the next release](https://blogs.vmware.com/cloud-foundation/2026/11/04/broadcom-vcf-portfolio-updates/), so keep it out of a design.

## Is it worth doing

The one hard limit Broadcom documents is that the service "cannot run" computer vision, time-series, optimization or audio and speech models. The rest is positioning. The Register reports Broadcom's argument that renting AI brings bigger bills. VMware highlights contact centres for ROI, [Forrester's Maisto](https://www.forrester.com/blogs/vmware-explore-paris-2025-our-take-on-private-ai-and-private-cloud-sovereignty/) reports (20 Oct 2025), and Maisto says a subscription fits poorly with sovereignty strategies. Neither gives figures for your estate.

<Callout type="analysis" title="Where it fits">

| Your position | Do this |
|---|---|
| LLM inference or RAG on data that can't leave, on VCF with a Supervisor on VDS or NSX VPC, or an All Apps org on NSX VPC if you enable through VCF Automation | Check the console, then pilot |
| Same, with no GPUs yet | Trial embedding models on the blueprint's CPU-only option once the licence question is answered. Size GPU hosts before serving LLMs |
| Vision, speech or time-series | Stop. These services won't run them |
| VVF | Stop. The catalogue doesn't mark it |
| Data may go to a public model | Leave it. Remote models need no GPUs, but your data leaves, which removes the reason to run this |

A written Broadcom statement on who receives the line, or a published add-on price, would change the first step.
</Callout>
