<InShort>
**The NSX Load Balancer is still in VCF 9, on narrower terms.** Broadcom keeps it entitled for VCF appliances and for Layer 4 on vSphere Supervisor. General-purpose use left the VCF entitlement at 9.0, and it may continue only until 30 May 2027 and only with Avi licences (<KB id={439411} />, Feature Comparison footnote 12, <AsOf date="2026-11-06" />). The network team's largest job before the upgrade is sorting every virtual server into those two groups. Several things blamed on 9 went earlier: the NSX 4.0.0.1 notes already remove host N-VDS and announce the end of KVM and third-party OpenStack support.
</InShort>

<Callout type="note" title="Corrected on 6 November 2026">
This note used to say the NSX load balancer is gone and that Avi replaces it. The position is the entitlement split above. It also gave NSX 4.1.0.2 as the import minimum, which came from a community guide: Broadcom's import pages say 4.2 for VCF 9.0 and 4.2.1 for 9.1.
</Callout>

## The load balancer position

Broadcom's 9.1 What's New and Product Support pages say nothing about load balancer entitlement. The position sits in two KBs, the 9.0 support notes and one footnote.

| Use today | Position | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| VCF appliances | Entitled. Footnote 12 lets VCF and VCF Edge use the NSX or the Foundation Load Balancer. | Keep. |
| Supervisor L4 (VM Service, vSphere Pods, VKS, Supervisor Services) | Entitled (<KB id={401492} />). A [July 2026 VMware blog on 9.0](https://blogs.vmware.com/cloud-foundation/2026/07/13/choosing-the-right-load-balancer-for-vsphere-supervisor-in-vsphere-9-0-and-vmware-cloud-foundation-9-0/) puts Foundation LB on VDS networks and, in its comparison table, NSX LB on NSX segments or VPCs. Its network-model section names only Avi for VPCs. | Keep, unless the Supervisor needs L7, WAF or GSLB. |
| Workload VIPs, L4 or L7 | Outside the entitlement from 9.0. Bridge to 30 May 2027 with Avi licences. | Buy Avi and migrate. |
| Same VIPs, still on NSX 4.x entitlements | "Not impacted" until end of life of that release (<KB id={401492} />). | Plan the migration around the upgrade date. |

**Sizing the bridge.** <KB id={439411} /> sets 100 qualified VCF cores to 1 Avi Service Unit, counting only cores tied to NSX Manager instances with the load balancer enabled, co-termed with the VCF licence. So the cost follows the NSX instance, whatever the number of VIPs on it.

Constructed example, with assumed numbers: NSX A has the load balancer on and 1,280 cores, NSX B has it off and 3,000 cores. That gives 1,280 / 100 = 12.8 SU, and NSX B adds nothing. The KB doesn't state how fractions round.

<Callout type="analysis" title="What this site would do">
Sort by upgrade date. If the upgrade lands well before 30 May 2027 (205 days after this note), the bridge buys migration time on 9. If it lands after, no bridge time remains, so migrate on 4.x first. An estate with only appliance and Supervisor L4 use needs no Avi purchase for this reason. Consolidating workload VIPs onto one NSX reduces the cores counted, if the KB's counting rule holds for that layout. Confirm it with Broadcom before relying on it. Licensing here isn't purchasing advice: read the KB.
</Callout>

**Two design rules for Avi.** The [9.1 Avi design page](https://techdocs.broadcom.com/us/en/vmware-cis/vcf/vcf-9-0-and-later/9-1/design/design-library/vcf-load-balancing-detailed-design/avi-load-balancer-detailed-design.html) says Avi must be deployed before Supervisor activation, and that a Supervisor which detects no Avi defaults to the NSX load balancer. It also requires a 1:1 relationship between Avi and NSX Manager, so each NSX instance adds a controller cluster. Broadcom's conversion tool, vACT, is named in <KB id={439411} />. This note hasn't tested it.

**Ask Broadcom, because no source opened answers these:**

- Whether the L4 load balancer on the 9.1 Virtual Network Appliance counts as the NSX Load Balancer for entitlement.
- Whether cores count when the load balancer is enabled only for appliances or Supervisor.
- How a DLB outside Supervisor is treated. <KB id={401492} /> lists only the two approved uses.
- What happens after 30 May 2027. The sources give the date and no technical behaviour.
- Whether an estate that reaches 9 later still qualifies for the bridge. <KB id={439411} /> words it for customers "currently utilizing" the load balancer in VCF 9.0/9.1.

<Callout type="analysis" title="Choosing">
This is this site's reading. Keep centralized if edges already exist and you use gateway services on them, such as IPSec VPN on a centralized transit gateway. Take distributed VLAN for a new design that prefers no edges and can extend an external VLAN across the VPC span. Choosing distributed is less of a trap in 9.1 than it was in 9.0, because stateful services now have a home on the VNA.
</Callout>

## What 9 changed, and what was already gone

| Item | When | Action for an estate on 4.x |
|---|---|---|
| Multi-NSX on one vCenter | Not supported from 9.0 (<KB id={417789} />) | Exit first. |
| Manager APIs and Advanced UIs | Removed at 9.0 | Promote to Policy. Replace IPSET, Logical Switch and Logical Port group members. |
| Overlay agents on physical servers | Not supported from 9.0 | Edge bridging. |
| Standalone NSX install or upgrade | Ended at 9.0, use the VCF BOM | Plan NSX inside the VCF upgrade. |
| NSX Migration Coordinator | Removed at 9.0 | NSX for vSphere goes to 4.x first. |
| Default DLB (path "ALL") | Not supported from 9.1 (<KB id={440901} />) | Attach to a group. |
| Enhanced Datapath Standard as default switch mode | Default at 9.0, with "Standard" announced for removal | Check NIC and driver fit in the design. |
| VPCs and transit gateways | NSX VPCs arrived in [4.1.1](https://techdocs.broadcom.com/us/en/vmware-cis/nsx/vmware-nsx/4-1/release-notes/vmware-nsx-411-release-notes.html). VCF 9 adds a [guided edge wizard in vCenter and NSX Manager](https://blogs.vmware.com/cloud-foundation/2025/06/25/vpc-centralized-network-connectivity-with-guided-edge-deployment/). 9.1 adds the VNA and VPC span set from vCenter. | Decide who owns VPC work. |
| Single NSX Manager | [Simple model](https://techdocs.broadcom.com/us/en/vmware-cis/vcf/vcf-9-0-and-later/9-0/design/design-library/nsx-management-and-control-plane-detailed-design/simple-nsx-manager-model.html) offered, with slower recovery after a node failure | Choose per domain. |
| Host N-VDS | Removed at [4.0.0.1](https://techdocs.broadcom.com/us/en/vmware-cis/nsx/vmware-nsx/4-0/release-notes/vmware-nsx-4001-release-notes.html). Stays on Edge nodes. | An NSX 4.x estate should already be clear. |
| KVM and third-party OpenStack | The 4.0.0.1 notes say NSX "will no longer support" them. VIO support remained then. | Check the interoperability matrix for VIO. |

**Importing.** The [9.1 import guide](https://techdocs.broadcom.com/us/en/vmware-cis/vcf/vcf-9-0-and-later/9-1/building-your-private-cloud-infrastructure/working-with-workload-domains/import-an-existing-vcenter-to-create-a-workload-domain.html) lists NSX 4.2.1 as the minimum, and the [9.0 guide](https://techdocs.broadcom.com/us/en/vmware-cis/vcf/vcf-9-0-and-later/9-0/building-your-private-cloud-infrastructure/working-with-workload-domains/import-an-existing-vcenter-to-create-a-workload-domain.html) says 4.2 or later. A vCenter with no NSX gets a new manager cluster and every eligible cluster prepared. A vCenter already registered with NSX keeps its unprepared clusters as they are.

## Where the sources disagree

- **KVM and OpenStack timing.** [StarWind's blog](https://www.starwindsoftware.com/blog/vcf9-deprecated-vsphere-nsx/) lists them among VCF 9 removals and says "NSX 9.0 no longer supports" them, though it also dates the shift to NSX 4.0. The [community guide](https://vcf9-deployment-guide.pages.dev/requirements/nsx) lists them as not migrateable to NSX 9. Broadcom's 4.0.0.1 notes announce the end without naming the release it takes effect in, and the 9.0 NSX support notes don't mention them.
- **"Deprecated".** The community guide calls the native load balancer deprecated, and <KB id={395937} /> says VMware "intends to deprecate" it, with no support intended "beyond the last NSX 4.x release". That KB doesn't name 9. The 9.1 design pages still list it for centralized connectivity and as the Supervisor default. Plan on the entitlement terms.
- **Supervisor on centralized only.** A [June 2025 VMware blog](https://blogs.vmware.com/cloud-foundation/2025/06/25/vpc-centralized-network-connectivity-with-guided-edge-deployment/) says VCF Automation All Apps and Supervisor require centralized Active/Standby. [What's New in 9.1](https://techdocs.broadcom.com/us/en/vmware-cis/vcf/vcf-9-0-and-later/9-1/release-notes/vmware-cloud-foundation-9-1-0-0-release-notes/what-s-new/whats-new-nsx.html) adds distributed VLAN support for both through the VNA, so that rule holds for 9.0 only.
- **Distributed NAT.** The [matching 2025 blog](https://blogs.vmware.com/cloud-foundation/2025/06/25/vpc-distributed-network-connectivity-no-nsx-edge-vms/) limits distributed to stateless 1:1 NAT with no DHCP relay. 9.1 added 1:N SNAT and 9.1.1 added relay on public subnets.
