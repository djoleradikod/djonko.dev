<InShort>
VCF Operations, the renamed and expanded Aria Operations, is [a required component of every fleet](https://www.vmware.com/docs/vmware-cloud-foundation-9-1-general-faqs) in 9.x and [the central console](https://techdocs.broadcom.com/us/en/vmware-cis/vcf/vcf-9-0-and-later/9-1/design/vmware-cloud-foundation-concepts/vcf-operations.html) for monitoring, licensing and lifecycle, so its availability is a design decision. Run production on the three-node HA model [Broadcom's single-site blueprint](https://techdocs.broadcom.com/us/en/vmware-cis/vcf/vcf-9-0-and-later/9-1/design/design-blueprints-for/infrastructure-modernization/vcf-fleet-in-a-single-site-blueprint/management-domain-design-selections%281%29/operations-layer-fleet-single-site/vcf-fleet-in-a-single-site-high-availability-vcf-operations-model.html) selects, with a second cloud proxy in a collector group. Choose the proxy size for your forecast at the start, because changing it later means a new collector group (<KB id={457248} />). In an outage, licensed workloads keep running, while an estate still in evaluation has a 90-day clock (<KB id={443240} />).
</InShort>

<Callout type="note" title="Corrected on 7 November 2026">
This note used to say that a 5.2 upgrade deploys VCF Operations if it is missing, that log ingestion continues and users can still sign in during an outage, that workloads are simply unaffected, and that 9.1 sizing comes from the 9.0 guide. The 9.1 guide has you deploy VCF Operations yourself if there's no Aria Operations 8.18.x, no Broadcom page this site opened states either outage claim, evaluation estates are exposed, and 9.1 sizing now points to Configuration Maximums.
</Callout>

## Pick the availability model first

[Broadcom's 9.1 design pages](https://techdocs.broadcom.com/us/en/vmware-cis/vcf/vcf-9-0-and-later/9-1/design/vmware-cloud-foundation-concepts/vcf-operations.html) describe three models, and the blueprints show which they would pick:

| | Simple | High availability | Continuous availability |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nodes | One | Primary, replica and data | Node pairs across two availability zones, with a witness in a third |
| After a failure | vSphere HA restarts the node. Recovery is slower, with possible interruption to monitoring, alerting and fleet management | Rapid recovery from a single-node failure | No service interruption if one zone fails |
| What it costs | Smallest footprint | [At least four ESX hosts in the management cluster](https://techdocs.broadcom.com/us/en/vmware-cis/vcf/vcf-9-0-and-later/9-1/design/design-library/vcf-operations-design/vcf-operations-ha-model.html), so the anti-affinity rule still allows maintenance mode | [A second VCF instance, manual steps across vSphere and VCF Operations](https://techdocs.broadcom.com/us/en/vmware-cis/vcf/vcf-9-0-and-later/9-1/design/design-library/vcf-operations-design/continuous-availability-vcf-operations-model.html), and at most 10 ms between zones |
| Blueprint that selects it | [Minimal footprint](https://techdocs.broadcom.com/us/en/vmware-cis/vcf/vcf-9-0-and-later/9-1/design/design-blueprints-for/infrastructure-modernization/vcf-fleet-in-a-single-site-with-minimal-footprint-blueprint.html) | [Single site](https://techdocs.broadcom.com/us/en/vmware-cis/vcf/vcf-9-0-and-later/9-1/design/design-blueprints-for/infrastructure-modernization/vcf-fleet-in-a-single-site-blueprint/management-domain-design-selections%281%29/operations-layer-fleet-single-site/vcf-fleet-in-a-single-site-high-availability-vcf-operations-model.html) and [multiple sites in a single region](https://techdocs.broadcom.com/us/en/vmware-cis/vcf/vcf-9-0-and-later/9-1/design/design-blueprints-for/infrastructure-modernization/vcf-fleet-with-multiple-sites-in-a-single-region-blueprint.html) | None of these three |

Choose HA unless a small footprint matters more to you than the interruption Simple accepts. Move to continuous availability only if the console must survive losing a location. The single-region blueprint selects HA and doesn't describe how VCF Operations recovers from a site failure, so if you follow it, write that recovery into your design.

Deploy the load balancer with HA. The HA attributes call it optional, yet [the same page recommends an external load balancer](https://techdocs.broadcom.com/us/en/vmware-cis/vcf/vcf-9-0-and-later/9-1/design/design-library/vcf-operations-design/vcf-operations-ha-model.html) to spread connections across the nodes. It [needs an extra IP and FQDN](https://techdocs.broadcom.com/us/en/vmware-cis/vcf/vcf-9-0-and-later/9-1/design/vmware-cloud-foundation-concepts/vcf-operations.html), and that FQDN must be in the certificate SAN.

HA on these nodes doesn't protect lifecycle. In 9.1, fleet management runs [within VCF management services and takes its protection from that configuration](https://techdocs.broadcom.com/us/en/vmware-cis/vcf/vcf-9-0-and-later/9-1/design/design-library/vcf-operations-design/vcf-operations-ha-model.html), so that model is a second decision, covered in [the failure-impact matrix](/vcf/library/architecture/failure-impact-matrix/).

The VCF Installer UI applies one choice of Simple or HA to VCF Operations, VCF Automation and NSX Manager together. William Lam's [October 2025 post](https://williamlam.com/2025/10/flexible-combinations-with-vcf-fleet-deployment-models.html) shows the JSON route to the eight possible mixes, and he confirmed in a September 2026 comment that it holds for 9.1.1.

The recovery path the HA model page documents is [a restore from backup](https://techdocs.broadcom.com/us/en/vmware-cis/vcf/vcf-9-0-and-later/9-1/design/design-library/vcf-operations-design/vcf-operations-ha-model.html). Those backups are [image-based, must cover all cluster nodes at the same time, and are best taken with the cluster offline](https://techdocs.broadcom.com/us/en/vmware-cis/vcf/vcf-9-0-and-later/9-1/design/design-blueprints-for/security-modernization/vcf-backup-and-restore.html). Keep them away from dynamic threshold calculations, which run at 2 a.m. by default.

## Size from the 9.1 sources

<KB id={397782} /> covers 9.0 only. Broadcom's sizing index (<KB id={324340} />) sends 9.1.x to VMware Configuration Maximums, so check your forecast object count there. The [installer's default profiles](https://techdocs.broadcom.com/us/en/vmware-cis/vcf/vcf-9-0-and-later/9-1/design/vmware-cloud-foundation-concepts/vcf-fleet-sizing-models-9-x.html) for a first instance are a starting point, and JSON allows other sizes:

| Profile | VCF Operations | Cloud proxy |
|---|---|---|
| Simple | 1x Small | 1x Small |
| HA - Small | 2x Small | 1x Small |
| HA - Medium | 3x Medium | 1x Standard |
| HA - Large | 3x Large | 1x Standard |

The HA - Small row conflicts with the HA model pages, which require [a three-node cluster](https://techdocs.broadcom.com/us/en/vmware-cis/vcf/vcf-9-0-and-later/9-1/design/design-library/vcf-operations-design/vcf-operations-ha-model.html). Budget for three until Broadcom resolves it. Lifecycle capacity isn't part of this choice in 9.1, because the [services runtime size sets the size of the other management services](https://techdocs.broadcom.com/us/en/vmware-cis/vcf/vcf-9-0-and-later/9-1/deployment/upgrading-cloud-foundation/deploy-vcf-management-services.html).

## Cloud proxies are part of the design

A cloud proxy is [mandatory and goes in the management domain of each VCF instance](https://techdocs.broadcom.com/us/en/vmware-cis/vcf/vcf-9-0-and-later/9-1/design/design-library/vcf-operations-design.html). In 9.1 it's [required before management services can be deployed](https://techdocs.broadcom.com/us/en/vmware-cis/vcf/vcf-9-0-and-later/9-1/deployment/upgrading-cloud-foundation/upgrade-backup-and-restore/deploy-vcf-operations.html), and the [first instance must have one before its upgrade](https://techdocs.broadcom.com/us/en/vmware-cis/vcf/vcf-9-0-and-later/9-1/deployment/upgrading-cloud-foundation/upgrade-backup-and-restore.html). Put it on the management network, give it DNS records, and include it in backups. Deploy [the Unified configuration](https://techdocs.broadcom.com/us/en/vmware-cis/vcf/vcf-9-0-and-later/9-1/deployment/upgrading-cloud-foundation/upgrade-backup-and-restore/configuring-cloud-proxies-in-vrealize-operations-cloud.html): Small covers up to 16,000 VMs with 4 vCPUs and 16 GB, Standard covers 16,000 to 80,000 with 8 vCPUs and 48 GB, and Classic is only for when support asks for it.

<Callout type="warn" title="Broadcom's scale-up advice conflicts with its own KB">
The [design requirement](https://techdocs.broadcom.com/us/en/vmware-cis/vcf/vcf-9-0-and-later/9-1/design/design-library/vcf-operations-design/vcf-operations-ha-model.html) says you may need to scale proxies up to a greater size. <KB id={457248} /> says resizing an active proxy is deprecated and mixed sizes in a collector group are unsupported: add same-size nodes, or build a new group and move the adapters.
</Callout>

Follow the KB, since it's the narrower and more specific statement. In practice that makes the first size a long-lived choice, so pick it from the VM forecast. That reading is this site's analysis.

One proxy is a single point of failure until you act on the HA model page's recommendations, all manual and all after deployment: [a collector group per instance, a second collector, a DRS anti-affinity rule, and the default VCF adapter pointed at the group](https://techdocs.broadcom.com/us/en/vmware-cis/vcf/vcf-9-0-and-later/9-1/design/design-library/vcf-operations-design/vcf-operations-ha-model.html). Expect a gap even then, because the log deployment page says [failover takes a few minutes and logs may be lost](https://techdocs.broadcom.com/us/en/vmware-cis/vcf/vcf-9-0-and-later/9-1/design/design-library/vcf-operations-design/vcf-operations-for-logs-deployment-models.html), and the cloud proxy FAQ says [collection failover starts after about 10 minutes](https://techdocs.broadcom.com/us/en/vmware-cis/vcf/vcf-9-0-and-later/9-1/fleet-management/collecting-data-with-cloud-proxies-in-vrealize-operations-cloud/cloud-proxy-faq.html).

Three faults are worth planning around:

- A secondary instance newer than VCF Operations gets a proxy that fails to register, since [the proxy needs an exact version match while the installer checks major.minor only](https://techdocs.broadcom.com/us/en/vmware-cis/vcf/vcf-9-0-and-later/9-1/release-notes/vmware-cloud-foundation-9-1-1-0-release-notes/vcfoperations-9-1-1-0-release-notes.html) (9.1.1). Upgrade VCF Operations before adding that instance.
- HAProxy on the proxy needs every node's FQDN to resolve, and DNS failures, certificate mismatches or moving VMs between segments can leave it Offline. If that's the management domain's proxy, the SSO page in VCF Operations fails to load (<KB id={437258} />), which can look like an identity fault. Firewall rules should [allow 443 and restrict by FQDN](https://techdocs.broadcom.com/us/en/vmware-cis/vcf/vcf-9-0-and-later/9-1/deployment/upgrading-cloud-foundation/upgrade-backup-and-restore/configuring-cloud-proxies-in-vrealize-operations-cloud.html).
- On 9.0.x, importing an existing vCenter and NSX as a workload domain can stop proxy collection and fail the VCF adapter. The cause there is a communication timeout with SDDC Manager, plus trust settings or UI state flags, and restarting the collector doesn't fix it (<KB id={443680} />).

## What happens when it is down

No Broadcom page this site opened gives an outage table for VCF Operations <AsOf date="2026-11-07" />, so keep documented and inferred behaviour apart in the runbook, and write it before go-live. Licensing is the documented part, on 9.1 (<KB id={443240} />), and this is technical behaviour, so check entitlement questions with Broadcom:

- **Fully licensed:** existing entitlements stay active and workloads keep running, but new assignments and capacity changes are blocked until connectivity returns.
- **Evaluation or expiring:** automatic sync fails. If the 90-day evaluation ends before connectivity returns, ESX hosts go Disconnected and VMs can't power on, though [existing workloads aren't proactively stopped](https://techdocs.broadcom.com/us/en/vmware-cis/vcf/vcf-9-0-and-later/9-1/licensing/licensing-overview/licensing-model.html). [The expired estate note](/vcf/library/licensing/expired-estate-recovery/) covers recovery and where Broadcom's pages differ.

The same KB recommends monitoring the VCF Operations appliance and the License Server VM, and alerting on the vCenter license server banner, so set those up from day one.

The rest is inference from where components sit, and [the failure-impact matrix](/vcf/library/architecture/failure-impact-matrix/) treats it the same way, so test it on non-production first. Expect no lifecycle console, no fleet password, certificate, SSO or DNS and NTP tasks, and no alerts or dashboards. Existing sign-on should continue because the broker runs separately. Broadcom's log design doesn't say what happens to ingestion or search in this outage, so make no promise to the log owners until you've tested it. Proxy failover starts [once VCF Operations detects the failure](https://techdocs.broadcom.com/us/en/vmware-cis/vcf/vcf-9-0-and-later/9-1/design/design-library/vcf-operations-design/vcf-operations-for-logs-deployment-models.html), so a proxy that fails during the outage may not fail over, though Broadcom doesn't state that.

The upgrade doesn't create VCF Operations for you on 9.1. With no Aria Operations 8.18.x, [you must deploy it before the upgrade can proceed](https://techdocs.broadcom.com/us/en/vmware-cis/vcf/vcf-9-0-and-later/9-1/deployment/upgrading-cloud-foundation/upgrade-backup-and-restore/deploy-vcf-operations.html), from an OVA in the vSphere Client on the management domain vCenter, and it starts in evaluation mode, so assign the entitlement before adding other components. Jonathan McDonald's [December 2025 FAQ](https://blogs.vmware.com/cloud-foundation/2025/12/18/upgrading-vmware-cloud-foundation-5-2-to-9-0-the-top-10-questions-answered/) on 5.2 to 9.0 conflicts with itself here: one answer says VCF Operations and fleet management "will be deployed as they are mandatory", and a later one says to download and deploy them yourself. Plan from the 9.1 guide.

The licensing clock may also apply during the upgrade itself. The components [move to evaluation mode during a 5.x upgrade](https://www.vmware.com/docs/vmware-cloud-foundation-9-1-general-faqs), although that FAQ answer is worded for 9.0, so treat a console outage in that window as the evaluation case above. Before management services go in, reserve at least 12 IP addresses on the management network (<KB id={440630} />, which also sets the upgrade sequence), and prepare their DNS records and certificates in advance. An SSL-terminating proxy server on VCF Operations [must be removed before they deploy](https://techdocs.broadcom.com/us/en/vmware-cis/vcf/vcf-9-0-and-later/9-1/deployment/upgrading-cloud-foundation/deploy-vcf-management-services.html).

## Where things live in the console

KBs and documentation refer to these paths, so they're worth knowing by name:

| Path | What's there |
|---|---|
