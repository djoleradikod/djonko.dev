<InShort>
**Default to vSAN ESA on the compute hosts**, in any domain whose hosts qualify. Choose a **storage cluster** when capacity, rather than compute, is what forces new hosts in: at the list prices in Signal65's Broadcom-sponsored model, a core costs nearly twice a TiB of add-on, so buying hosts for their entitlement loses money. Choose an **array** when you own one with life left, can't buy ESA hosts, or get a quote under the price worked out below. The management domain's default cluster can't use a storage cluster at all. vVols are out of every new design. Every cost figure below comes from someone with a stake, and the newest study prices no storage-only hosts, so get a quote at usable capacity and run the example with it.
</InShort>

<Callout type="analysis" title="Whose reasoning this is">
Broadcom documents the matrix and limits. The question order, defaults and example are this site's.
</Callout>

## What is supported where

The management default cluster is the only column where a vSAN storage or compute cluster is not supported. Principal storage is what a domain or cluster is created on. Supplemental storage is added afterwards, and can run workloads or hold templates, backups and ISOs. Support by cluster type, from Broadcom's [Storage Models page](https://techdocs.broadcom.com/us/en/vmware-cis/vcf/vcf-9-0-and-later/9-1/design/vmware-cloud-foundation-concepts/storage-models.html) <AsOf date="2026-11-05" />:

| Storage | Management default cluster | Management additional clusters | Workload domain |
|---|---|---|---|
| vSAN ESA or OSA, HCI | Principal | Principal | Principal |
| vSAN storage cluster or compute cluster | **Not supported** | Principal | Principal |
| VMFS on Fibre Channel, NFSv3 | Principal, supplemental | Principal, supplemental | Principal, supplemental |
| NFSv4.1 | Principal\*, supplemental | Principal\*, supplemental | Principal\*, supplemental |
| iSCSI, NVMe/TCP, NVMe/FC, NVMe/RDMA | Principal\*, supplemental | Supplemental | Principal\*, supplemental |

\* Only by converging or importing an environment you built by hand (<KB id={416270} />). Greenfield workflows don't offer these.

Broadcom contradicts itself on NFSv4.1. The [NFS model page](https://techdocs.broadcom.com/us/en/vmware-cis/vcf/vcf-9-0-and-later/9-1/design/design-library/storage-models-9-x/nfs-storage.html) says NFSv4.1 "is only supported as a supplemental storage option", against the matrix and the KB, so use NFSv3 for principal storage unless Broadcom confirms otherwise in writing.

## The questions, in order

1. **Is this the management domain's default cluster?** Storage and compute clusters are then out, leaving HCI, Fibre Channel or NFSv3 on a new build. Choose as if it were permanent: SDDC Manager changes principal storage like for like, NFSv3 to NFSv3 or VMFS FC to VMFS FC, with vVol to vSAN the only cross-type route, and FC "must be selected during the initial deployment" (<KB id={435850} />). vSAN is recommended but not required ([FAQ Q33](https://www.vmware.com/docs/vmware-cloud-foundation-9-1-general-faqs)). It needs 3 hosts for a simple install and 4 for high availability. FC and NFS allow 2 for a simple install, but Broadcom [recommends starting at 4](https://techdocs.broadcom.com/us/en/vmware-cis/vcf/vcf-9-0-and-later/9-1/design/design-library/storage-models-9-x/fibre-channel-storage.html). At four hosts the count stops separating the options.

2. **Does the design lean on vVols?** Change it, since vVols are due for removal (below). The array stays eligible, as VMFS or NFS.

3. **Can the hosts run ESA?** ESA takes [NVMe only](https://techdocs.broadcom.com/us/en/vmware-cis/vcf/vcf-9-0-and-later/9-1/design/design-library/storage-models-9-x/standard-vsan-storage-model/single-rack-storage-models/vcf-hardware-configuration-for-vsan.html), attached directly to a PCIe slot and never through a Tri-Mode controller. Hosts that can't meet that lose storage clusters and global deduplication, which leaves an array or OSA; [vSAN ESA or OSA](/vcf/library/architecture/vsan-esa-osa/) covers that choice.

4. **Will this cluster ever stretch, or run on two nodes?** Global deduplication is then out, [as Broadcom states for both](https://techdocs.broadcom.com/us/en/vmware-cis/vcf/vcf-9-0-and-later/9-1/design/design-library/storage-models-9-x/standard-vsan-storage-model/single-rack-storage-models/vcf-hardware-configuration-for-vsan.html). A storage cluster [must be stretched before its compute clusters](https://techdocs.broadcom.com/us/en/vmware-cis/vcf/vcf-9-0-and-later/9-1/building-your-private-cloud-infrastructure/stretching-clusters.html), at [four hosts per zone](https://techdocs.broadcom.com/us/en/vmware-cis/vcf/vcf-9-0-and-later/9-1/design/design-library/storage-models-9-x/standard-vsan-storage-model/stretched-cluster-storage-models/vsan-stretched-storage-cluster.html). On arrays, Broadcom's [Storage Models page](https://techdocs.broadcom.com/us/en/vmware-cis/vcf/vcf-9-0-and-later/9-1/design/vmware-cloud-foundation-concepts/storage-models.html) rules out stretching on iSCSI and NVMe, and points FC and NFS to vSphere Metro Storage Cluster, so a stretched design on an array comes down to those two.

5. **Is planned raw capacity at or below your core count, across the estate?** Then HCI needs no add-on. Above it, price the add-on, using the rules in [The vSAN entitlement](/vcf/library/value/vsan-entitlement/). Either way an array leaves its hosts' entitlement idle: 576 TiB in the example below.

6. **Is capacity, rather than compute, forcing new hosts?** Only then consider a storage cluster, as new hosts: an HCI cluster [can't be converted](https://techdocs.broadcom.com/us/en/vmware-cis/vcf/vcf-9-0-and-later/9-1/vsan-deployment-administration-and-monitoring/administering-vmware-vsan/expanding-and-managing-a-vsan-cluster/sharing-remote-vsan-datastores-with-hci-mesh.html) without deactivating vSAN. Check it clears the floor: ESA hardware, [4 hosts minimum and 32 maximum](https://techdocs.broadcom.com/us/en/vmware-cis/vcf/vcf-9-0-and-later/9-1/design/design-library/storage-models-9-x/standard-vsan-storage-model/single-rack-storage-models/vsan-storage-cluster-storage-model.html), [20 TiB per host and under 5 ms to clients](https://techdocs.broadcom.com/us/en/vmware-cis/vcf/vcf-9-0-and-later/9-1/vsan-deployment-administration-and-monitoring/administering-vmware-vsan/expanding-and-managing-a-vsan-cluster/sharing-remote-vsan-datastores-with-hci-mesh.html), 25 GbE for storage traffic and 10 GbE for clients. Design to seven hosts and 104, the stricter figures, because two Broadcom pages differ on size: the sharing page recommends six hosts and allows 128 per datastore, the Design Library says design for seven and allows 104.

7. **Do you already own an array with life left?** Compare it on price, below, and on what it gives up: every array protocol is marked without storage policy management, automated deployment and scale, and automated lifecycle in Broadcom's [feature table](https://techdocs.broadcom.com/us/en/vmware-cis/vcf/vcf-9-0-and-later/9-1/design/vmware-cloud-foundation-concepts/storage-models.html). An array that loses that comparison can still stay as supplemental storage beside vSAN principal, in all three columns.

## vVols

Don't start on them. They "will be fully removed in a future release" ([FAQ Q32](https://www.vmware.com/docs/vmware-cloud-foundation-9-1-general-faqs), dated 4 November 2026), and their 9.0 certification ended at once (<KB id={401070} />). The [workload domain page](https://techdocs.broadcom.com/us/en/vmware-cis/vcf/vcf-9-0-and-later/9-1/building-your-private-cloud-infrastructure/working-with-workload-domains/deploy-a-vi-workload-domain-using-the-sddc-manager-ui.html) still offers a vVol-based domain while calling the capability deprecated, and the Storage Models matrix has no row for vVols, so don't read that offer as support for a new design.

A management domain on vVols has one cross-type route in SDDC Manager: vVol to vSAN (<KB id={435850} />). For the removal-date dispute and the exit, follow [the deprecation radar](/vcf/library/architecture/deprecation-radar/) and [vSAN ESA or OSA](/vcf/library/architecture/vsan-esa-osa/).

## Not design inputs yet

Leave vSAN over Fibre Channel out of a 9.1 design. It appears in the show notes of [Unexplored Territory #129](https://www.yellow-bricks.com/2026/10/26/129-enhanced-compression-qlc-vsan-over-fc-and-much-more-featuring-anoop-gunturi/) (26 Oct 2026), a roadmap discussion with a disclaimer that it is "not a binding commitment", and the [9.1 what's new page for vSAN](https://techdocs.broadcom.com/us/en/vmware-cis/vcf/vcf-9-0-and-later/9-1/release-notes/vmware-cloud-foundation-9-1-0-0-release-notes/what-s-new/whats-new-vsan.html) doesn't mention it.

QLC "for various workloads" sits under the same disclaimer. In 9.1 today the devices are "not intended" for regular production and aim mainly at Cyber Recovery, [Duncan Epping's May post](https://www.yellow-bricks.com/2026/05/07/whats-new-for-vsan-in-9-1/) says, so don't plan regular production capacity on them.

Traffic separation and stretched vSphere-only clusters are only named as topics in the notes for [#114](https://www.yellow-bricks.com/2026/03/16/114-vsan-storage-clusters-with-kalyan-krishnaswamy/), which isn't enough to design against.

## A constructed example

At 300 TiB, HCI wins on licences. At 600 TiB with compute hosts limited to 8 drive bays, the storage cluster does.

Assumptions, invented except where a source is named:

- One workload domain, 12 compute hosts at 2 x 24 cores: 576 cores. It holds 300 TiB, with no data reduction or growth, and nothing else draws on the pool.
- RAID-6 at 1.5x and this site's 30% free margin, as in [The vSAN entitlement](/vcf/library/value/vsan-entitlement/). Raw needed: 300 x 1.5 / 0.7 = 642.9 TiB. A 7.68 TB drive is 6.98 TiB.
- Storage hosts run 2 x 16 cores and carry VCF. Confirm that with Broadcom: <KB id={313548} /> doesn't name storage clusters, though the 2024 Signal65 model licensed them this way.
- Prices come from Signal65's October 2026 list-price model: add-on at $17.50 per TiB per month, $1,050 over five years, and VCF at $2,000 per core over five years ($4,096,000 / 2,048 cores). Replace them with your quotes.

| Design | Drives | Raw claimed | Licensed cores | Add-on | Extra licence cost, 5 years |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ESA HCI | 8 x 12 hosts | 670.6 TiB | 576 | 95 TiB | $99,750 |
| Storage cluster, 6 hosts | 16 x 6 hosts | 670.6 TiB | 576 + 192 = 768 | None, 97 TiB spare | 192 x $2,000 = $384,000, plus 6 servers |
| FC array | None | 0 | 576 | None, 576 TiB idle | $0, plus array and fabric |

HCI wins by $284,250 in licences before the extra servers, and by $64,000 more at the seven hosts advised above. The price gap explains it: a core brings 1 TiB (<KB id={313548} />) for $2,000, and a TiB alone costs $1,050.

Double the data to 600 TiB and limit the compute hosts to 8 drive bays, and capacity is now what forces new hosts in, as in question 6. The storage cluster saves $182,400 for the same number of added servers, because storage hosts don't need 48 cores. The 1,285.7 TiB of raw needed takes 192 drives either way, which claims 1,341.1 TiB, and add-on is that figure less all licensed cores, rounded up. HCI needs 24 hosts: 576 more cores ($1,152,000), making 1,152, plus 190 TiB of add-on ($199,500), or $1,351,500. Twelve 32-core storage hosts with 16 drives each need 384 cores ($768,000), making 960, plus 382 TiB ($401,100), or $1,169,100.

The array's price to beat in the 300 TiB case is about **$2.32M**: an array with fabric and five years' support, quoted at 300 TiB usable, wins below that, before admin time and either side's data reduction. The sum starts from the HCI flash. 96 drives is 737.3 TB, which at the same model's tier-one $2,466 per TB is $1,818,132. Its 4.39% yearly support adds $399,080 over five years, and the add-on $99,750.

| Evidence | Who paid | Claim | What limits it |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Signal65, Oct 2026](https://www.vmware.com/docs/signal65-storage-tco-comparison-october-2026) | Report: "In partnership with", "was asked to compare". [Broadcom's post](https://blogs.vmware.com/cloud-foundation/2026/10/23/signal65-storage-tco-october-2026/): "sponsored by Broadcom" | vSAN 13.5% cheaper at 12 hosts, 39.5% at 64. "Up to 62" in the post | vSAN on the compute hosts against FC, at equal raw capacity, with no storage-only hosts priced. Excludes facilities, migration and downtime. The report itself won't call it a TCO |
| [Signal65, July 2024](https://www.signal65.com/research/the-economics-of-disaggregated-private-cloud-storage/) | Page: "Research commissioned by" VMware by Broadcom | $2.66M against $3.69M over 5 years, 27.83% | The only one modelling storage-only nodes. 2024 prices, with a 38% array discount and 17% on servers |
| [Pure Storage blog, Jun 2025](https://blog.everpuredata.com/solutions/rethink-vsan-save-up-to-62-on-your-vmware-stack/) | An array vendor; credits ESG, no sponsor stated | 42% to 62% lower TCO than vSAN over 3 years | The post gives no inputs to test |

<Callout type="analysis" title="How to read them">
Use the October report for its inputs and ignore its headline. The 62% needs build-to-order hosts priced from one integrator, with no response-time commitment. The 13.5% doesn't apply to a VCF buyer, because at 12 hosts the report licenses FC on vSphere Standard while vSAN carries VCF: add the $672,000 licence gap to its capital table and vSAN leads by 31%. The report lists its raw-capacity basis among simplifications that understate FC cost, yet an array returning more usable capacity per raw TB would narrow the gap. The 2024 model's saving comes entirely from year one: that year's $1.09M exceeds the whole $1.03M total, and vSAN costs more in years 3 to 5. The direction is plausible, since arrays carry controllers, fabric and dearer support. The size won't transfer to your estate, so get a quote at usable capacity and run the example with it.
</Callout>
