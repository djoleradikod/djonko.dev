<InShort>
**Lab or proof of concept: Simple.** **New 9.1.1 production fleet: Small HA**, unless Configuration Maximums or your design review pushes you to Medium. Medium costs only 6 vCPU and 24 GB more, so let capacity limits decide that step. Start small because documented scaling only goes up. **Upgraded from 9.1.0:** you keep the old sizes until you run <KB id={455842} />, and only a Day-0-only cluster can. **Network:** take the VM management network unless you have a reason to separate it, because Identity Broker has to go with it. This site ran none of this; figures are Broadcom's or William Lam's, as marked.
</InShort>

<Callout type="note" title="Corrected on 7 November 2026">
This note said a reduced footprint was available after upgrade, and that the runtime pool had to sit on the management network in 9.1.0. The reduction applies only to Day-0-only clusters, the 9.1.0 API already accepted a VLAN-backed network, and 9.1.1 adds the choice to the UI.
</Callout>

## The decision

| Your situation | Choose | What decides it |
|---|---|---|
| Lab or proof of concept | Simple Small | Broadcom recommends it for labs and proofs of concept. Losing the one control plane node means a redeploy and a restore. |
| New production fleet on 9.1.1 | Small HA | <KB id={452952} /> calls it "optimized for production environments" that need HA from 9.1.1.0. Three control plane nodes for 14 vCPU and 20 GB over Simple. |
| Production beyond Small's limits, or a design tied to Broadcom's design library | Medium, then Large | The installer points to Configuration Maximums for limits. The HA design page lists only Medium and Large. |
| Staying on 9.1.0 and needing HA | Medium | <KB id={452952} /> warns that forcing HA onto Small on 9.1 leaves an "unsupported, broken, and unrecoverable state". Lam's June script is, in his words, not officially supported. |

<Callout type="analysis" title="What would change this answer">
Lam documents each option and leaves the choice open. This site's reasoning for Small HA: Broadcom's [Simple](https://techdocs.broadcom.com/us/en/vmware-cis/vcf/vcf-9-0-and-later/9-1/design/design-library/vcf-management-services-availabilty-detailed-design/vcf-management-services-simple-deployment-model.html) and [HA](https://techdocs.broadcom.com/us/en/vmware-cis/vcf/vcf-9-0-and-later/9-1/design/design-library/vcf-management-services-availabilty-detailed-design/vcf-management-services-high-availability-deployment-model.html) design pages describe scaling out and up and never down (checked 7 Nov 2026). If your change process makes a later scale-up expensive, 6 vCPU and 24 GB is cheap insurance, so take Medium on day one.
</Callout>

| Small HA | 3 × 4/10 | 3 × 10/16 | 42 | 78 | 3,100 |
| Medium | 3 × 4/10 | 3 × 12/24 | 48 | 102 | 3,600 |
| Large | 3 × 8/14 | 4 × 16/32 | 88 | 170 | 4,302 |
| *Upgraded 9.1.0 Simple* | 1 × 4/10 | 3 × 12/24 | 40 | 82 | |
| *Upgraded 9.1.0 Medium* | 3 × 4/10 | 3 × 24/48 | 84 | 174 | |
| *Upgraded 9.1.0 Large* | 3 × 8/12 | 4 × 24/48 | 120 | 228 | |

**Whose they are.** The first four rows' sizes and disk are from [Broadcom's workbook](https://techdocs.broadcom.com/content/dam/broadcom/techdocs/us/en/assets/vmware-cis/vcf/vcf-9.1.1-planning-and-preparation-workbook.xlsx) (v1.9.1.103, 12 Oct). Worker counts are the workbook's, except Large, which follows the [Fleet Sizing page](https://techdocs.broadcom.com/us/en/vmware-cis/vcf/vcf-9-0-and-later/9-1/design/vmware-cloud-foundation-concepts/vcf-fleet-sizing-models-9-x.html) (5 Nov). Italic rows are [Lam's 22 June node sizes](https://williamlam.com/2026/06/vcf-9-1-enabling-high-availability-for-a-small-vcf-management-services-vcfms-deployment.html). Totals are this site's sums. Lam's [3 Sep installer screenshots](https://williamlam.com/2026/09/10-exciting-enhancements-in-vmware-cloud-foundation-9-1-1.html) show the same 28/58 and 42/78, with disk of 2,900 and 3,201 GB, so plan on the higher number. For whole-fleet totals, see [Sizing a VCF 9.1 lab](/vcf/library/labs/sizing-a-vcf-9-lab/).

**Where Broadcom's pages disagree.** The Simple design page still requires three workers, the HA design page has no Small option, and the Fleet Sizing page's "High Availability - Small" profile still pairs with a Medium runtime. The workbook, the installer and <KB id={452952} /> agree with each other, so go with those. They part on Large: the Fleet Sizing page shows 4 workers, as Lam's June list does, and the workbook 3, which would make it 72 vCPU and 138 GB. Plan on 4. On an additional instance, the Fleet Sizing page shows 2 workers at every size and the workbook 1 on Simple and 3 on HA.

**Workers are a floor.** An autoscaler changes the count as components are added, which Broadcom calls standard operation (<KB id={455806} />). The workbook budgets Real-time Metrics at 16 vCPU and 20 GB on Small sizes and 31 vCPU and 41 GB on Medium, and Log Management at 8 vCPU and 16 GB per Small replica. By this site's working of its formula, adding Real-time Metrics takes Small HA to 5 workers (62 vCPU, 110 GB) and Medium to 5 (72 vCPU, 150 GB). Treat those as planning figures.

## What 9.1.1 changed

- **Footprint.** Lam's [3 Sep post](https://williamlam.com/2026/09/10-exciting-enhancements-in-vmware-cloud-foundation-9-1-1.html) explains that 9.1.0 sized for the largest optional Day-N service and 9.1.1 sizes for Day-0, removing one worker from Simple. The workbook shows it reaching further: a Medium worker halves from Lam's June 24/48 to 12/24.
- **Small HA.** Lam's [22 June post](https://williamlam.com/2026/06/vcf-9-1-enabling-high-availability-for-a-small-vcf-management-services-vcfms-deployment.html) scripted it, unsupported, for 9.1. His 3 Sep post records the supported version: `small_ha` in the installer's `vspClusterSpec`, or later through Scale on VCF Services Runtime in VCF Operations. VCF Automation has no matching size and deploys as a singleton, he adds in the comments.
- **UI placement.** Lam's [19 Oct post](https://williamlam.com/2026/10/vcf-9-1-1-new-ui-support-for-deploying-vcf-management-services-vcfms-vcf-automation-vcfa-on-non-management-networks.html) covers the network choice when deploying the runtime during an upgrade to 9.1.1, which used to need the API. The 9.1.0 installer already offered it, he notes.

## After an upgrade: run the script or not

New 9.1.1 deployments get the small sizes. Upgraded ones keep theirs until you run the script in <KB id={455842} />, which [Lam walks through](https://williamlam.com/2026/09/vcf-9-1-1-adopting-the-reduced-vcf-management-services-vcfms-footprint-after-an-upgrade.html) on Simple (3 Sep). The script stops without changes if Log Management or Real-time Metrics is installed or on a consumption cluster, and the KB excludes vSphere Foundation. It needs a maintenance window because the worker rollout can disrupt operations. The KB doesn't publish its target values or cover going back.

Lam's example saves 12 vCPU and 24 GB. By the table above an upgraded Medium would save 36 vCPU and 72 GB, which is this site's inference: the KB says only that the script applies values matching the size.

<Callout type="analysis" title="Run it when">
Run it on a Day-0-only cluster if neither Day-N service is planned this year. Skip it on an upgraded Simple if one is: the autoscaler will add workers back and you'll have spent a maintenance window on 12 vCPU.
</Callout>

## Network placement and addressing

**Where it goes.** Leave the deployment network blank and 9.1.1 uses the VM management network. Pick another only with a stated separation requirement, because [Broadcom requires](https://techdocs.broadcom.com/us/en/vmware-cis/vcf/vcf-9-0-and-later/9-1/deployment/upgrading-cloud-foundation/deploy-vcf-management-services.html) management services and Identity Broker to share a network. If a 9.0.x broker sits off the management network, settle that first: the [route chooser](/vcf/library/upgrades/upgrade-route-chooser/) covers it. Without the 9.1.1 UI, the API's `xRegionNetwork` is the route.

**Pool.** Minimum 12 addresses (/28), recommended 30 (/27), and <KB id={440630} /> allows 18 more afterwards. Take the /27. Small HA with Real-time Metrics is already 8 nodes, and [why-did-it.fail's 10 June write-up](https://www.why-did-it.fail/blog/2026-05-lets-talk-about-vcf-mgmt-svc/) notes that lifecycle operations create new VMs.

**Internal range.** The default is `198.18.0.0/15`, and Broadcom lists two alternatives in the 240 and 250 ranges. It must not overlap your management, VM or pool networks. The 9.1.0 UI can't change it, the 9.1.1 UI can, and the API spec carries it as `internalClusterCidrIpv4`.

**Names.** Five FQDNs: runtime, fleet, instance, Identity Broker, License Server. The first three must resolve to unused addresses outside the pool. Runtime, fleet, instance and broker names take no capitals, and `.local` isn't supported.

**Firewalls.** The depot uses runtime pool addresses, so rules written for the installer may not cover it (<KB id={443216} />).

## What you can't change later

| Choice | Status | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Size or HA, downward | No documented path. Lam's June post says his unsupported method scaled back down in testing; his 9 July reply on it says "you can not scale back down". Trust the later word | Lam; Broadcom silent |
| Addresses excluded from the pool | Can't later be used for VCF components | Broadcom |
| Node VM folder, resource pool, datastore | Don't move them. A changed cluster, pool or network object fails the upgrade with `VCFMS-HEALTH-002` (<KB id={452124} />) | Broadcom |
| Deployment network, internal range | No documented change after deployment. Treat as fixed | This site's inference from the same KB |
| Return to the 9.0 appliance | Broadcom's deploy page describes no way back (checked 7 Nov 2026) | Broadcom silent |

## What moved from Aria Lifecycle

5.x ran Aria Suite Lifecycle as an appliance, and 9.0 renamed it VCF Operations fleet management. 9.1 splits it into two services on the runtime, a Kubernetes cluster that is mandatory for VCF and optional for vSphere Foundation (<KB id={440630} />). KBs and logs call it the services runtime, VCFMS or VMSP.

| 5.2 | 9.0 | 9.1 service | Runs in |
|---|---|---|---|
| Aria Suite Lifecycle | Fleet management | Fleet lifecycle | First instance |
| Aria Suite Lifecycle | Fleet management | SDDC lifecycle | Every instance |
| Workspace ONE Access | Identity Broker | Identity broker | First instance, others Day-N |
| Aria Operations for Logs | Operations for logs | Log management | First instance, Day-N |

[Broadcom's table](https://techdocs.broadcom.com/us/en/vmware-cis/vcf/vcf-9-0-and-later/9-1/deployment/vcf-management-appliances.html) gives no predecessor for the software depot (first instance), Salt RaaS (first instance), Salt master and telemetry (every instance), or real-time metrics (every instance, Day-N).

The deployment step is in [Going from 9.0 to 9.1](/vcf/library/upgrades/90-to-91-management-services/). Day-2 references: health dashboard <KB id={417255} />, shutdown script <KB id={440874} />, power-on order after a graceful shutdown <KB id={440862} />, pre-upgrade checks <KB id={454898} />.
