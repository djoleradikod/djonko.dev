<InShort>
Start with one fleet and add instances before fleets: at HA Medium a second fleet costs 110 vCPU more than a second instance. **A second fleet** is justified when a site sits beyond 300 ms of VCF Operations and VCF Automation, or when a group's staff must have their own console and sign-on. **A second instance** is justified when a site sits beyond 100 ms of SDDC Manager, when you need Broadcom's DR design, or when you want a separate SSO realm or lifecycle boundary inside the fleet. Licensing rarely forces either, and a business unit alone forces neither. Make the call early, since a 9.1 instance has no supported way to move to another 9.1 fleet, William Lam reports <AsOf date="2026-11-07" />.
</InShort>

The terms are in <a href="/vcf/library/architecture/vcf9-mental-model/">the VCF 9 mental model</a>. Ask these in order. Questions 1, 2 and 4 can end the discussion; 3 and 5 rarely do.

| # | Question | Answer that decides it | What it rules out |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | How far is each site from VCF Operations and from SDDC Manager? | Beyond 300 ms or beyond 100 ms | Sharing that fleet, or sharing that instance |
| 2 | Must the console sign-on differ between groups? | Yes | One fleet |
| 3 | Does the contract split capacity? | Rarely decisive | A license server or license shared across fleets |
| 4 | Must an auditor, regulator or buyer see separate management? | Yes | One fleet |
| 5 | Will one VCF Operations carry the load? | Only at very large scale | Nothing fixed: no instance count limit is published |

## 1. Latency

On this note's reading, two bounds change the count. A site past 300 ms of VCF Operations and VCF Automation goes to a second fleet, and a site past 100 ms of SDDC Manager goes to a second instance. The other rows add a local component or mean that NSX Manager can't serve the site.

The bounds are from the fleet latency diagram, Ver 2.0, labelled for VCF 9.1, which William Lam published on 12 May 2026. His post doesn't mention 9.1.1 and the diagram lives on Broadcom's ports site, so check there for revisions. The third column is this site's reading of those bounds.

| Path | Upper bound (9.1) | If you can't meet it |
|---|---|---|
| VCF Operations and VCF Automation to an instance's vCenter and NSX Manager | ≤300 ms | Second fleet |
| Identity broker to VCF Operations, VCF Automation, vCenter and NSX Manager | ≤300 ms | Run a broker in that instance, drawn as optional |
| License server to vCenter | ≤300 ms | Local license server, which Broadcom documents for this case |
| SDDC Manager to vCenter, including workload domain vCenters | ≤100 ms | Second instance |
| NSX Manager to NSX Edge and to ESX | ≤150 ms | That NSX Manager can't serve the site |
| Collector to vCenter, NSX Manager and SDDC Manager | ≤50 ms | Local collector |

Long-distance vMotion and Enhanced Linked Mode between vCenters have their own ≤150 ms on the same diagram.

A site at 80 ms clears the SDDC Manager bound but not the collector's, so it needs a collector and can still be a workload domain. A remote workload domain keeps its ≤50 ms with a local collector while staying within ≤300 ms of VCF Operations, in Lam's post on the 9.0 diagram (11 December 2025). These are upper bounds, the same post says, warning that transfers and the UI slow as latency rises, so meeting a bound doesn't promise a fast site.

<Callout type="analysis" title="Two readings behind these limits">
The diagram doesn't say whether its figures are round trip or one way, so design to round trip, the stricter reading. A second fleet beyond 300 ms is an inference: the 9.1 diagram draws the VCF Operations and VCF Automation paths at ≤300 ms, and this note takes that as the limit. A reader asked Lam under the 9.0 post whether additional instances can sit within 300 ms of the first, and no reply appears there (checked 7 Nov 2026).
</Callout>

## 2. Identity

A group that needs its own sign-on to VCF Operations needs its own fleet. A group that needs its own sign-on to vCenter and NSX can stay in the fleet. The single-instance model limits a realm and an outage to one instance, but it doesn't separate the console, because VCF Operations and VCF Automation "can connect to only one of identity broker instances".

Every SSO model Broadcom documents stops at the fleet. The fleet-wide model has a single broker per fleet, and the cross-instance model serves instances "in the same VCF fleet". So two fleets mean two realms and two brokers, on this site's reading. Broadcom says an additional broker in the cross-instance model is "managed separately" for tasks such as backup and restore, so expect at least that across fleets.

<a href="/vcf/library/architecture/identity-broker/">Identity in VCF 9</a> covers modes and blast radius.

## 3. Licensing

Licensing is mostly a consequence of the fleet count and rarely justifies a fleet on its own. A second fleet means a second license server and its own license, split from your capacity, since you "cannot share a license server between fleets" and "cannot add one license to more than one license servers", in Broadcom's words.

A contract that demands separate administration can still force a fleet. A second Site ID alone doesn't, since from 9.1 one vCenter can take licenses from several Site IDs (VMware, 18 May 2026). A license split for administrative division or a weak link doesn't need a fleet either: Broadcom documents extra license servers inside a fleet for both. This isn't purchasing advice, so check your contract.

## 4. Non-technical separation

Geography alone doesn't call for a second fleet, since a fleet can "span across multiple regions" (Broadcom's 4 November FAQ, Q53). TechDocs' one example of a split is a fleet each for AMER, EMEA and APAC, and it comes with a warning about overhead. Availability or "other non-technical requirements" may require multiple fleets, Lam says, but he doesn't list them, so test the requirement with the question below.

<Callout type="analysis" title="The test this note would apply">
Ask who may see the other group's inventory, metrics and logs. If the answer is nobody, including platform administrators, build a second fleet. Do the same for a business likely to be sold: with no supported instance move, it is easier to hand over if it never shared a fleet. An organisation chart doesn't pass, and <a href="/vcf/library/architecture/vcf9-mental-model/">the VCF 9 mental model</a> points business units to workload domains.
</Callout>

## 5. No fixed instance limit

This note doesn't expect instance count alone to force a second fleet, short of very large scale. On 9.0 there's no fixed number of instances per fleet: it depends on objects, metrics, latency and bandwidth, Lam reported on 3 October 2025. Broadcom's 9.1 sizing page states no maximum either.

Size from Broadcom's 9.0 sizing article, <KB id={397782} />, and confirm 9.1 values, which it doesn't cover. The article and Lam's Extra Large figure agree on the totals (1,056,000 and 126,000,000 in the KB, 1M objects and 126M metrics in his post) and differ on nodes: he gives 16, and the KB lists 12 as the cluster maximum.

## What a second fleet costs

A second fleet carries its own VCF Operations, VCF Automation and license server, and a second instance doesn't. At HA Medium the two sizing templates in Broadcom's 9.1 docs differ by 110 vCPU, 412 GB of RAM and 5,632 GB of disk. The initial-instance template includes all three, and the template for an instance joining an existing fleet omits them. A second fleet starts from its own initial instance, so it takes the first template and a second instance takes the other.

| Template | vCPU | RAM | Disk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Initial instance, HA Medium | 184 | 656 GB | 11,445 GB |
| Additional instance, HA Medium | 74 | 244 GB | 5,813 GB |
| **Difference** | **110** | **412 GB** | **5,632 GB** |

## What can and can't change later

If you're upgrading several 5.x or 9.0.x instances, settle the fleet map before any of them reaches 9.1. After that step the instance has no supported move to another 9.1 fleet, Lam reports.

| Change | Status |
|---|---|
| 5.x or 9.0.x instance into a 9.1 fleet | Supported per Lam, before that instance goes to 9.1. Historical Aria Operations data doesn't move |
| 9.1 instance into another 9.1 fleet | Lam, 14 July 2026, updated 1 August: "no supported method". Product management is evaluating it, with no commitment |
| Merging fleets | Not described in the pages cited here (checked 7 Nov 2026). Plan as if unavailable |
| Fleet services off the first instance | TechDocs places them there and describes no move. Choose the site as if permanent |
| Broker mode | Changeable. The 9.1 procedure starts with deleting the SSO configuration, and a separate embedded-to-instance migration exists |

## Example 1: three sites, one company (constructed)

**Assumptions:** VCF Operations in Dublin. Round trips of 25 ms to Frankfurt and 180 ms to Singapore. One operations team, identity provider and contract, no separation demanded, HA Medium.

- Singapore: 180 is within 300, so it stays in the fleet. 180 is over 100, so it needs its own instance.
- Frankfurt: 25 is within 50, 100 and 150, so it can be a workload domain of the Dublin instance.

**Result:** one fleet, two instances, 184 + 74 = 258 vCPU of management templates. If Frankfurt becomes the DR target, the DR design in TechDocs gives it an instance, which makes three instances and 332 vCPU. If Singapore's regulator demands a separate console, the answer becomes two fleets.

## Example 2: a regulated subsidiary on one campus (constructed)

**Assumptions:** two companies 2 ms apart. The subsidiary's regulator requires separate administrators and no shared console, and it has its own identity provider. A sale within three years is possible. HA Medium.

Latency allows one fleet. Identity doesn't, since one VCF Operations connects to one broker, and the regulator settles it.

**Result:** two fleets, at 368 vCPU, 1,312 GB and 22,890 GB against 258 vCPU, 900 GB and 17,258 GB for one fleet and two instances. It also means a second license server and a split license. If the regulator accepts role-based separation in a shared console, a second instance with single-instance SSO saves the 110 vCPU, but that instance can't later move fleets, which is the risk if the sale goes ahead.
