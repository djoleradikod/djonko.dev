<InShort>
**Start with Security Posture Management, which the 4 November FAQ puts in the baseline VCF entitlement, and the free hardening guide, and buy an add-on where core has no substitute.** That order is analysis; the product facts below are checked against Broadcom's 4 November documents <AsOf date="2026-11-07" />.

- Core VCF and VCF Edge include Security Posture Management, the Security Operations dashboard, Audit Trail and drift detection on vCenter and clusters. The hardening guide is free.
- The add-ons are VMware Compliance and Configuration Management for what runs inside guests, VMware vDefend for firewall rules, VMware Live Recovery for clean-room recovery, VMware HyperArmor for runtime defence of ESX.
- VVF has no marks on the Feature Comparison's compliance-with-remediation or Security Operations rows, which argues against it if the auditor wants posture reports from the platform.
- Plan on the 4 November FAQ, which says Security Posture Management needs no add-on, and get that in writing, because a May post says it does.

Not legal, compliance or purchasing advice. Your order documents and your auditor decide.
</InShort>

## Decide in this order

1. **VCF, Edge or VVF?** If the auditor wants posture reports from the platform, that alone argues against VVF: it has no marks for compliance, SecOps or drift (FC p13-15), though it does have audit events (FC p14) and the p4 hardening rows. Firewall rules raise a second doubt. <KB id={438228} /> ties vDefend to a VCF base licence, and [the matrix](/vcf/library/value/using-what-vcf-includes/) records the conflict over vDefend on VVF.

2. **Do you hold Advanced Cyber Compliance?** List which of its functions you depend on, because question 5 turns on it. You can use it until the entitlement ends, and Broadcom then offers Live Recovery and Compliance and Configuration Management (Q86, p13). [The portfolio note](/vcf/library/licensing/november-2026-portfolio/) covers the renewal.

3. **Is the audit scope VCF or what runs on it?** Guest OS scope points to the add-on or to a configuration tool you already run. Security Posture Management lists 14 components, including ESX, vCenter, NSX, SDDC Manager and "Virtual Machines". Read that last entry as the VM object. That's an inference from TechDocs, which says the add-on is what reaches operating systems and databases. The list has no vSAN entry, so ask if vSAN settings are in scope.

4. **Which framework does your auditor name?** PCI DSS v4.0.1 and the SCG are built in.
   - Don't plan a 9.1 audit on TechDocs' [Regulatory Benchmark Details](https://techdocs.broadcom.com/us/en/vmware-cis/vcf/vcf-9-0-and-later/9-1/security-and-compliance/viewing-and-configuring-compliance/measuring-compliance-of-objects/regulatory-benchmark-details.html) (5 Nov) or assume its packs carry over. It lists HIPAA, ISO/IEC 27001, FISMA and NIST controls, but states "Compliance Benchmarks are deprecated in VCF 9.1", and the newest CIS benchmark it lists is ESXi 7.0 and the newest DISA STIG is vSphere 8.0. It says compliance now sits in Security Posture Management, and that product's page names none of them (checked 7 Nov 2026).
   - The route Broadcom documents is the SCG's mappings or a custom benchmark. Show the auditor the mapping before relying on it, since [the guide](https://github.com/vmware/vcf-security-and-compliance-guidelines/tree/main/security-configuration-hardening-guide/cloud-foundation/9.1) warns there is "not a one-to-one mapping". The SCG has 260 controls aligned to NIST 800-53 R5, PCI DSS 4.0.1 and the Secure Controls Framework, by [Kacy Reed](https://blogs.vmware.com/cloud-foundation/2026/08/06/securing-your-vmware-cloud-foundation-9-1-environment/)'s count (6 Aug).
   - For DISA, the only set marked for 9.1.1.0 is the [Y26M09 content](https://github.com/vmware/dod-compliance-and-automation/tree/master/vcf/9.x), and it is a STIG Readiness Guide. The official DISA-reviewed STIG was "in progress" on 14 July, Broadcom's [Darrick Williams](https://blogs.vmware.com/cloud-foundation/2026/07/14/securing-the-foundation-vmware-cloud-foundation-9-1-stig-compliance/) wrote, so check whether your auditor accepts a readiness guide.

5. **Is detection enough, or must drift be corrected automatically?** In core, a person acts: on demand in Security Posture Management, by hand in vCenter for drift. Automatic ESX remediation is an Advanced Cyber Compliance function, by Reed's description. "VCF infrastructure configuration management and compliance" is listed as a native feature "planned as part of the next release", estimated for Q1FY27 ([4 November post](https://blogs.vmware.com/cloud-foundation/2026/11/04/broadcom-vcf-portfolio-updates/)). Leave it out of a design until it ships.

6. **Does your design rely on firewall rules between workloads?** Then you need vDefend, and the plan has to include the License Hub appliance. Check which line you hold, since the name varies by document: FC p17 sells "with ATP", Q15 says "vDefend Firewall", TechDocs says "VMware vDefend", and KB 438228 lists both firewall products.

7. **Must you prove a restore point is clean?** Then Live Recovery. If a restorable copy is enough, core covers it, and [the recovery note](/vcf/library/integrations/dr-and-cyber-recovery-choice/) carries that decision. Ask whether isolation needs a vDefend line too, because the [release notes](https://techdocs.broadcom.com/us/en/vmware-cis/vcf/protection-and-recovery/9-1/release-notes/protection-and-recovery-91-release-notes.html) describe "vDefend for push-button network isolation".

8. **Is the unpatched window on ESX your remaining exposure?** That is HyperArmor's case, and it goes last because Broadcom has published no date. It covers virtual patching, positive guardrails and MFA on the SSH path ([13 Oct](https://blogs.vmware.com/cloud-foundation/2026/10/13/proactive-hypervisor-defense-introducing-vmware-hyperarmor/)), for [ESX 8.x and later](https://mfn.se/all/a/broadcom/broadcom-introduces-vmware-hyperarmor-expanding-hypervisor-security-and-protections-for-vmware-cloud-foundation-893cf3cd). Network threats stay with vDefend and recovery with Live Recovery, the 13 October post says. [Channel Dive](https://www.channeldive.com/news/broadcom-vmware-hypervisor-security-software-ai-attacks/833031/) (press, 14 Oct) reports per-core sale through partners, general availability expected in Q1FY27 and no disclosed price.

<Callout type="analysis" title="Where to put the money">
Run Security Posture Management and the SCG first, since the FAQ puts the first in the baseline entitlement, the SCG is free and the results show what is left. Then buy where core has no substitute: vDefend if rules between workloads are required, Live Recovery if clean restore must be proven. Compliance and Configuration Management is the easiest to defer when another tool already governs guests. Leave HyperArmor until the date and price are written down.

The gap to raise at renewal is automatic ESX remediation between an ACC expiry and the native feature shipping. Q86 names the replacement add-on "for workloads", while Reed credits Advanced Cyber Compliance with that function, and no Broadcom page says what does the job in between. The gap is inference from those two statements, so ask before the term ends.
</Callout>

## Where the sources split

| Question | One says | Another says | Plan on |
|---|---|---|---|
| Is Security Posture Management an add-on? | "[Will require](https://blogs.vmware.com/cloud-foundation/2026/05/05/scale-simplify-and-secure-your-private-cloud-operations-with-vcf-9-1/)" the ACC add-on (Hah, 5 May) | Baseline entitlement (Q89 p14, 4 Nov). TechDocs is silent | Q89. It names a product added on 4 November, so it is new text, unlike Q41 (p7), which still calls 9.1 "upcoming". Get it in writing |
| Does core remediate VCF components today? | Yes, for listed rules (Q41 p7, FC p14, TechDocs) | Enforcement and drift remediation are planned (4 Nov post) | Today: on-demand and rule-limited. Read the planned item as the automatic layer, though no page confirms it |
| Data-in-use encryption (Intel TDX, AMD SEV-SNP) | "[Available through](https://blogs.vmware.com/cloud-foundation/2026/05/05/platform-security-vcf-9-1/)" ACC (Pisolkar, 5 May) | VCF and Edge marked, no footnote (FC p4). "Native" (4 Nov post) | Core, on the newer pair |
| Does VVF have a compliance framework? | Yes, lists NIST, CIS, PCI, ISO, HIPAA ([Romero, 3 Aug](https://blogs.vmware.com/cloud-foundation/2026/08/03/your-first-line-of-defense-how-vmware-vsphere-foundation-9-1-addresses-ai-era-security-threats/)) | No mark (FC p14) | FC, the entitlement document |
| Is HyperArmor orderable? | Listed as available, with no date (4 Nov post), and for [VCF and VVF](https://blogs.vmware.com/cloud-foundation/2026/10/13/proactive-hypervisor-defense-introducing-vmware-hyperarmor/) (13 Oct) | Absent from the TechDocs list (5 Nov) and the FAQ text | Ask for a date and a quote |

Names split too, which matters on an order form.

- Compliance is replaced by Security Posture Management, the 9.1 [support notes](https://techdocs.broadcom.com/us/en/vmware-cis/vcf/vcf-9-0-and-later/9-1/release-notes/vmware-cloud-foundation-9-1-0-0-release-notes/vcf-91-product-support-notes.html) say, yet the FC's p14 row reads "Compliance Management with remediation".
- FC p17 has "Workload Compliance" and "Advanced Security" as separate categories with products under them. Q3 (p1) prints them as one bullet and names no product.
- The May [release notes](https://techdocs.broadcom.com/us/en/vmware-cis/vcf/protection-and-recovery/9-1/release-notes/protection-and-recovery-91-release-notes.html) say Live Recovery "has been renamed and integrated" into VCF as Protection and Recovery. The 4 November FC (p17) and FAQ (Q86) still sell VMware Live Recovery as an add-on, and FAQ Q78 uses Protection and Recovery for core rights. [The recovery note](/vcf/library/integrations/dr-and-cyber-recovery-choice/) offers a reading of the overlap.
