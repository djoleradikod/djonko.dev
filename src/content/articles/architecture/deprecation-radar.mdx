<InShort>
**Clear the blockers, then fix what changes without warning.** The vCenter upgrade to 9.1 stops on IPFIX on uplinks or LACP ports. It doesn't stop for the rest: vCLS goes off for good, syslog to vCenter on port 514 is blocked, vCenter logins to VCF Operations end, and the Network Share plugin is deleted with its data. Only two calendar deadlines exist: the NSX Load Balancer bridge on 30 May 2027, and vSphere 8 and Aria Operations 8.18 on 11 October 2027. Buy no server that depends on anything marked deprecated below.
</InShort>

Broadcom keeps this list across a page per release and one per component, and a KB per hardware class. The 9.1 page reads "None" under vSAN, which is easy to take for good news: the 9.0 notices against OSA hybrid and vVols still stand. Here it is as one table, sorted by who has to act, with the wording of each deadline kept and the disputes named. <AsOf date="2026-11-06" />

## What the upgrade does by itself

**It stops** for IPFIX on uplinks or LACP ports (vCenter 9.1), for HCX WAN Optimization or VMs under HCX protection (HCX 9.0), and for a discontinued CPU (the installer). Clear those first. End of Life I/O devices may not stop it, which is worse: Broadcom says the upgrade can fail or cost you storage and network access, so replace them before you start. Estates with several NSX instances per vCenter have to change topology before NSX 9.0.

**It carries on** through everything marked "Effective now" or "Removed" for 9.1 under Operations. The one that destroys something is the Network Share plugin, where [Broadcom's notes](https://techdocs.broadcom.com/us/en/vmware-cis/vcf/vcf-9-0-and-later/9-1/release-notes/vmware-cloud-foundation-9-1-0-0-release-notes/vcf-91-product-support-notes.html) say the plugin, its settings and its data are removed automatically during the upgrade.

An estate on 5.2 takes both lists in one move, because [the upgrade guide](https://techdocs.broadcom.com/us/en/vmware-cis/vcf/vcf-9-0-and-later/9-1/deployment/upgrading-cloud-foundation.html) (5 Nov 2026) allows a skip-level upgrade to 9.1.x. Read every 9.0 row as well as the 9.1 rows.

## How to read the status column

| Status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Removed | Gone in that release, or the installer refuses it |
| Effective now | Behaviour changes on upgrade, whatever the heading on Broadcom's page says |
| Deprecated: next major | Works today. Broadcom names the next major release |
| Deprecated: future major | Works today. A major release, not necessarily the next |
| Deprecated: future release | Works today. Could be any release |
| Flagged by others | Not Broadcom's statement. These rows sit at the end of the table |

[KB 318697](https://knowledge.broadcom.com/external/article/318697/cpu-support-deprecation-and-discontinuat.htm) defines a major release as one that increments the first digit, so "next major" means the release after the 9.x series.

<Callout type="analysis" title="Whose reasoning this is">
Status, release and any named replacement come from the linked sources. The "What to do" column and generic entries such as "a NIC listed in the Compatibility Guide" are this site's advice, and so is applying the KB's CPU definition of "major" to the rest of the table. Broadcom doesn't say whether the [next VCF release](https://blogs.vmware.com/cloud-foundation/2026/11/04/broadcom-vcf-portfolio-updates/), planned for "Q1FY27", is major or minor. [StorageReview](https://www.storagereview.com/news/broadcom-vcf-portfolio-update-vsphere-express-live-recovery) reads that quarter as running to the start of February, which is why this note is due for review on 15 February 2027. Python 3.7 and 3.8 are the only items worded "next release".
</Callout>

## Where the sources disagree

**vVols.** [Dell](https://www.dell.com/support/kbdoc/en-us/000414330/dell-unity-vvols-support-ending-upgrade-and-migration-considerations) says "removal planned for 9.1", as [TechTarget](https://www.techtarget.com/searchstorage/news/366627334/Broadcom-discontinues-vVols-storage-capability-for-VMware) reported in July 2025. Broadcom's <KB id={401070} /> says only "future VCF/VVF releases". Broadcom's own 9.1 pages count against Dell's date: the [workload domain page](https://techdocs.broadcom.com/us/en/vmware-cis/vcf/vcf-9-0-and-later/9-1/building-your-private-cloud-infrastructure/working-with-workload-domains/deploy-a-vi-workload-domain-using-the-sddc-manager-ui.html) still offers a vVol-based domain, and the [import page](https://techdocs.broadcom.com/us/en/vmware-cis/vcf/vcf-9-0-and-later/9-1/building-your-private-cloud-infrastructure/working-with-workload-domains/import-an-existing-vcenter-to-create-a-workload-domain.html) says the framework is deprecated and "not recommended", both dated 5 November 2026. Broadcom has also ended vVols certification for 9.0, and [Protection and Recovery 9.1](https://techdocs.broadcom.com/us/en/vmware-cis/vcf/protection-and-recovery/9-1/release-notes/protection-and-recovery-91-release-notes.html) is the last release to support vVols.

<Callout type="analysis" title="This site's reading of the vVols dispute">
On the public record, removal in 9.1 looks unlikely. The advice in [the vSAN note](/vcf/library/architecture/vsan-esa-osa/) stands anyway: the migration is coming either way, and a broken datastore costs far more than moving early. Get written confirmation from Broadcom for your exact build if you plan to keep vVols through 9.1.
</Callout>

**Skylake-SP.** Table 2 of <KB id={318697} /> still lists it among discontinued CPUs, with a pointer to <KB id={428874} />, which extended it on 18 September 2026 to deprecated mode for all of 9.x. Follow the later KB. [Andrea Mauro's May write-up](https://vinfrastructure.it/2026/05/deprecated-and-removed-features-in-vcf-9-1/), a useful independent list, predates that and shows Skylake as discontinued. It also shows EPYC 7002 as discontinued, which KB 318697 doesn't: the KB has it as deprecated.

**vCLS.** Three wordings exist. The [9.0 notes](https://techdocs.broadcom.com/us/en/vmware-cis/vcf/vcf-9-0-and-later/9-0/release-notes/vmware-cloud-foundation-90-release-notes/platform-product-support-notes/product-support-notes-vsphere.html) call it deprecated, the vCenter section of the [9.1 notes](https://techdocs.broadcom.com/us/en/vmware-cis/vcf/vcf-9-0-and-later/9-1/release-notes/vmware-cloud-foundation-9-1-0-0-release-notes/vcf-91-product-support-notes.html) says it is off and can't be re-activated, and the Installer section of the same page still says it "will be deprecated in a future release". Plan on the vCenter section. <KB id={316514} /> says vCLS can be disabled from vCenter 9.0 without affecting DRS or HA.

**Port 514.** Broadcom's sentence covers syslog transfers "to vCenter". It doesn't say whether vCenter forwarding out to a collector on 514 is affected, so test that path before the upgrade. Port 1514 needs TLS, so a collector or sender with no certificate handling has work to do.

**OSA hybrid.** [Duncan Epping](https://www.yellow-bricks.com/2026/09/29/will-vsan-osa-hybrid-be-available-in-future-versions/), a Chief Technologist at Broadcom writing on his own blog, says he has had the question three times in two weeks and expects hybrid to go in a major upcoming version. Read his post first if you run hybrid. Broadcom still names no release, and all-flash OSA carries no notice.
