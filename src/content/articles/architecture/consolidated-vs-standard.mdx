<InShort>
In 5.x, **consolidated** meant running workloads in the management domain, separated by resource pools. **Standard** meant a dedicated management domain plus workload domains. Both patterns still exist in 9.x. The bigger footprint decision now is the deployment model of each management component, Simple or HA, because that decides how many hosts the management cluster needs. The short rule: with NSX in HA, plan for four hosts. With NSX Simple, three hosts on vSAN or two on external storage is the documented floor.
</InShort>

## Where the host count comes from

The number that drives the minimum is NSX Manager. In HA it is three nodes with anti-affinity, so you need at least three hosts to place them and a fourth to stay compliant during maintenance or a failure. That is why the HA minimum is four hosts, whatever the storage. NSX Simple, a single manager supported since 9.0, removes that constraint. That is how the documented three-host vSAN and two-host external storage minimums become possible (William Lam explains the reasoning in detail).

VCF Operations and VCF Automation also change size with the model. In HA, VCF Operations moves to the Medium size with primary, replica and data nodes and a load balancer FQDN, and VCF Automation needs more addresses in its pool. Size the management cluster from the sizing guides (<KB id={397782} />), not from the minimum host count.

**Consolidated** suits small estates where the management overhead of a separate domain would dominate. Its cost is that management and workloads share failure domains and maintenance windows.

**Standard** suits anything with separate lifecycle, compliance or tenancy needs. Each workload domain has its own vCenter and can be upgraded on its own schedule within the support matrix.

**More than one instance** makes sense for separate sites or regions, or when you want a hard lifecycle boundary. Separating business units alone is not a reason for one.

## Pitfalls

- **Quoting the floor as the design.** Two or three hosts is a minimum for the platform to install, not a recommendation for production.
- **Forgetting the 9.x management additions.** The 5.x management cluster did not carry VCF Operations, fleet management, a cloud proxy or, in 9.1, a services runtime with a dozen or more addresses. Re-size the management cluster during upgrade planning.
- **Mixing models without writing it down.** Per-component Simple or HA choices are supported, but they are invisible after the fact. Record them in the design so the next engineer knows why NSX is a single node.
