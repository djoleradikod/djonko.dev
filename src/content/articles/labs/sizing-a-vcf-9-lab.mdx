<InShort>
Size a VCF 9.1.1 lab from **76 vCPU, 251 GB of memory and 7,448 GB of disk**: Broadcom's Simple profile, with VCF Automation included (updated 5 Nov 2026). Those are appliance allocations, so they can run on much less physical CPU and disk.

- **Buying one machine:** 14 cores, 256 GB of DRAM and two 1 TB NVMe drives beyond the boot device, one of them for memory tiering. William Lam runs a complete 9.1 fleet on that, using lab workarounds.
- **Avoiding the host-count workaround:** three hosts. Lam's bill of materials uses Minisforum MS-A2 boxes with 128 GB each, at 16 cores each by his 2025 post.
- **Holodeck's 48 cores, 650 GB and 2.2 TB** is a recommendation for seven nested hosts, so borrow that server. Don't buy to it unless you need Holodeck itself.
</InShort>

<Callout type="note" title="Corrected on 6 November 2026">
This note gave 48 vCPU and 194 GB as the total of its 9.0 table. Those are the totals printed on Lam's post, but the rows add up to 50 vCPU and 201 GB. It also gave 30 cores for Holodeck 9.0, which the current docs list as 32 or 24 depending on vSAN type, and it had no 9.1 figures.
</Callout>

## The 9.1 figures, side by side

None of these builds was run for this note. Each row belongs to the source named, and "Sum" marks this note's arithmetic on that source's numbers <AsOf date="2026-11-06" />.

| Build | Source and date | CPU | Memory | Disk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Simple profile, appliance total | Broadcom [Fleet Sizing Models](https://techdocs.broadcom.com/us/en/vmware-cis/vcf/vcf-9-0-and-later/9-1/design/vmware-cloud-foundation-concepts/vcf-fleet-sizing-models-9-x.html), 5 Nov 2026 | 76 vCPU | 251 GB | 7,448 GB |
| HA Small profile, appliance total | Same page | 106 vCPU | 335 GB | 8,422 GB |
| Holodeck, single site (4 + 3 nested hosts) | [Holodeck docs](https://vmware.github.io/Holodeck/latest/), toolkit 9.1.1, Oct 2026 | 48 cores | 650 GB | 2.2 TB |
| Holodeck, dual site | Same page | 96 cores | 1,536 GB | 5 TB |
| Lam's nested script, 3 management hosts | [Repo sample for 9.1.0](https://github.com/lamw/vcf-fleet-automated-lab-deployment) | Sum: 3 × 32 = 96 vCPU | Sum: 3 × 112 = 336 GB | Sum: 3 × (64 + 32 + 500) = 1,788 GB thin |
| Add 3 workload domain hosts | Same repo | Sum: 3 × 16 = 48 vCPU | Sum: 3 × 32 = 96 GB | Sum: 3 × (64 + 32 + 250) = 1,038 GB thin |
| Lam's physical build, 1 host | [Lam, 1 Jun 2026](https://williamlam.com/2026/06/vcf-9-1-vmug-x-intel-x-micron-collaboration-on-single-vcf-host-deployment-for-vmug-connect.html) | 14 cores, 28 threads | 256 GB | 2 × 1 TB NVMe (vSAN ESA and tiering) |
| Lam's physical build, 2 or 3 hosts | [VCF 9.1.1 in a Box](https://github.com/lamw/vcf-91-in-box), 23 Oct 2026 | 16 cores each ([Lam, 2025](https://williamlam.com/2025/06/minimal-resources-for-deploying-vcf-9-0-in-a-lab.html)). Sum: 32 or 48 | 128 GB each. Sum: 256 or 384 GB | Per host: 1 TB vSAN ESA, 1 TB tiering, 500 GB boot |

Don't read these rows as one scale, since they measure different things. Broadcom's row is what the VMs are given. Holodeck's is a host recommendation that also pays for a workload domain and four management hosts, which the Simple profile doesn't, and its pre-checks warn below it without blocking the build. Lam's physical rows are hardware he reports working. They don't state how much room is left for workloads, so size for your own VMs separately.

<Callout type="analysis" title="Reading memory against CPU">
Spend on memory or a tier device before extra cores. On Lam's one-host build, 251 GB on 256 GB of DRAM leaves 5 GB before ESX takes its share, so that build depends on tiering. CPU has more slack: 76 vCPU on 28 threads is 2.7 to 1, which a quiet lab can absorb. Three of the 16-core hosts ease both ratios, with 76 vCPU on 96 threads and 251 GB on 384 GB.
</Callout>

## Which to buy

| Your situation | Build | Hardware |
|---|---|---|
| One purchase, full stack, you accept workarounds | Physical, one host | The 14-core, 256 GB spec above. Single-host deployment [needs a workaround](https://williamlam.com/2026/05/vcf-9-1-comprehensive-vcf-installer-sddc-manager-configuration-workarounds-for-lab-deployments.html), and adding clusters or domains later [needs the SDDC Manager API](https://williamlam.com/2026/09/vcf-9-1-1-single-esx-host-for-vcf-fleet-vcf-workload-domain-or-vcf-cluster.html) |
| vSAN and vMotion must behave as they would across hosts | Physical, three hosts | Three of the 16-core, 128 GB boxes, plus a 10 GbE switch as in Lam's BOM. Simple mode asks for 3 hosts with vSAN or 2 with external storage, in the docs Lam [quotes](https://williamlam.com/2026/06/clarifying-minimum-required-esx-hosts-for-vcf-deployments.html) |
| You already have a vSphere host with memory free | [Lam's nested script](/vcf/library/labs/nested-lab-automation/) | Nothing new. Allow 336 GB of nested host memory for management alone |
| A team needs identical environments, or a workload domain | [Holodeck](/vcf/library/labs/holodeck/) | Borrow a server near the single-site row, plus the Holorouter at 6 CPU, 12 GB and 75 GB. The docs don't say whether their table includes it, so allow for it on top |

Check memory prices before choosing. The 128 GB kit in Lam's bill of materials is listed at $279.99, then at $1,195 as of 7 Jan 2026, and three kits at that price cost $3,585 before anything else <AsOf date="2026-11-06" />.

Holodeck, Lam's scripts and the single-host workarounds are for labs. Holodeck is "not a VMware supported product", in its docs' words, and Lam [documents his workarounds](https://williamlam.com/2026/05/vcf-9-1-comprehensive-vcf-installer-sddc-manager-configuration-workarounds-for-lab-deployments.html) for non-production use.

## What you can cut, and one small addition

Broadcom's Simple profile counts VCF Automation, so leaving it out takes the total to 76 − 24 = 52 vCPU and 251 − 96 = 155 GB, well inside the one-host build's 256 GB of DRAM. It can go in later: Broadcom's [9.1 Day-N page](https://techdocs.broadcom.com/us/en/vmware-cis/vcf/vcf-9-0-and-later/9-1/fleet-management/manual-adding-vcf-components-post-deployment/install-vcf-automation-as-a-day-n-operation.html) documents adding it after deployment, and Lam [describes](https://williamlam.com/2026/06/vcf-9-1-deploying-vcf-automation-vcfa-to-non-management-network.html) adding it through Fleet LCM as a Day-N step in an upgrade flow. [Consolidated or standard](/vcf/library/architecture/consolidated-vs-standard/) has the sources.

The 24 and 96 come from the reference tables in Broadcom's [9.1.1 Planning and Preparation Workbook](https://techdocs.broadcom.com/content/dam/broadcom/techdocs/us/en/assets/vmware-cis/vcf/vcf-9.1.1-planning-and-preparation-workbook.xlsx), since the sizing page prints only totals. Small is 24 vCPU, 96 GB and 600 GB there, the same CPU and memory as Lam's 9.0 table, and Arun Nukula's [19 Sep 2026 post](https://arunnukula.com/blog/vcf-automation-sizing-what-you-are-actually-choosing) reads the tables the same way. Those figures fit the total: with Automation counted, the workbook's values for the other listed components come to 74 vCPU and 247 GB, leaving 2 vCPU and 4 GB for the License Server, which the workbook doesn't size. These subtractions and sums are this note's, not Broadcom's.

Holodeck makes Automation opt-in with `-DeployVcfAutomation`. Its docs don't say whether their 9.1 row includes it, so don't subtract it from that row.

The other cuts depend on the build you chose:

- Staying on Simple rather than HA Small saves 106 − 76 = 30 vCPU and 335 − 251 = 84 GB.
- If the job fits VVF, Holodeck lists 24 cores, 512 GB and 2 TB for VVF 9.1 single site, and Lam's script README asks for 12 vCPU, 104 GB and 100 GB thin.
- An environment upgraded from 9.1.0 can drop a VCF Management Services worker and save [12 vCPU and 24 GB](https://williamlam.com/2026/09/vcf-9-1-1-adopting-the-reduced-vcf-management-services-vcfms-footprint-after-an-upgrade.html). New 9.1.1 deployments get one fewer automatically, but an upgraded one keeps it until you run the procedure in <KB id={455842} />, which is limited to Day-0-only deployments without Log Management or Real-time Metrics.
- From 9.1, nested ESX VMs run on tiered physical hosts with no workaround, Lam [reports](https://williamlam.com/2026/05/vcf-9-1-automated-vmware-cloud-foundation-vcf-vmware-vsphere-foundation-vvf-nested-lab-deployment.html), so NVMe tiering can cover part of the DRAM there. Size nested 9.0 labs from DRAM: tiering on the outer host is unsupported according to Dave Morera's [29 Dec 2025 post](https://blogs.vmware.com/cloud-foundation/2025/12/29/nvme-memory-tiering-design-and-sizing-on-vmware-cloud-foundation-9-part-5/), and Holodeck's 9.0.x notes warn of instability. Don't carry the 9.0 commands across, since they [no longer enable it properly in 9.1](https://williamlam.com/2026/06/vcf-9-1-are-you-using-the-correct-esxcli-command-to-enable-nvme-tiering.html).
- In Holodeck, `-ManagementOnly` deploys the management domain alone. The docs give no separate figure for it, so keep planning on the single-site row.

Disk needs less attention than the 7,448 GB suggests. In Lam's 9.0 table the provisioned disk is about 10 times what is written (3,275 ÷ 334), and his 9.1 builds fit a 1 TB vSAN device. That margin closes as you add snapshots and workloads, and Holodeck's 9.0.x notes warn that nested vSAN ESA may use more than expected.

One addition is worth its small cost if you go physical: Lam's [VIS appliance](https://williamlam.com/2026/07/vcf-infrastructure-services-vis-appliance-for-vcf-9-1-lab-poc.html) at 2 vCPU, 4 GB and 30 GB bundles the supporting lab services. He pitches it at people who don't meet Holodeck's requirements.

## Where the sources disagree

- Broadcom's own pages differ on workers in Simple. Its Fleet Sizing Models page shows 1 control plane and 2 workers, while its [Simple model page](https://techdocs.broadcom.com/us/en/vmware-cis/vcf/vcf-9-0-and-later/9-1/design/design-library/vcf-management-services-availabilty-detailed-design/vcf-management-services-simple-deployment-model.html) still requires 3 (both updated 5 Nov 2026). Lam's posts account for the gap: [3 workers on 9.1.0](https://williamlam.com/2026/06/vcf-9-1-enabling-high-availability-for-a-small-vcf-management-services-vcfms-deployment.html), one fewer on new 9.1.1. With his node sizes of 4 vCPU and 10 GB, and 12 vCPU and 24 GB, the runtime takes 4 + (2 × 12) = 28 vCPU and 10 + (2 × 24) = 58 GB on new 9.1.1. An upgraded fleet that hasn't shrunk would be 76 + 12 = 88 vCPU and 251 + 24 = 275 GB. These sums are this note's, not Lam's.
- Lam's nested README conflicts with its own defaults. It asks for "up to 275 GB" and VMs of up to 24 vCPU, yet its sample gives each management host 112 GB and 32 vCPU. Plan on the 336 GB, because that is what the script allocates. The 275 GB matches the upgraded total above, so it looks like the fleet's memory rather than the nested hosts' allocation, though that reading is an inference.
- For Holodeck 9.0, use the current docs. They list 32 cores with vSAN ESA and 24 with OSA, both at 325 GB and 1.1 TB, and the [release notes](https://vmware.github.io/Holodeck/latest/release_notes/) tie those to VCF Automation's minimum. The [June 2025 launch post](https://blogs.vmware.com/cloud-foundation/2025/06/30/announcing-the-general-availability-of-holodeck-9-0/) by Jatin Purohit says about 30 cores.

## If you are staying on 9.0

These figures come from [Lam's table of 18 June 2025](https://williamlam.com/2025/06/minimal-resources-for-deploying-vcf-9-0-in-a-lab.html). Its printed totals don't match its rows, so the bottom row is this note's own addition.

| Component | vCPU | Memory | Provisioned | Used |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| vCenter | 4 | 21 GB | 642 GB | ~30 GB |
| NSX Manager | 6 | 24 GB | 300 GB | ~14 GB |
| SDDC Manager | 4 | 16 GB | 931 GB | ~77 GB |
| VCF Operations | 4 | 16 GB | 290 GB | ~13 GB |
| Fleet management | 4 | 12 GB | 206 GB | ~117 GB |
| Collector | 4 | 16 GB | 280 GB | ~11 GB |
| VCF Automation | 24 | 96 GB | 626 GB | ~72 GB |
| **Lam's printed total** | 48 | 194 GB | 3.2 TB | 330 GB |
| **Sum of the rows** | **50** | **201 GB** | **3,275 GB** | **~334 GB** |

Plan on the sums, because they are the larger figures and the post doesn't explain its totals. Without VCF Automation the rows add up to 26 vCPU, 105 GB and 2,649 GB.

VCF Automation sets the CPU floor on 9.0. Its 24 vCPU VM needs a host with at least 12 cores and 24 threads, and an 8-core, 16-thread box won't run it, the same post says. Expect the same floor on 9.1, though that's an inference from Broadcom's 9.1.1 workbook keeping Small at 24 vCPU. Manish Jha's [9.1 pre-deployment checklist](https://vstellar.com/2026/05/vcf-9-1-home-lab-series-part-3-pre-deployment-planning/) of 18 May 2026 also asks for 12 cores and 24 threads per host (he gives no reason), and Lam's 14-core host clears it.

Lam also says VCF Automation's memory has no workaround. For the vCPU, he records Tomas Fojta's report that it can be edited down to 16 or perhaps 12, with a warning that behaviour past initial setup may suffer. Treat that as a report and test it yourself; this note doesn't recommend it.
