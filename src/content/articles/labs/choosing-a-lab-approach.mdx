## Getting a licence

Evaluation mode doesn't solve the first problem. A 9.x deployment runs [90 days without a licence](https://vmware.github.io/Holodeck/latest/) (60 for 5.2), but the binaries and the nested ESX appliance [need an active entitlement](https://github.com/lamw/vcf-fleet-automated-lab-deployment) to download. On those terms, someone with no entitlement can't reach evaluation mode alone.

| Route | Who qualifies | Cost | Term | Rules that bite |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [VMUG Advantage + VCP-VCF](https://blogs.vmware.com/code/2025/03/19/vmug-advantage-home-lab-license-guide/) | Members who pass the exam | [$210 a year](https://vmug.com/advantage), plus an exam at [$250](https://blogs.vmware.com/cloud-foundation/2024/05/06/exciting-updates-to-the-vmware-certification-program/) less [$125 for members](https://vmug.com/advantage) | One year, refreshable for two more | Personal use only, not "work environments of any kind". Clock runs from your exam pass date |
| VCP certification alone | VCP-VVF or VCP-VCF holders | Exam only | One year | [32 cores of vSphere Standard](https://blogs.vmware.com/cloud-foundation/2025/04/14/free-home-lab-licenses-for-vmware-certified-professionals/). No VCF |
| [Partner NFR](https://knowledge.broadcom.com/external/article/373980) | Pinnacle, Premier or Select resellers in the Broadcom Advantage Partner program | Not stated | One year, renewal requested within 30 days of expiry | Test, enablement and pre-sales labs. No production, customer-hosted labs, customer POCs or revenue-generating use. Customers can't receive one |
| [Trial](https://community.broadcom.com/vmware-cloud-foundation/discussion/vmware-cloud-foundation-trials-license) | Enterprise customers with a site ID, per a July 2025 forum reply | Not stated | Up to 90 days, "Try & Buy" | Forum post by an unaffiliated user, so confirm through the Trials & Beta tab or your account team |
| Your subscription | Current VCF or VVF customers | Already paid | Your contract | Holodeck's docs send you to [your account team](https://vmware.github.io/Holodeck/latest/) for licences. Ask whether lab cores count against what you bought |

The VMUG sum is $210 + ($250 − $125) = $335 for year one, and 3 × $210 + $125 = $755 over three years if prices hold <AsOf date="2026-11-07" />. VMUG also [advertises free on-site exams](https://vmug.com/advantage) at its 2026 Connect events, which have their own ticket. Buy the membership before the exam, because the discount needs it and joining later [won't extend the licence](https://blogs.vmware.com/code/2025/03/19/vmug-advantage-home-lab-license-guide/). Use the same email for VMUG and the exam. Requests go through Broadcom's certification portal, and delivery [can take over a day](https://blogs.vmware.com/code/2025/03/19/vmug-advantage-home-lab-license-guide/).

The 128 cores cover less than they seem to if you go nested. Lam's [licensing tip of 11 Nov 2025](https://williamlam.com/2025/11/quick-tip-configure-nested-esx-cores-per-cpu-for-optimal-licensing.html) shows nested CPU layout changing the count: a 32 vCPU host needs 32 core licences as one CPU and 512 as 32 single-core CPUs. His [repo sample](https://github.com/lamw/vcf-fleet-automated-lab-deployment/blob/master/sample-william-vcf-9.1.0.ps1) allocates 3 × 32 = 96 for management, which fits. Add his workload domain at 3 × 16 = 48 and it's 144. That's an inference from his tip and sample, and Broadcom doesn't state it, so skip the workload domain or trim its hosts if you mean to stay licensed. Three physical 16-core hosts need 48.

An NFR can serve several labs. Keys [may be reused](https://knowledge.broadcom.com/external/article/373980) across demo environments, including Holodeck, if the request says so. A paid customer rehearsal sits close to the "revenue-generating" exclusion, so ask your partner manager first.

### Where the terms are unclear

**Whether the VMUG route reaches 9.1 cleanly.** Check this before paying, because it's the one gap that could strand you. VCF 9.1 [replaced download tokens with an Activation Code](https://knowledge.broadcom.com/external/article/443647), generated in the VCF Business Services console. Broadcom's VMUG guide still describes generating "VCF 9 Tokens" and doesn't mention 9.1 or activation codes. A [12 May 2026 reader comment](https://williamlam.com/2025/07/how-to-deploy-vvf-vcf-9-0-using-vmug-advantage-vcp-vcf-certification-entitlement.html) on Lam's post reports a token that failed in the Installer, with no reply. Ask in the VMUG community whether certified members currently get an activation code, or plan on 9.0.

**How long the licence lasts.** The guide says VCF for three years, a one-year token, and refreshes for two more years. Those fit if three years means one plus two refreshes, though that's a reading the guide doesn't confirm.

**The refresher.** Tom Fenton's Explore report describes an [open-book, 30-question exam every 12 months](https://virtualizationreview.com/articles/2026/09/10/vcf-ai-assistants-gated-lab-licenses-and-a-new-vegas-venue-my-highlights-of-explore-2026.aspx). The guide says under 20 questions, within 60 days of expiry, with a 14-day wait before a retake. The press report is newer and second-hand, so expect a short exam and read your VMUG email for the current version.

**Which exams qualify.** The [April 2025 post](https://blogs.vmware.com/cloud-foundation/2025/04/14/free-home-lab-licenses-for-vmware-certified-professionals/) lists VCP-VCF Administrator, Architect and Support. Fenton names Administrator and Architect. Take one of those two.

**Hands-on Labs pricing.** Broadcom called them ["completely free"](https://blogs.vmware.com/cloud-foundation/2025/06/19/vmware-cloud-foundation-9-0-hands-on-labs-dive-in-and-unlock-your-potential/) in June 2025, and the [September 2026 post](https://blogs.vmware.com/hol/2026/09/the-next-generation-of-hands-on-labs.html) retiring the HOL-26xx labs doesn't mention price.

The VMUG keys also carry [vDefend at 128 cores and ALB at 12 tokens](https://blogs.vmware.com/code/2025/03/19/vmug-advantage-home-lab-license-guide/), in the guide's terms.

## Physical or nested

Choose physical when the behaviour under test lives in the hardware: vSAN ESA, NVMe tiering, edge performance. Choose nested when you rebuild often and already own the capacity. Lam himself [moved to physical](https://williamlam.com/2026/05/vcf-9-1-automated-vmware-cloud-foundation-vcf-vmware-vsphere-foundation-vvf-nested-lab-deployment.html), mainly for lack of resources.

The three-host figure is from his bill of materials: $789 + $1,195 + $44.09 + $399 = $2,427.09 a host, so $7,281.27 for three. It leaves out the tiering NVMe, which he lists as previously owned, plus switching and transceivers. Only the memory carries a date: his row lists $279.99, then "$1195 USD as of 01/07/26". The sum takes the dated figure. At $279.99 three hosts would be $4,536.24, so check that price first. The one-host spec comes from a [Lenovo ThinkStation P5](https://williamlam.com/2026/06/vcf-9-1-vmug-x-intel-x-micron-collaboration-on-single-vcf-host-deployment-for-vmug-connect.html) given away at VMUG Connect events, so it has no price to borrow. On one host, a new fleet works from the 9.1.1 UI, but workload domains and clusters [still need the SDDC Manager API](https://williamlam.com/2026/09/vcf-9-1-1-single-esx-host-for-vcf-fleet-vcf-workload-domain-or-vcf-cluster.html).

The 9.1 run deploys three nested ESX VMs, initialises the Installer, syncs the depot, generates the JSON and starts the fleet deployment, with settings in one file passed through `-EnvConfigFile`. These details sit across his post, its comments and the README, so check them before the first run:

- Binaries come first. An offline depot must have no basic auth for the script to use it, so build it with [Getting binaries](/vcf/library/upgrades/getting-binaries/) to suit. The README's online option still says "Download Token", which 9.1 no longer accepts.
- The 9.1 nested ESX appliance has no default password, so set one at OVF deployment.
- A [reader reports](https://williamlam.com/2026/05/vcf-9-1-automated-vmware-cloud-foundation-vcf-vmware-vsphere-foundation-vvf-nested-lab-deployment.html) the script waiting forever on the OVA until PowerCLI is set to ignore invalid certificates. The script doesn't set that itself.
- Log Management isn't deployed. Lam calls it a Day-N activity in the comments.
- Every name in the file needs a DNS record. A hard-coded domain was [reported on 31 May 2026 and fixed on 1 June](https://williamlam.com/2026/05/vcf-9-1-automated-vmware-cloud-foundation-vcf-vmware-vsphere-foundation-vvf-nested-lab-deployment.html), so pull the latest copy before reporting a naming bug.

Keep one environment file per lab variant in version control, without passwords. Upgrade rehearsals need a starting point that matches the real estate, so keep a long-lived lab at that version and disposable ones at the target. Copy the estate's naming and network shape with placeholder addresses. Write down how long each phase takes, but don't quote those times as a production window, since nested timings won't predict real hardware.

## Lab hardware misbehaving

<Callout type="warn" title="Lab workarounds, not production settings">
Lam publishes these lists for "non-production usage", functional testing and education. Neither presents them as supported, so treat every one as unsupported for production. Nothing here recommends them.
</Callout>

Find your symptom, then read that section of his page for your build.

| Symptom | Where |
|---|---|
| High CPU on consumer AMD (Zen 4 or 5), slow entropy | [ESX workarounds, 11 May 2026](https://williamlam.com/2026/05/vcf-9-1-comprehensive-esx-configuration-workarounds-for-lab-deployments.html): AMD Ryzen and CPU Entropy |
| VMs fail to power on with NVMe tiering on consumer AMD | Same page: AMD Ryzen and NVMe Tiering |
| NSX Edge or VNA won't deploy on consumer AMD, which lacks DPDK | Same page: AMD Ryzen and NSX Edge |
| Poor throughput on Intel X710, including the MS-A2 | Same page: Intel x710 and LRO/TSO |
| vSAN compression hurting a CPU-limited host | Same page: vSAN Compression Algorithm |
| Installer error on mixed hardware vendors | Same page: Fake Server Hardware Vendor |
| Nested vSAN on physical vSAN fails or won't reclaim space | Same page: the two nested vSAN entries |
| pNIC error alarms in a small lab | Same page: vSAN High pNIC Error Rate |
| Installer refuses host count, 10 GbE, HCL, MTU, or times out | [Installer and SDDC Manager workarounds, 11 May 2026](https://williamlam.com/2026/05/vcf-9-1-comprehensive-vcf-installer-sddc-manager-configuration-workarounds-for-lab-deployments.html) |
| A lab on 9.0, or checking a CPU or NIC against ESX 9 | [Ultimate Lab Resource, 25 Jun 2025](https://williamlam.com/2025/06/ultimate-lab-resource-for-vcf-9-0.html) |

Apply only what your hardware needs, and note why. Keep the settings in one script headed "LAB ONLY", out of any host profile or template shared with production. Re-check after each upgrade: his HTTP depot workaround became [natively supported in 9.1.1](https://williamlam.com/2026/05/vcf-9-1-comprehensive-vcf-installer-sddc-manager-configuration-workarounds-for-lab-deployments.html), and his nested tiering workaround for 9.0 [isn't needed from 9.1](https://williamlam.com/2026/05/vcf-9-1-automated-vmware-cloud-foundation-vcf-vmware-vsphere-foundation-vvf-nested-lab-deployment.html). Don't benchmark a nested lab, since these settings get it running and say nothing about speed.

Once a lab is up, [break-fix exercises](/vcf/library/labs/break-fix-exercises/) and [PowerCLI and the APIs](/vcf/library/labs/powercli-and-apis/) are the natural next pages.
